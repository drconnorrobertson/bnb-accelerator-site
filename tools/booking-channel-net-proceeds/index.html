Booking Channel Net-Proceeds Comparison

AI-assisted editorial guide and tool · Published November 21, 2026

Use this comparison when two booking routes quote different percentage and fixed charges. Enter the same gross booking amount for both and identify which costs actually apply to each route. The result is a narrow net-proceeds comparison, not a claim that a direct booking or particular platform is always better.

Enter your assumptions

The starting values are a hypothetical worked example, not your property data. Calculations run in your browser; entries are not submitted or saved after a reload. Use one consistent currency where money is involved.

Percentage charges use the entered gross amount. The example names no provider and contains no current fee claim.

Choose a consistent gross basis

Confirm the base on which each percentage actually applies. This model applies both rates to the single gross figure entered. If a provider applies its fee to a different subtotal, calculate that amount from its actual statement before using an equivalent rate here, or compare the statement manually. Do not mix a guest’s full bill with an owner’s already reduced payout.

Include only relevant fixed costs

The fixed-charge fields represent amounts allocated to this one booking. They may include a transaction charge or a documented allocation you choose. Do not allocate a monthly subscription in one route while ignoring an equivalent cost in the other without identifying the difference. Advertising, refunds and customer-service work are outside the model unless you deliberately include their amounts.

Worked example

On a gross amount of 1,000, route A at three percent with no fixed charge costs 30 and leaves 970. Route B at 2.9 percent plus 20 costs 49 and leaves 951. The modeled proceeds difference is 19 in favor of A. These sample percentages are illustrative inputs, not current fees for any named provider.

Keep commercial differences visible

Payment timing, distribution reach, dispute handling and booking terms can differ even when the arithmetic favors one route. Keep those facts beside the cost comparison. A negative net result is possible if entered charges exceed gross receipts; it signals the input scenario rather than a payment instruction. Reconcile the actual completed booking against its provider statement.

Rental Maintenance Backlog Capacity Calculator

AI-assisted editorial guide and tool · Published November 21, 2026

Use this capacity screen for a backlog that has already been assessed and assigned estimated work hours. It subtracts recurring work from a team’s scheduled weekly hours and compares the remaining capacity with the one-time backlog. It does not prioritize safety issues, authorize repairs or build a contractor schedule.

Enter your assumptions

The starting values are a hypothetical worked example, not your property data. Calculations run in your browser; entries are not submitted or saved after a reload. Use one consistent currency where money is involved.

Uniform available hours are assumed. No completion date is guaranteed; tasks may require different workers or dependencies.

Build the backlog estimate

Use task-level estimates from the people qualified to assess the work. Include access, preparation, cleanup and expected coordination where relevant. Separate work that needs a specialist from tasks the entered team can perform. The tool treats all backlog hours as compatible with the remaining team capacity, so a mixed trade backlog needs separate calculations or a real schedule.

Reserve recurring work once

Enter recurring work as a total for the team, not per person. Scheduled team hours equal worker count multiplied by hours per person. Remaining capacity is that total less recurring hours. If the remainder is zero or negative, the model cannot calculate a completion time; increasing the calendar horizon does not create capacity that has not been assigned.

Worked example

Two workers scheduled for 20 hours each provide 40 team hours weekly. Reserving 24 hours for recurring work leaves 16 for the backlog. A 48-hour backlog takes three continuous-capacity weeks. If only 15 hours remain, the continuous result is 3.2 weeks and four whole weekly periods are required. Partial-week arithmetic does not specify a completion day.

Check dependencies before promising a date

Parts delivery, guest access, trade availability and task sequence can make elapsed time longer than this capacity screen. Record those constraints separately and review urgent work through the appropriate process. After each work period, update the remaining backlog with completed tasks and revised estimates. Do not count a task as finished solely because its estimated hours have been spent.

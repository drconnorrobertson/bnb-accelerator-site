Rental Replacement Reserve Worksheet

AI-assisted editorial guide and tool · Published November 21, 2026

Build a simple reserve schedule for one group of comparable items, such as mattresses or patio chairs. Enter your chosen future replacement cost, item count, months until purchase and cash already assigned to that group. This model spreads the remaining gap evenly. It does not predict product life or establish an accounting depreciation schedule.

Enter your assumptions

The starting values are a hypothetical worked example, not your property data. Calculations run in your browser; entries are not submitted or saved after a reload. Use one consistent currency where money is involved.

The purchase date and future cost are user assumptions. Keep each reserve group separate to avoid counting cash twice.

Define one replacement group

Use items with a shared replacement date and cost basis. Separate groups with different sizes, delivery requirements or purchase timing. Enter a future cost that you selected from an actual quote or documented assumption, including the charges you intend to fund. A current retail listing is not a promise that the same price will be available when the purchase occurs.

Count only assigned cash

Use money specifically assigned to this group in your own plan. The worksheet does not determine whether the cash is available to spend or subject to other obligations. If several reserve worksheets are used, do not count the same cash in each. Maintain a separate register connecting the amount entered here to the overall cash plan.

Worked example

Four items at 450 each create a target of 1,800. With 600 already assigned, the remaining gap is 1,200. Over 24 months, the equal monthly contribution is 50. If assigned cash exceeds the target, the contribution becomes zero and the surplus is shown separately. The model does not withdraw money or move it between accounts.

Revisit the plan when facts change

Update the worksheet if a quote changes, an item fails earlier or the purchase date moves. A shorter remaining period can substantially increase the monthly amount. Keep the prior version so the reason for the change is visible. Interest, inflation and tax treatment are not modeled; incorporate those questions separately when they matter to the decision.

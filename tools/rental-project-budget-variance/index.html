Rental Project Budget and Contingency Worksheet

AI-assisted editorial guide and tool · Published November 21, 2026

Use this worksheet for a defined furnishing or maintenance project with a base quote and known additional costs. Apply a contingency percentage that you choose to the combined cost base, then compare the result with the budget. This is an arithmetic scenario; the percentage does not establish how much uncertainty a project actually carries.

Enter your assumptions

The starting values are a hypothetical worked example, not your property data. Calculations run in your browser; entries are not submitted or saved after a reload. Use one consistent currency where money is involved.

The contingency is user-selected and applies to the entire known-cost subtotal. No risk allowance is recommended.

Establish the base quote

Identify the dated quote, included work and exclusions before entering the base amount. The delivery field is for charges not already included in that quote. The other-cost field is for known items rather than a balancing amount. If a vendor revises the scope, update the underlying quote reference so the worksheet can still be traced to the actual proposal.

Choose what the allowance covers

This tool applies the chosen percentage to base work, delivery and other known costs combined. It does not apply the percentage only to labor or to selected line items. Record the intended purpose of the allowance and keep known work out of it. An uncertainty that can be priced now belongs in the cost subtotal rather than being hidden in an arbitrary percentage.

Worked example

Base work of 5,000, delivery and installation of 600, and other known costs of 400 produce a 6,000 subtotal. A ten-percent selected allowance adds 600, creating a planned total of 6,600. Against a 6,500 budget, the variance is negative 100. A negative figure means the modeled total exceeds the entered budget.

Track commitments separately

A budget is not a bank balance or proof that spending is approved. Keep signed commitments, deposits and completed payments in a separate project record. When an allowance is used for identified work, revise the known-cost subtotal and decide whether any remaining allowance should change. Avoid adding the same expense to both committed costs and the contingency balance.

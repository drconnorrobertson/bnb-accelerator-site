Rental Consumable Reorder Planner

AI-assisted editorial guide and tool · Published November 20, 2026

This planner uses one consumable measured in one consistent unit, such as rolls, pods or bottles. Enter observed use per occupied night, expected occupied nights per week and delivery lead time. The result is a stock-planning calculation, not a demand forecast or supplier guarantee. Review shortages and actual consumption before treating the assumptions as a recurring purchasing rule.

Enter your assumptions

The starting values are a hypothetical worked example, not your property data. Calculations run in your browser; entries are not submitted or saved after a reload. Use one consistent currency where money is involved.

Whole-pack rounding may create stock above the target. Lead time and expected use are entered assumptions, not guarantees.

Choose a consistent usage basis

Occupied nights here are the combined nights for the inventory group, not necessarily calendar nights at one property. Keep the units-per-night measure consistent with that group. If you track use per guest-night, convert both inputs to that basis before using the planner. Do not mix guest-nights and property-nights in the same calculation.

Understand both stock figures

The reorder point equals modeled use during delivery lead time plus your chosen safety stock. Inventory position is usable on-hand stock plus confirmed units on order. The suggested order compares that position with target-week use plus safety stock and rounds upward to whole packs. It does not account for an unconfirmed order or a shipment arriving after the need date. Packs to target is a quantity comparison, not an automatic instruction to order before reaching your reorder point.

Worked example

Two units per occupied night across ten occupied nights per week give twenty units of weekly use. With a two-week lead time and ten safety units, the reorder point is fifty units. Twenty-five on hand plus fifteen on order make forty. A four-week target plus safety stock is ninety, leaving fifty units to purchase, rounded to five packs of twelve.

Interpret zero use and lead time

When modeled use is zero, on-hand coverage in weeks is not defined; the calculator displays that instead of dividing by zero. Safety stock can still affect a target. Zero delivery lead time is allowed as an assumption, but it is not a supplier promise. Test a delayed delivery or busier occupancy case before relying on a small inventory buffer.

Review the actual purchase

Check pack size, usable storage, expiration and delivery date. Confirm that on-order stock has not been counted twice after receiving it. Keep a dated usage sample and investigate unusual consumption rather than automatically raising the order every time. A numerical reorder point supports purchasing decisions without determining which product or supplier is appropriate.

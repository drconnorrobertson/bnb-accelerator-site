Should you get an STR insurance quote before your purchase offer?

Published September 25, 2026·6 min read

A short-term rental can meet your price and revenue targets yet fail as an acquisition because the policy you budgeted will not cover paying guests, the insurer will not bind at closing, or the actual premium breaks the cash-flow case. For a specific property, start insurance shopping before the offer is firm and keep a contract right—if your attorney recommends one—to verify coverage, cost, and lender acceptance before your money is exposed. A generic homeowner quote or the seller's current premium does not answer the buyer's question. Book a call to test insurance costs against an STR offer.

Quote the property and the intended rental use, not just a house

Give a licensed insurance professional the address, proposed ownership entity, closing and first-guest dates, expected rental frequency, whether the entire home or a unit will be rented, any owner use, occupancy and bedroom count, amenities, planned work, and the lender's coverage requirements. Ask which facts need inspection or insurer approval before a quote can become a bindable policy. If your plan depends on opening immediately after closing, say so; a policy that excludes guests during a renovation or vacancy period may not fit the launch plan.

The Insurance Information Institute's 2026 STR coverage report says ordinary homeowners insurance typically does not cover commercial short-term-rental activity. Coverage depends on the actual policy and insurer. Ask for written confirmation of the proposed STR use, property and liability limits, guest-related exclusions, deductible, loss-of-income conditions, and any separate flood or wind coverage. Platform protection is not a substitute for confirming the buyer's own insurance.

Compare at least two suitable written proposals if available, using like-for-like coverage and deductibles. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau recommends written quotes and showing them to the loan officer to check lender requirements. Its homeowner guidance is a starting point; the buyer still needs to disclose and insure the STR use. If no carrier will quote that use, do not turn a standard personal-use quote into an assumed operating cost.

Put insurance timing into the offer decision

Ask the agent and local real-estate attorney what the current contract already protects and whether an explicit insurability or insurance-cost provision is needed. The National Association of REALTORS® insurance transaction checklist advises reviewing insurance contingencies, contacting insurers immediately after acceptance, and getting written commitments. A quote, underwriting approval, binder, and issued policy are different stages; counsel should define which one must exist before a contingency expires and what happens if underwriting later changes the offer.

A useful buyer instruction is specific rather than 'subject to satisfactory insurance': identify the intended STR use and ownership, the types and limits of coverage needed, the maximum annual premium or other objective threshold if appropriate, the documentation deadline, notice method, and available cure, extension, or cancellation right. These are negotiation questions, not model legal language. Florida Realtors describes a homeowner's/flood-insurance rider that can cap cost and allow cancellation when coverage exceeds the cap; that is a Florida form example, not a nationwide STR-specific protection. Local counsel must select or draft the right terms.

Check the insurance deadline against the earnest-money, inspection, financing, and permit deadlines. If the insurer needs a roof record, claims history, or inspection from the seller, request it early and document any extension before a protection lapses. An insurance contingency does not replace verifying legal STR use, and a financing contingency may not protect the buyer from an unaffordable policy that technically satisfies the lender.

Recalculate cash to close and the maximum offer

Feed the actual quote into the maximum-offer model: annual premium, deductible exposure, separate hazard coverage, any initial deposit, and reserves. Ask the lender to update the monthly payment and required escrow or prepaid insurance. The CFPB's Loan Estimate explainer notes that the insurer sets the premium and the buyer often pays months of insurance at or before closing. A premium change can therefore affect both operating margin and cash to close.

Illustrative only: a buyer models an STR at a $3,000 annual premium, but a suitable written quote is $7,200. The $4,200 annual difference is $350 per month before any financing or tax effect. If the buyer's conservative annual cash-flow cushion was $5,000, that quote consumes most of it; the buyer should re-price the offer, seek genuinely comparable coverage, or pass. These invented numbers are a decision worksheet, not a typical STR premium or return. A higher deductible may lower premium while shifting more loss risk to the buyer; compare the total risk, not price alone.

Do not use the seller's old bill as proof of the buyer's premium. Ownership, entity, claim history, property condition, location, rental intensity, limits, and carrier appetite can change the quote. If the only available proposal excludes the very guest activity that supports your underwriting, treat the deal as uninsured for that use until a qualified professional confirms otherwise.

Use a clear proceed, renegotiate, delay, or reject rule

Proceed when a suitable insurer has confirmed the intended STR use in writing, the proposed coverage meets lender and buyer requirements, the premium and deductible fit the conservative model, and a binding path to closing is documented. Renegotiate when the higher confirmed cost or required property work lowers your offer ceiling; a seller credit is useful only if the lender and settlement team allow it and the ongoing cost still works. Delay under a valid written contract right if insurer underwriting or a needed inspection can be completed before protections expire. Reject the acquisition if adequate guest-use coverage cannot be obtained at any price that preserves your buy box, or if closing would require an uninsured gap you cannot accept.

Before removing a contingency, have the insurance professional explain what remains conditional, the lender confirm acceptability, and local counsel confirm your rights and deadlines. Frequently asked questions

Can I get an STR insurance quote before I own the property?

Ask a licensed insurance professional for a proposal using the specific address, intended short-term-rental use, buyer entity, and expected closing date. A quote is not the same as an issued or bound policy; ask what remains subject to underwriting.

Is the seller's insurance cost enough to underwrite my STR purchase?

No. Request your own written proposal for your ownership and rental plan, then compare coverage, exclusions, deductible, premium, and lender acceptance.

Should an STR purchase offer have an insurance contingency?

Discuss the current contract and local forms with your agent and real-estate attorney. Where needed, negotiate objective coverage, cost, documentation, deadline, and cancellation terms rather than assuming a general financing contingency resolves an insurance problem.

What if the STR insurance quote is much higher than my model?

Update cash-to-close and ongoing cash flow, seek like-for-like alternatives, then re-price, use a valid extension to finish underwriting, or reject the deal if suitable coverage cannot fit the buy box.

Can someone sign your STR purchase loan using a power of attorney?

Published October 6, 2026·7 min read

Potentially, but having a signed power of attorney is not proof that someone can close your financed short-term rental purchase. Confirm the actual loan accepts delegation, the chosen signer is eligible, and counsel plus the closing team approve the instrument for this transaction. If you plan to buy in the next six months but cannot attend settlement, choose an executable signing route before setting deposit and closing deadlines. Book a call to coordinate an STR acquisition around your approved borrower-signing and funding plan.

Decide who signs before deciding who travels

Ask the lender about the actual investment purchase: intended guest use, borrowers, ownership, property and proposed signer. A remote purchase and delegated signing are different arrangements. You might sign personally through an accepted process or authorize another person if permitted; do not assume every online or out-of-state closing requires a power of attorney.

The international STR buyer guide flags that some particular products prohibit delegated signing. Here, the question applies to a buyer choosing the signer and authority for an imminent acquisition, regardless of citizenship. Confirm the chosen program rather than copy a generic mortgage instruction or another buyer's accepted paperwork.

If no accepted route exists, tell the acquisition team before it negotiates a short closing. A seller's willingness to receive an offer does not validate the buyer's mortgage documents. Keep the contract calendar, actual lender review and proposed signature method linked. Do not promise that BNB Accelerator, your agent or a property manager can sign merely because they are helping with the purchase.

Clear the instrument and the person's eligibility separately

Fannie Mae's current POA topic allows specified purchase-loan documents subject to its conditions. It requires lender-held, matching, valid, notarized, property-referencing authority, with jurisdiction-dependent recording and original-document requirements. Agent restrictions cover certain transaction participants, with relative exceptions and particular permitted-exception conditions; a lender determination that applicable law requires acceptance is separately addressed. Trust-held title has additional delegation limits. These are conditional rules, not your program's acceptance or a substitute for counsel. Have the lender assess the named person and complete document rather than treat a broad authorization as sufficient.

Give counsel the proposed loan and property facts, the person you want to appoint and that person's relationships to you and to the transaction. Give the lender and closing professional the instrument through their approved channels. Ask each to confirm what it reviews and which conditions remain. Do not draft your own signature block from this educational page or assume notarization alone establishes sufficient authority.

An acceptable relative is not automatically an appropriate choice for every buyer. Explain the scope and implications with counsel, including how changes to price, loan terms or property affect the authority. Keep private identity and financial records secure. Delegating a signature is not delegating responsibility to choose an unsuitable loan, and it does not mean the borrower is released from obligations.

Compare three executable closing routes—not three assumptions

Request actual availability and cost for personally attending, personally signing through an accepted remote process, and delegated signing if approved. Do not describe remote notarization as universally available or a POA as automatically cheaper. The lender, title/settlement team and qualified counsel must determine which routes work for the actual property and loan.

Illustrative route budget only: suppose the buyer has reserved $5,000 for closing-logistics contingencies, separate from required equity, ordinary closing costs, furnishings and operating cash. Construct three scenarios with invented quotes solely to show the comparison. Travel costs $1,500. An accepted delegated route has $600 document expense and $400 delivery/coordination costs. A negotiated seven-day delay incurs an agreed $900 seller charge and $500 lock-extension cost. None of these routes or fees is asserted to be available.

This is why the signing method belongs in the complete acquisition and launch budget, not just the travel calendar.

Resolve authority before signing protection expires

Create a responsibility list naming the actual borrower, proposed signer, reviewing lender, legal adviser, closing professional and buyer's acquisition contact. Record each open condition and expected evidence date. The list coordinates review; it is not itself a grant of authority. Someone authorized to inspect a property or coordinate furnishings is not necessarily authorized to bind the borrower to mortgage obligations.

Send the proposed instrument early enough for review before protected deadlines. If the loan, lender, property or signer's relationship changes, ask whether re-review or different documents are needed. Do not reuse an old purchase POA because the same person is helping again. The trust-title closing framework applies separately when the proposed owner or signing role involves a trust.

Have the agent and counsel evaluate any required extension before the relevant protection expires. Keep the actual loan lock, seller possession and purchase-to-launch timetable aligned. A replacement signer does not resolve an expired lock, unavailable funds or unsupported property revenue. Continue address-level legal-use diligence rather than let document logistics consume the entire review window.

Verify any payment or wire instructions independently through previously verified channels with the appropriate closing professional. Do not treat a new email from a purported signer as permission to send purchase money to a different account. Authority to sign and authenticity of payment instructions are separate checks. Avoid sharing passwords or broad account access as a shortcut to funding.

Proceed only with an accepted route and independently sound STR

Proceed when the lender, counsel and settlement team have cleared the actual route, named signer and instrument, the schedule is feasible, and funded launch plus property downside checks still work. Renegotiate timing or logistics when a defined review condition can be cured under agreed protections. Select a personally signed route when it is actually accepted and the complete cost is supportable.

Delay when authority, participant eligibility, legal form or execution requirements remain unresolved. Reject a plan that depends on an undisclosed transaction relationship, a prohibited delegate, inaccurate identities or unverified payment instructions. A completed signature does not by itself establish proper authority, loan acceptance, legal hosting permission or that the STR is worth its price.

Book a call to find and underwrite an STR with a reviewed closing-signature route and achievable funding timetable. Bring the lender's confirmed parameters, non-sensitive authority status and remaining conditions. Frequently asked questions

Does a notarized POA guarantee I can close my STR loan?

No. Obtain review of the actual loan, person, authority and execution requirements before relying on delegated signing.

Can the acquisition team sign for me because I hired it?

Do not assume so. Coordination services are not a grant of signing authority or proof that a transaction participant is an eligible delegate.

Is a remote STR purchase the same as buying through a POA?

No. Personally signing through an accepted remote process and authorizing someone else to sign are different arrangements.

What if the proposed signer is not accepted before closing?

Compare real personally signed alternatives or seek an appropriate written timing change under professional guidance. Do not rely on an unapproved substitute.

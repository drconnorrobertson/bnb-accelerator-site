Does an HOA master policy cover your short-term-rental condo?

Published September 24, 2026·6 min read

Not by itself. A condo association's master policy may insure the building and common elements, but the exact boundary for unit interiors, improvements, contents, liability, deductibles, and assessments depends on the governing documents and policy. It may not address the owner's short-term-rental operation. Before buying, obtain the full master policy and declaration, have a broker who knows the intended STR use map the gaps, and request a bindable unit-level quote that satisfies the lender and the buyer's own risk tolerance.

The master policy and your STR policy answer different questions

The general Airbnb insurance guide explains why a standard homeowner policy may not match hosting use. The condo-specific problem is the seam between association and unit owner. Fannie Mae's condo buyer guide distinguishes a project master policy from an HO-6 unit policy and tells buyers to examine whether the master policy covers interiors. Its definitions are a starting point, not a determination of this project's coverage. The insurer's policy language and declaration control.

Considering an STR-friendly condo with a large master-policy deductible or unclear interior coverage? Book a BNB Accelerator acquisition call to put a broker-reviewed insurance stack and assessment reserve into the deal before the contingency ends.

The National Association of Insurance Commissioners warns that many ordinary homeowner or dwelling policies are not designed for accidents arising from short-term rentals. A buyer should disclose the planned guest activity to every relevant insurer and ask for written confirmation of the unit-level form and endorsements. Do not infer coverage because another owner hosts in the building.

Get the documents, not a certificate alone

Request the declaration and insurance section, the complete current master policy with endorsements, coverage schedule, deductibles by peril, claims or loss history available to buyers, and any assessment or litigation notices. A certificate can summarize limits but omit critical exclusions, endorsements, and definitions. Ask the HOA or manager whether the master policy treats interiors as bare walls, original specifications, or another defined scope; those labels are shorthand until the actual text is read.

Fannie Mae's current master-policy guide addresses project coverage, deductibles, and when unit-owner insurance is needed for its mortgage channel. These are lender standards, not a promise of claim payment or a universal insurance rule. Have the lender and broker review the current requirements and the actual policy together.

Ask the broker to map a realistic guest claim

Give the broker the address, unit type, intended nights rented, maximum guest count, amenities, furnishing value, management arrangement, master policy, and declaration. Ask for a written coverage map: who responds if a guest causes a kitchen fire; if a pipe leak damages the unit below; if someone is injured in the unit versus a common stair; if a covered building loss suspends bookings; and if the association assesses owners for a wind or other peril deductible. These examples are questions, not predictions that a particular policy covers them.

Confirm the master policy's per-occurrence and any per-unit deductible, separate wind or catastrophe deductible, loss-assessment provisions, subrogation language, exclusions, and the unit policy's own deductible. Ask whether the association may charge an owner for a loss originating in that unit under the declaration. A high master deductible can become an owner cash-call even when the building is insured. Do not add the entire project deductible to one unit's forecast without checking the allocation method.

If the unit will have a hot tub, pool access, dock, EV charging, or other features, describe them rather than assuming a generic STR policy includes them. Make the insurance quote bindable for the proposed use and closing date. A cheap preliminary indication that has not reviewed the condo documents is not a reliable acquisition expense.

Translate gaps and deductibles into the offer model

Separate annual premium, unit-policy deductible, potential loss-assessment exposure, a realistic cash reserve, and any uninsured or excluded exposure. Do not simply put the HOA dues in the expense model and treat insurance as paid. If the lender will not accept the project master policy or the unit insurer will not bind for transient guests, financing and launch timing may both change. The condo financing guide covers the separate project-eligibility gate.

Illustrative only: suppose the buyer's first quote is $2,400 annually, a revised STR-compatible unit quote is $4,000, and the broker recommends a separate $10,000 liquid reserve for possible deductible or assessment exposure after reading the declaration. The $1,600 recurring premium difference changes annual operating cash flow; the $10,000 reserve changes initial cash required. These invented figures are not insurance prices or a policy recommendation. Run both through the downside case without counting the reserve as an annual expense.

Proceed when the broker and lender have reviewed actual documents, the intended STR use is disclosed and bindable, and the supported cost and reserve fit the deal. Renegotiate if new coverage cost or a documented assessment materially changes value. Extend diligence or stop if the master-policy boundary, deductible allocation, coverage for guest use, or lender acceptance remains unresolved. Book a call to compare this unit with better-insured alternatives. This is educational guidance, not an insurance coverage opinion, legal, tax, lending, or investment advice.

Frequently asked questions

Do HOA dues mean the condo unit is fully insured?

No. Dues may fund a master policy, but interiors, improvements, contents, liability, STR activity, deductibles, and assessments require separate document review.

Is an HO-6 policy automatically suitable for Airbnb guests?

No. Disclose the actual short-term-rental use and obtain a broker-reviewed, bindable policy or endorsement for that use.

Can a master-policy deductible become my cost?

Possibly. It depends on the declaration, policy, event, and allocation terms. Ask the association and broker for a written explanation and model a reserve accordingly.

Will the lender approve any master policy the HOA carries?

Not necessarily. The lender applies current project insurance rules; ask it to review the actual master policy and any required unit policy before the financing contingency ends.

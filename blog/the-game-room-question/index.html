Is a Game Room Worth It, and Where

Published February 17, 2022·6 min read

A game room is one of the few amenities that changes both nightly rate and length of stay, which makes it unusually valuable where it works. It is also expensive, and in several markets it is money spent on something guests do not evaluate.

Where game rooms pay

Markets where the guest is a group staying multiple nights with weather-dependent activity. The Smokies, the Orlando corridor, the Poconos, Hocking Hills, Broken Bow and Midwest lake markets all qualify.

The common factor is that guests spend meaningful time indoors, either because the weather forces it or because the trip includes children who need something to do between activities.

In the Orlando corridor specifically, a converted-garage game room is the single most common upgrade that lifts a property out of the bottom pricing tier, because the competing inventory is otherwise near-identical.

Where they do not

Premium beach properties where the guest is outside all day and the interior is for sleeping and eating. A 30A house with a game room in place of a better living space has traded down.

Small properties where the space would come at the cost of a bedroom. Bedroom count sets the search bracket, and trading a bedroom for a game room usually moves the property into a worse competitive set.

Couples-oriented markets. A two-bedroom Joshua Tree property or a Hocking Hills couples cabin serves guests who did not book for a pool table.

What to put in it

  • Pool table. The anchor item and the one that photographs. A used slate table refurbished costs far less than new and lasts longer than a cheap new one.
  • Arcade cabinet or multicade. High photographic impact, high guest engagement, moderate cost.
  • Shuffleboard or air hockey. Good secondary items, particularly for mixed-age groups.
  • Theater seating and a large screen. Works well as a second zone in larger spaces.
  • Board games and card tables. Trivially cheap and mentioned in reviews constantly.

Avoid anything requiring specialist service, complex electronics that fail, or equipment with meaningful injury exposure.

The cost and the return

A basic game room conversion, meaning flooring, lighting, a refurbished pool table and seating, can be done for a few thousand dollars in an existing finished space. A garage conversion with insulation, climate control and finishes costs substantially more.

The return shows in two places: nightly rate, because the property moves up its comparable set, and length of stay, because guests with something to do indoors book longer.

Length of stay is the underrated half. Longer bookings mean fewer turnovers, which means lower cleaning cost and less wear, which compounds with the rate improvement.

Climate control is not optional

The most common game room failure is an unconditioned garage. A space that is 100 degrees in July or 40 degrees in January is not usable, and a listing photograph of a game room that guests cannot use generates disappointment rather than bookings.

Insulation and a mini-split system are the standard solution and they are a meaningful part of a conversion budget. Skipping them to save money produces an amenity that exists in photographs and not in practice.

The same applies to flooring. Bare concrete reads as a garage regardless of what is in it, and inexpensive flooring is what converts the space visually.

Photographing it properly

Game rooms photograph poorly under overhead fluorescent light and well under warm directional lighting. This is the single most common reason a real game room does not read as an amenity in a listing.

Photograph it in use: balls racked on the table, cues in hand position, arcade lit, seating arranged. An empty game room reads as a garage with furniture.

Give it a dedicated position in the shot order, immediately after the great room and hero amenity, because it is a differentiator that guests are actively comparing.

The alternative in small properties

Where a full game room is not possible, a games corner in a living space captures a meaningful share of the benefit at almost no cost: a card table, a stocked cabinet of board games, a dartboard where appropriate.

In mountain and lake markets, outdoor equivalents work too. Cornhole, a fire pit with seating and yard games photograph well and serve the same function of giving groups something to do together.

The underlying need is not a pool table. It is that a group of eight people staying four nights needs something to do together in the property, and the market rewards whichever version of that fits the space.

Frequently asked questions

Is a game room worth adding to a short-term rental?

In markets where guests are groups staying multiple nights with weather-dependent activity, yes. The Smokies, Orlando corridor, Poconos and Midwest lake markets all reward it. Premium beach properties and couples-oriented markets generally do not.

What should go in a rental game room?

A pool table as the anchor, an arcade cabinet or multicade, a secondary game like shuffleboard, theater seating where space allows, and board games. Avoid equipment needing specialist service or carrying injury exposure.

Does a garage game room need climate control?

Yes. An unconditioned space at 100 degrees in July or 40 in January is not usable, and a photographed amenity guests cannot use produces disappointment. Insulation and a mini-split are a necessary part of the budget.

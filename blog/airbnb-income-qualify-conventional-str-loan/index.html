Can projected Airbnb income help you qualify to buy an STR?

Published September 24, 2026Updated October 8, 2026·9 min read

Potentially—but a projected Airbnb revenue screenshot is not a mortgage approval. Fannie Mae's subject-property short-term-rental guidance addresses a one-unit investment-property purchase that is legally permitted for STR use. For a purchase, the lender can use a standard long-term-rent schedule or validated STR comparable data from an MLS or property manager, applies a 50% factor to monthly gross rent, then subtracts the property's full monthly principal, interest, taxes, insurance, and association dues (PITIA). A positive result may offset the property's PITIA only; a negative result counts in debt-to-income analysis. Ask the lender whether it is implementing the policy for your application and what evidence it will accept before you make an offer.

Check the rule's timing and property eligibility first

Fannie Mae's September 2026 announcement, reissued September 23, encourages lenders to implement the revised rental-income policy immediately and requires it for loans with application dates on or after December 1, 2026. A lender may adopt it earlier, but a buyer should not assume every loan officer or product is using it today. Get a written answer on the intended application date, lender overlays, and whether this is a Fannie Mae-eligible conventional investment-property loan rather than a DSCR or portfolio product.

The Fannie Mae STR-income topic limits this treatment to a one-unit investment property legally permitted to operate as an STR, with applicable local registration and licensing. It excludes STR income from an accessory dwelling unit under this topic. This is not a route to treat a second home or primary residence as an investment property. Obtain the address-specific permit and HOA evidence; see the permit-transfer guide and HOA diligence guide.

Ask which rent-evidence path the lender will use

For a purchase, Fannie Mae permits either a Single-Family Comparable Rent Schedule (Form 1007) based on standard long-term rentals or validated STR rental data from an MLS or property management company. The STR path requires three comparable properties, rates, days rented in the last calendar year, and relevant factors affecting STR use; same-market comparables are preferred when possible. The lender must retain the documentation. An AirDNA estimate, seller spreadsheet, or attractive Airbnb listing does not by itself satisfy the specified validated-data path. Ask the lender to show the selected source and unit consistency. For daily STR comps, the guide describes averaging the rates and the days rented across the three properties to establish monthly market rent; do not mix annual booked nights with a monthly payment. A property manager's report should document actual comparable rental activity, not only asking rates. The lender decides whether the comparables and borrower file satisfy current policy.

Work the qualifying calculation without mistaking it for profit

Illustrative only: suppose validated market gross rent is $8,000 a month and the subject property's PITIA is $3,500. Under the purchase method, $8,000 × 50% = $4,000 net rental income for qualification; $4,000 − $3,500 = positive $500 adjusted net rental income. Fannie Mae says that positive amount may offset the PITIA only, rather than being added as $500 of free-standing income. If PITIA were $4,500 instead, the result would be negative $500 and would be included in the borrower's debt-to-income ratio. These numbers are not a lender preapproval or a property operating forecast.

The 50% factor is a qualifying convention that Fannie Mae says accounts for vacancy and maintenance; it is not a guarantee that your real costs equal 50% of gross. Separately model cleaning, management, utilities, insurance, property tax, repairs, replacements, platform fees, seasonality and launch ramp using the revenue framework and downside case. A deal can pass loan qualification yet fail your investment threshold—or the reverse.

Do not assume rental income from a second home can be used the same way. Fannie Mae's general rental-income guidance distinguishes property occupancy and borrower history; the lender must apply all relevant eligibility and documentation rules. The subject-property STR purchase topic is narrower than a general claim that 'Airbnb income counts.'

Document your current housing payment before relying on the rent

The specific subject-property STR topic requires the lender to document the borrower's current housing payment to use subject-property rental income for qualification. A satisfactory comparable-rent report does not close this separate borrower-evidence gate. Ask the loan officer which evidence is accepted for your actual primary residence and whether anything remains unresolved before setting an offer price around rental-income qualification.

The general rental-income topic defines current housing payments to include rent, mortgage PITIA and applicable leasehold payments, or property taxes and applicable leasehold payments for a non-mortgaged primary residence. Mortgage-free does not automatically mean no qualifying housing payment. Where the liability is absent from the credit report, its documentation examples include rent verification, six months of bank statements or canceled checks showing housing payments, or the latest property-tax payment. These are policy examples, not a promise that one document resolves every file. Let the lender specify what is needed and confirm acceptance.

Hiring a property manager is also not proof of the borrower's own rental-income or property-management history. Do not use the general topic's treatment of other rental situations to override the specific STR purchase topic's offset-only treatment of a positive result. Have the lender reconcile the applicable topics and its actual implementation date rather than selecting whichever paragraph produces the largest modeled buying power.

Hypothetical comparison: two buyers consider the same property with the $8,000 gross-rent and $3,500 PITIA assumptions above. Buyer A's lender has accepted the current-housing-payment evidence and rent documentation under the applicable policy. The lender may apply the specified offset-only calculation, subject to all remaining loan requirements. Buyer B has the same rent report but no accepted housing-payment evidence. The arithmetic is identical; the supported qualification file is not. Buyer B should ask whether verified employment or other income supports a loan without this subject rent, or whether another legitimate product is available. Missing evidence does not establish universal ineligibility for every mortgage.

Separately, suppose either buyer has $280,000 of cleared acquisition funds and a $260,000 closing-and-launch allocation that already includes $35,000 of retained reserves. The unallocated margin is $20,000. A genuinely available alternative loan requiring $12,000 more cash reduces that margin to $8,000; an additional, non-overlapping $11,000 opening obligation then creates a $3,000 funding gap. Do not spend the protected reserve or count expected rent or a future tax benefit as cleared funds. These invented inputs illustrate a decision, not actual loan pricing, approval, customer results or an operating forecast.

Keep tax returns, bank records and identifying borrower documents in authorized secure channels, not a public worksheet or seller email chain. Make the financing answer part of the buy decision

Before an offer, send the lender the address, intended investment use, proposed ownership, local STR permission, purchase price, expected PITIA, and the source of comparable rent evidence. Ask for a written explanation of the rental-income method, any lender overlay, the application-date policy version, cash to close, required reserves and appraisal or property-condition conditions. Compare a conventional path with a DSCR quote if both are genuinely available; the conventional-versus-DSCR guide lays out the tradeoff. Obtain Loan Estimates where applicable or the product's written financing proposal, and compare total payment and cash needed, not merely a headline rate.

Proceed when the lender confirms a viable product and evidence, the property has a legal STR path, and your independent downside model meets your own hurdle. Renegotiate if a lower lender-supported rent or higher PITIA reduces buying power and changes the price the deal can support. Extend diligence or reject the property if permission or lender acceptance cannot be established before the financing contingency expires. Frequently asked questions

Can I use projected Airbnb income to qualify for a Fannie Mae investment-property purchase?

Potentially, for an eligible one-unit investment property legally permitted for STR use, using the lender's approved rent evidence and calculation. Ask when the lender implements the September 2026 policy and whether any overlays apply. The lender must also document the borrower's current housing payment before using subject-property rent.

Is an AirDNA estimate enough for the Fannie Mae STR-income method?

Not by itself under the specified purchase paths. The lender can use a long-term-rent Form 1007 or validated STR comparable data from an MLS or property manager that meets Fannie Mae's documentation criteria.

Does a positive STR rent calculation increase my qualifying income?

For this subject-property purchase rule, Fannie Mae says a positive adjusted net rental income may offset the property's PITIA only; a negative amount is included in debt-to-income analysis.

When must lenders use the revised rental-income policy?

Fannie Mae encourages immediate implementation and requires the changes for loan applications dated on or after December 1, 2026, according to its September 23, 2026 reissued announcement.

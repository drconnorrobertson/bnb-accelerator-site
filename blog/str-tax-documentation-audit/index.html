STR Tax Documentation That Holds Up

Published June 11, 2026·7 min read

The short-term rental strategy is legitimate and well documented in the regulations. It is also a position that produces large deductions against ordinary income, which means the file should be built to be read by someone who did not take your word for anything.

My BnB Accelerator, LLC is a real estate acquisition firm, not a CPA firm. This is a plain English explanation of the mechanics so you can have an informed conversation with a qualified professional, not tax advice. Our tax partner is AE Tax Advisors, an independent firm.

The three things an examiner would test

1. The average period of customer use. This is arithmetic, and it either holds or it does not. Total rented days divided by number of bookings across the tax year. The supporting record is your platform reservation export showing every booking with check in and check out dates. Export and archive it annually rather than relying on a dashboard that may change. See the seven day rule explained.

2. Material participation. The most commonly tested element, because it depends on records you created yourself. A contemporaneous log with dates, hours, and specific descriptions, corroborated by independent evidence. See how to keep a defensible log.

3. The depreciation position. The cost segregation study, the engineering methodology behind it, the placed-in-service date, and the allocation between land and improvements. A study performed by a qualified firm with a defensible methodology is a substantially different document from a spreadsheet estimate.

What makes a participation log credible

  • Contemporaneous. Entered as the work happened. A log created annually in one sitting reads exactly as what it is.
  • Specific. Not "guest management 3 hours" but "responded to five inquiries, rebooked the March 14 cancellation, adjusted spring break pricing, 2.75 hours."
  • Corroborated. Platform message timestamps, calendar entries, vendor texts, purchase receipts, travel records, and photographs.
  • Uneven. Real work produces irregular hours. Turnover weeks are heavy and quiet weeks are light. A log showing three hours every single week is not what actual operations look like.
  • Not suspiciously convenient. A total landing at exactly 101 hours invites the question it is trying to avoid.

The competing participation question

Under the 100 hour test, no other individual may participate more than you do. That means the hours worked by a co-host, cleaner, or manager are part of the analysis, and having a rough sense of their hours is part of the file. This is why the management structure decision belongs before closing.

The file gets built during the year, not after

We tell clients what to document from day one, because the position is only as strong as the record supporting it.

The annual file

  1. Platform reservation export for the full year, showing every booking with dates.
  2. The computed average period of customer use, with the arithmetic shown.
  3. The participation log with supporting evidence attached or referenced.
  4. An estimate of hours worked by any co-host, manager, or vendor.
  5. Financial statements from a dedicated property account, with the platform payout report reconciled.
  6. Invoices for repairs and improvements, with descriptions.
  7. An itemized furnishing schedule.
  8. The cost segregation study, in the year performed and permanently thereafter.
  9. Evidence of the placed-in-service date, such as the listing publication and the first booking.
  10. Personal use records, if any. See personal use days.

That file takes a few hours a year to maintain if it is built as you go, and it is close to impossible to reconstruct afterward. Keep it with your return rather than in a folder you will not find in three years.

The right posture

None of this is a reason to be nervous about the strategy. The seven day carve out is an explicit provision of the regulations and material participation is a defined test with defined evidence. The buyers who have problems are not the ones who took an aggressive position. They are the ones who took a correct position and could not document it. Work with a CPA who does this specific work, and see our partner firm's material on short-term rental tax strategy and cost segregation studies.

Frequently asked questions

What documentation supports the short-term rental tax strategy?

Three elements: a platform reservation export proving the average period of customer use, a contemporaneous participation log with specific descriptions and corroborating evidence, and the cost segregation study with its methodology, placed-in-service date, and land allocation.

What makes a material participation log credible?

It is contemporaneous rather than reconstructed, specific about what was done rather than generic, corroborated by platform timestamps, vendor communications, receipts, and travel records, and uneven in the way real work is. A log showing identical hours every week does not resemble actual operations.

Do I need to track my cleaner's or co-host's hours?

You need a reasonable sense of them. The 100 hour test requires that no other individual participate more than you do, so the hours worked by a co-host, manager, or cleaner are part of the analysis rather than a separate matter.

Is the short-term rental strategy risky from a tax perspective?

The seven day carve out is an explicit provision of the regulations and material participation is a defined test with defined evidence. The owners who encounter problems are usually not taking aggressive positions, they are taking correct positions they cannot document. Work with a CPA experienced in this specific area.

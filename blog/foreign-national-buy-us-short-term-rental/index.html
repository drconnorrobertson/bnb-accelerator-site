Can a foreign national finance and buy a U.S. short-term rental?

Published October 6, 2026·7 min read

An international buyer may have an available path to a U.S. STR purchase, but a general statement that foreign nationals can get a mortgage is not enough. Establish your actual borrower category, legal purchase eligibility, accepted ownership, documented dollar funds and permitted closing method for this address before offering. Those decisions can change which property you can close on and how much cash remains to launch it. Identify the borrower category before comparing STR mortgages

Citizenship, residence, tax status and a lender's program label are different facts. Fannie Mae's non-U.S.-citizen guidance addresses lawful permanent and non-permanent residents of the United States and makes the lender responsible for determining status from appropriate documents. It does not establish that a buyer living abroad without that residence status qualifies under the same path. Ask the lender how it classifies your actual circumstances instead of assuming every noncitizen needs, or qualifies for, a foreign-national product.

Providers differ. Visio's published lending process says it does not lend to foreign nationals or non-permanent resident aliens. Lima One's rental FAQ says foreign nationals can qualify and separately notes different STR underwriting criteria. The second statement is an invitation to verify a combined borrower-and-STR scenario, not approval for any property, country or entity. Neither statement is a universal lending rule.

Request a current written scenario for the intended purchase, not a refinance or a personally occupied vacation home. Specify residence, citizenship information the lender requests, available identity and tax identifiers, ownership proposal, property type, address and intended guest use. Confirm the accepted income evidence and minimum loan size. Do not rely on an old rate chart or assume domestic down-payment and reserve terms transfer to the international-buyer program.

Align purchase permission, ownership and lender approval

Have independent qualified counsel check whether you and the proposed ownership can acquire this address under applicable law. Do not equate lender willingness with legal purchase permission, or legal ownership with permission to host guests. Treasury's CFIUS residential-property FAQ describes a single-housing-unit exception with limits for adjacent land. That federal explanation is not a blanket clearance for every STR, multi-unit property, land parcel, entity acquisition or applicable state rule. Ask counsel to resolve the actual transaction and ownership before committing money.

Compare proposed ownership with lender vesting, insurance, banking and the address's STR approval process. Ask qualified cross-border tax advisers to review consequences in both relevant jurisdictions. A structure advertised as convenient for a mortgage is not automatically suitable for the buyer's tax, estate or liability position. Do not form a U.S. LLC merely because a generic guide says all foreign buyers need one, and do not change the contract buyer late without checking every dependent document.

Turn overseas wealth into verified, spendable purchase dollars

For its applicable programs, Fannie Mae's foreign-assets guidance requires documented sources, complete accurate English records or translations, and evidence of foreign assets exchanged into dollars and held in a U.S. or state-regulated financial institution, with dollar funds verified before closing. Confirm your actual lender's treatment separately. Do not transplant that rule to every foreign-national product or assume an overseas statement alone establishes acceptable cash to close.

Preserve the evidence chain from the original asset and account owner through conversion, transfer and the destination account. Ask what records support a large deposit, business-account funds, account-name differences or funds passing through another institution. Obtain the bank's transfer instructions and the lender's verification deadlines before moving money. Send personal records through approved secure channels; do not place passport scans or bank statements into a public property-analysis file.

Illustrative currency test, not an exchange-rate quote: assume a $500,000 STR with hypothetical $150,000 down, $18,000 closing expenses, $32,000 launch spending and an independently chosen $50,000 retained-cash floor. The complete dollar target is $250,000. A buyer plans to convert 400,000 units of an unnamed home currency. At an assumed $0.64 per unit, that produces $256,000; less a hypothetical $2,000 total conversion and transfer cost leaves $254,000, only $4,000 above the target.

At an alternative assumed $0.60 per unit, the same 400,000 units produce $240,000 and $238,000 after the same assumed cost: $12,000 below the target. The $16,000 change in dollar proceeds comes from the constructed exchange assumptions, not a forecast. Get real conversion quotes, accepted funds requirements and time buffers; do not spend the retained-cash floor to hide the gap. Use the STR purchase-and-launch cash planner in dollars, while tracking household commitments separately in the currency in which they are due.

Approve the signing method before promising a closing date

A remote purchase is not automatically a power-of-attorney purchase. American Heritage Lending's foreign-national guide discusses generic remote-signing options but its specific Invest Star program section says foreign-national borrowers must sign personally and prohibits power of attorney. It also specifies U.S.-domiciled entity vesting and a U.S. bank account for automatic payments. Confirm the current product-specific requirements rather than treating the general discussion as permission for your loan.

Have the lender and closing professional approve how and where you will sign, the identity checks, any notarization or authentication, document delivery and the last acceptable signing date. If using entity ownership, verify who signs and how their authority is established. Do not assume an online notary, embassy appointment or local representative is acceptable to every lender, title insurer or transaction. If the parties cannot confirm an executable method, do not promise the seller a date based on a generic advertised closing timeline.

Put transfer clearance, lender verification, final-document availability, approved signing and funding authorization on one closing calendar. Ask each responsible party for its actual lead time and contingency, including time-zone differences. Recheck the calendar when an entity, bank account, signature method or purchase price changes. Coordinate protections and extension rights with the agent and attorney before deadlines; an overseas transfer sent on time is not necessarily cleared, verified money available for disbursement.

The final deadline should also leave room for independent property diligence. Do not replace inspections, permission checks or supported revenue with a remote-closing success story. The existing out-of-state acquisition guide covers local-team and inspection challenges; this page adds cross-border borrower, funds and signing dependencies rather than repeating a remote-owner checklist.

Decide whether this U.S. STR is ready for an offer

Proceed when the address-specific legal and rental-use questions are resolved within appropriate protections, actual lender terms fit the borrower and ownership, documented dollar funds cover closing and launch while retaining downside cash, and an approved signing route fits the contract calendar. Underwrite the property independently: a dollar-denominated loan does not guarantee dollar rental income or positive cash flow, and ownership does not confer an immigration status.

Renegotiate price or timing when a documented gap can be repaired without exhausting retained funds. Compare another genuinely available lender when the first product excludes the borrower or closing method. Delay when ownership advice, accepted overseas-funds evidence, transfer clearance or signing approval remains open. Reject when the only plan relies on unauthorized guest use, unverified eligibility, an assumed exception or a later refinance to replenish purchase cash.

Frequently asked questions

Can a non-U.S. citizen get a mortgage for an STR purchase?

An available path depends on actual residence status, lender program, ownership and property use. Noncitizens living lawfully in the United States and buyers residing abroad should not assume the same eligibility treatment.

Does a foreign-national mortgage offer mean I can legally buy and rent any house?

No. Independent counsel must assess the actual buyer, ownership and transaction, and intended STR use needs its own address-level review. Financing is not legal purchase or rental permission.

Can I use a power of attorney to close my U.S. STR from abroad?

Only if the actual lender and closing parties accept it. Some specific programs prohibit it even where general guides discuss it. Confirm the executable signing method before promising a date.

How should I budget overseas funds for the STR purchase?

Use lender-accepted, documented dollar proceeds after conversion and transfer costs, with separate launch spending and retained cash. Test a worse conversion result instead of treating home-currency wealth as fixed dollar cash.

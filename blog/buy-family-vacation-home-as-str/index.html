Can you buy a relative's vacation home as an STR with a gift of equity?

Published October 6, 2026·7 min read

You may be able to buy the property, but do not assume a relative's promised equity gift supplies the down payment for an investment-purpose STR loan. Confirm the real occupancy, seller relationship and proposed price or credit with the actual lender before committing. A family discount does not establish that the property can legally operate or earn a supported return. Book a call to compare the family property with another fundable STR acquisition. This guide addresses a buyer closing in the next zero to six months, not general gift-tax planning or transferring a home for personal occupancy.

Tell the lender what you are buying and how you will use it

Start with the intended use after closing: a property acquired for short-term rental income, not the seller's former use as a family vacation home. Explain who will occupy it, how guest rentals will work and whether projected rental income is needed for qualification. Disclose the family relationship and every proposed price, credit, repayment agreement or retained right. A familiar seller is not a reason to leave transaction facts off the application.

Fannie Mae's current Occupancy Types guidance distinguishes investment properties from second homes. Its second-home requirements include borrower occupancy, exclusive control and restrictions on management control; identifying rental income does not waive the other conditions, and that income cannot be used to qualify under the stated second-home treatment. Have the lender classify your actual plan. Do not label a full-time investment STR a second home solely to access a favorable gift rule.

Ask for program-specific acceptance of the related-party purchase. An answer about a parent's cash gift to buy an unrelated seller's house does not answer a sale in which the parent is also the seller. A portfolio or DSCR lender may have different requirements, but its name or property-income approach does not establish permission for the proposed credit. Obtain the real eligibility and documentation answer before relying on it.

Keep the question narrow: can the named buyer acquire this address, from this seller, at these documented terms, for the disclosed rental use, with the identified funds? If the answer is uncertain, work on the transaction structure before promising the family a closing date. The financing conversation should not depend on a later change of use that has been concealed from the lender.

Distinguish an equity credit, price reduction, cash gift and seller note

The current Fannie Mae Gifts of Equity topic describes a seller's equity transferred as a transaction credit and permits that treatment for principal-residence and second-home purchases, not a listed investment-property path. It excludes equity gifts from financial reserves and requires appropriate documentation. This is a rule for that framework, not a statement that no lender can finance a family sale. Ask how your investment-purpose structure must be funded.

Its separate Personal Gifts guidance expressly disallows gifts on investment properties. Changing an ineligible equity credit into a last-minute cash gift does not make it eligible under that rule. Use the cash-gift guide for the separate donor and transfer question, but do not treat it as approval of this related-party sale. Primary rules were checked October 6, 2026.

Have the lender and closing professional reconcile the complete structure. A seller note must be documented as the actual obligation, not hidden behind a gift letter. Compare that path with the seller-financed acquisition framework. Qualified counsel and tax advisers should independently review their parts of any family transaction; a financing worksheet does not decide tax characterization or legal rights.

Model a lower price without inventing a zero-cash mortgage

An actual negotiated lower price can reduce the acquisition commitment, but the lender determines its effect on the loan. The current Fannie Mae LTV guidance generally calculates purchase LTV using the lower of sale price and current appraised value, subject to its stated exceptions. A higher appraisal is not automatically a substitute for buyer funds. Confirm the treatment of your actual non-arm's-length purchase instead of assuming every program uses the same calculation.

Illustrative purchase comparison, not a valuation, loan offer or recommended family price: suppose a property appraises at $500,000. The family first discusses a $500,000 contract with a $100,000 equity credit. If the lender rejects that credit for the proposed investment loan, the buyer cannot simply show zero down while borrowing $400,000. The purchase must be reworked with an accepted source or another effective structure; a family promise is not underwriting acceptance.

Now assume the seller genuinely agrees to a $400,000 sale, the lender accepts that related-party transaction and the hypothetical loan permits eighty-percent purchase LTV using the lower price. Modeled loan principal is $320,000, leaving $80,000 of buyer purchase funds—not a $400,000 loan requiring no cash just because the appraisal is $500,000. If modeled closing costs are $12,000, remaining preparation is $24,000 and retained STR reserves are $25,000, the buyer needs $141,000 in total supported acquisition-and-launch resources. The LTV and cost inputs are examples, not eligibility promises.

The lower price also must be executable for the seller and closing: ask the professionals whether actual payoffs, required releases and costs can be met. Do not assume a discount can occur while an undisclosed portion is repaid later. Keep any real obligation in the lender-reviewed documents. Use the first-STR purchase budget to test available buyer cash after household reserves and avoid double-counting deposits credited at closing.

Make the family property compete on the same STR evidence

Treat familiarity with the house as helpful context, not a substitute for the buyer's legal-use and revenue file. A parent's historic permission, listing or personal-use pattern does not determine the new owner's authorization. Verify the address's permit process, HOA restrictions and insurer acceptance for the intended buyer and guest use. Do not spend launch cash based only on the family saying they have always rented it.

Separate the negotiated transaction price from the maximum price supported by your intended STR economics. Build independent revenue and expense assumptions with real buyer financing, ownership costs and setup scope. If family occupancy will continue, disclose and document the arrangement with the appropriate professionals and remove unavailable nights from the model. An informal promise of unlimited family holidays can change the investment you thought you were purchasing.

Example of a decision conflict, not a revenue forecast: the model assumes guests can book peak-season weekends, but the seller expects recurring personal use of those dates after sale. Do not retain both the full guest revenue and the seller's use expectation as if they were compatible. Resolve what is actually being conveyed and how use affects bookings, loan classification and legal authority before pricing the acquisition.

Use independent title, condition and financial review even when negotiating feels socially awkward. Protect both parties by setting clear evidence and decision deadlines, rather than promising to solve material issues after closing. Compare an unrelated-seller candidate using the two-property purchase framework. Family sentiment or an attractive appraisal gap should not exclude a better supported investment from consideration.

Agree on a decision the buyer can actually fund

Proceed when actual investment-purpose financing accepts the related-party terms, approved funds cover closing and launch, title can convey, legal rental use works and the property survives conservative underwriting. Renegotiate a price, documented financing structure or retained-use expectation only with effective professional review. Delay commitments while credit eligibility or possession terms remain unresolved. Reject a plan that relies on hidden repayments, false occupancy or treating paper equity as spendable reserves.

Present the family with a clear purchase file: disclosed relationship and intended use, lender-reviewed price and credits, actual buyer funds, seller payoff confirmation, possession and retained rights, permit evidence and independent economics. A polite, evidence-based decision can be to buy another STR. Do not make a weak acquisition appear affordable by assuming family members will rescue future operating losses.

Frequently asked questions

Can a relative's equity gift replace my STR down payment?

Do not assume so. Confirm the actual investment-loan program's eligibility. Fannie Mae's stated gift-of-equity path applies to principal-residence and second-home purchases, not a listed investment-property path.

Does a lower family sale price mean I can borrow the full price?

Not automatically. Loan sizing depends on the lender's valuation and purchase rules. In the illustrated eighty-percent lower-price case, the buyer still supplies the remaining purchase funds.

Can I call the property a second home to use a gift rule?

Disclose the real use and let the lender classify it. Do not misrepresent a full-time investment STR's occupancy or conceal rental-income qualification or management terms.

Should I buy the family property simply because it is discounted?

Compare legal rental use, buyer financing, remaining setup cash and conservative economics with other candidates. A discount does not automatically make the STR a suitable acquisition.

Wifi, Workspace and the Guest Who Extends Your Shoulder Season

Published October 31, 2022·6 min read

The remote worker is the most underserved segment in vacation rental markets and the one most able to fill a shoulder season. They also have a specific, checkable set of requirements, and properties that meet them face very little competition.

Why this guest matters

Shoulder season is where most seasonal properties lose money, and the remote worker is one of the few guest types who does not care whether it is peak season. They care whether they can work.

They also book longer than a vacation guest. A one to three week stay in a shoulder month is revenue that would otherwise not exist, at low turnover cost per night.

And they are less price-sensitive on the shoulder rate than a vacation guest, because they are comparing against staying home rather than against another vacation.

What they actually need

  • Genuinely fast, reliable internet. Not 'wifi available'. A stated speed, tested, with a backup plan.
  • A real desk and a real chair. A dining table and a dining chair is not a workspace for eight hours a day.
  • A door that closes. For calls, and for separating work from the rest of the stay.
  • Adequate lighting at the workspace, which most vacation properties do not have.
  • A monitor, or at minimum a laptop stand. Cheap and disproportionately appreciated.
  • Power outlets at the desk, which sounds obvious and is frequently missing.

Proving the internet claim

Every listing says the wifi is good. Almost none prove it, which means a stated, tested speed is a genuine differentiator that costs nothing.

Run a speed test, screenshot it, and include it in the listing photographs. State the download and upload speeds in the description. Upload speed matters more than most owners realise, because video calls depend on it.

Have a backup. A cellular hotspot as a failover, or a documented process for what happens if the connection drops, turns an outage from a refund into an inconvenience.

The physical setup

A dedicated workspace does not require a dedicated room. A desk in a bedroom corner with proper lighting, a real chair and outlets is sufficient and costs a few hundred dollars.

In larger properties, converting an underused space into a small office is frequently the highest-return small renovation available, because it opens an entire guest segment at minimal cost.

Photograph it. Remote workers scanning listings are looking specifically for a workspace image, and a property that has one and does not photograph it is invisible to them.

Which markets suit it

Markets with a severe trough and an appealing setting. Sedona, the Smokies, the Poconos, the Gulf Coast in autumn and Southwest Florida in early summer all have shoulder windows where the setting is still attractive.

Markets within reasonable range of a metro also work, because a remote worker may need to return occasionally.

It works less well in extreme-climate troughs. Phoenix in July and a Michigan lake in November are difficult to sell to anyone, remote worker or otherwise.

The tax caution

This segment books long, and long bookings interact directly with the seven-day average stay test that supports the short-term rental tax strategy for high earners.

The average is total rented days divided by total rental periods across the tax year. A few multi-week bookings added to a calendar of short stays can push the annual average past the threshold, removing the treatment entirely.

For an owner relying on the strategy, remote work bookings should be sized against the running annual average rather than accepted freely. Compute it monthly. This is an explanation rather than tax advice; confirm with your CPA before building a shoulder strategy on long stays.

Marketing to them specifically

The listing has to say it. A property marketed as a family beach house does not read as a remote work base, and the guest searching for one will not find it.

Add a workspace section to the description, use the photograph, state the internet speeds, and mention proximity to coffee shops, coworking or anything else that supports a working stay.

Consider a separate listing emphasis or a distinct set of shoulder-season rates aimed at longer stays. The property is the same; the positioning is what changes, and positioning costs nothing.

Frequently asked questions

What do remote work guests need in a short-term rental?

Genuinely fast and reliable internet with a stated tested speed, a real desk and chair, a door that closes for calls, proper task lighting, power at the desk, and ideally a monitor or laptop stand.

How do I prove my wifi is actually good?

Run a speed test, screenshot it, include it in the listing photographs, and state download and upload speeds in the description. Upload speed matters more than owners realise because video calls depend on it.

Do long remote work bookings affect the STR tax strategy?

They can. The seven-day average stay test is computed across the full tax year, and a few multi-week bookings can push the annual average past the threshold. Compute the running average monthly and confirm with your CPA.

Should you use a 401(k) loan for an STR down payment?

Published October 6, 2026·6 min read

A 401(k) loan can sometimes supply part of an STR down payment, but permission to borrow is not proof that buying now is prudent. Before offering on a short-term rental, verify your plan's terms and your mortgage lender's acceptance, then test repayment without relying on Airbnb bookings. Keep launch cash and usable reserves separate from the remaining retirement balance. Book a call to test your STR purchase budget before borrowing against retirement savings. BNB Accelerator helps evaluate acquisitions; your plan administrator, lender and independent advisers must resolve the funding and retirement questions.

Confirm the actual plan loan, not an online borrowing limit

The IRS plan-loan FAQ explains that qualified plans may offer loans but are not required to. It describes maximum amounts, existing-loan adjustments and repayment requirements. Ask your administrator for the amount available to you now, the interest rate, fees, repayment schedule, disbursement time and all relevant restrictions. Do not apply the headline $50,000 figure to every account or assume a past loan no longer affects the calculation.

IRS guidance generally requires repayment within five years, with a different possible term for acquiring a principal residence. An investment STR is not automatically your principal residence because you occasionally stay there. Describe the intended purchase accurately. A 401(k) participant loan is also different from a taxable withdrawal or an IRA buying property; the IRS says IRAs do not permit loans. Do not treat these as interchangeable funding paths.

Build a timeline backward from your offer: administrator approval, funds disbursement, documentation delivery, lender acceptance and closing clearance. If money would arrive after a financing deadline, the property is not funded yet. Ask your agent and counsel about appropriate protection rather than hoping the administrator processes the request faster. A plan loan should not be taken merely to demonstrate cash that your lender has not agreed to accept.

Get lender acceptance and reserve treatment in writing

Fannie Mae's borrowed-funds guidance includes funds secured by financial assets, including 401(k) accounts, as an acceptable source under its rules. It addresses documentation, debt treatment and reducing the same asset when it is also used for reserves. That is a program rule, not a promise that your DSCR lender, loan product or file will qualify. Ask the actual lender to apply its current requirements to your investment property.

A lender may exclude a qualifying financial-asset loan payment from a particular debt calculation; your household still has to make the payment. Keep underwriting eligibility and affordability in separate columns. Similarly, a retirement balance accepted as mortgage reserves is not necessarily cash immediately available to pay a cleaner, replace equipment or carry an unopened rental. Ask both the lender and administrator what can actually be accessed, on what terms and by when.

Test the purchase gap and monthly repayment separately

Illustrative only: a candidate STR needs $90,000 at closing, $25,000 for preparation and furnishings, and $30,000 of retained operating cash. The total funding requirement is $145,000. With $105,000 in liquid savings and a $40,000 plan loan, the buyer can reach that number on paper. But only if the quoted closing and launch expenses are complete, the lender accepts the source, and disbursement clears on time. Do not count the remaining retirement balance as the same $30,000 of cash.

For a purely illustrative $40,000 loan amortized monthly over five years at 8%, the payment is approximately $811 a month before fees. Use your administrator's actual schedule instead. If the property's conservative first-year forecast leaves $500 a month after operating expenses and its mortgage, those receipts would not cover the added $811 payment. You would need roughly $311 a month from another dependable source, and the full payment during months without available property cash. The example is a funding test, not an expected STR return or offered plan rate.

Run a no-bookings launch period and a weaker operating year through the STR downside calculator. Keep a household schedule alongside the property's accounts so retirement-loan repayment is visible without incorrectly presenting it as a property operating expense. Compare buying a lower-entry-cash property, waiting to accumulate cash, or using another lender-approved funding source. Each alternative needs its own documented costs and risks.

Model a job change before committing retirement-backed cash

Ask what your plan requires if employment ends, payroll deductions stop or the plan terminates. Do not assume your employer will let the same repayment schedule continue. The IRS retirement-loan overview discusses potential distribution treatment and rollover timing when a loan is not repaid. The applicable outcome depends on the facts. Obtain personalized tax advice rather than treating a potential rollover deadline as free time or guaranteed relief.

Make a job-change column in the purchase worksheet: accessible household emergency cash, the administrator's required repayment amount and date, obligations at the STR, and a launch delay. Do not use the property's furnishing reserve to solve a retirement-loan problem and continue calling the property fully funded. If the same dollar is assigned to three emergencies, the purchase budget is overstated. A hoped-for refinance, tax saving or sale of the STR is not a reliable substitute for accessible funds.

Borrowing also changes your retirement investment exposure. There is no defensible universal return assumption to insert so that the STR wins the comparison. Ask an independent adviser to assess the effect on your own retirement plan, contribution strategy and risk tolerance. This page does not recommend retirement borrowing; it helps prevent an acquisition decision from hiding a second financial commitment.

Choose proceed, renegotiate, delay or reject before the offer

Proceed only when the administrator confirms the loan, your lender accepts the source, the full purchase-and-launch budget fits, and repayment and job-change stress tests remain affordable without optimistic STR income. Verify legal STR use and property evidence independently. Available retirement credit cannot make an unsuitable address or an unsupported seller revenue claim investable.

Renegotiate the price or preparation scope when verified costs create a small funding gap, but never omit necessary safety, legal or launch work to force a fit. Delay when funds cannot be documented in time, household income is uncertain or professional review is incomplete. Reject the financing plan if repayment requires peak-season income every month, the remaining usable reserves are inadequate, or a job separation would leave an unfunded obligation. Reject the property separately if its operating downside fails even without the plan loan.

Bring the written plan terms, mortgage conditions, cash ledger and one actual listing to your acquisition discussion. Book a call to set a fundable STR search budget before borrowing. Frequently asked questions

Can I use a 401(k) loan for an Airbnb down payment?

Potentially, if your plan permits the loan and your mortgage lender accepts and documents that source for your investment purchase. Obtain written approval and test repayment and launch liquidity before offering.

Can I use the longer principal-residence loan term for an STR?

Do not assume so. IRS guidance treats acquiring a principal residence differently; describe your investment and personal-use plans accurately and ask the administrator to apply its rules.

Does a lender's debt calculation show whether I can afford the plan loan?

Not by itself. Regardless of qualification treatment, include actual repayment in your household funding schedule and stress it against delayed STR opening and weaker property cash flow.

What happens to the plan loan if I leave my job after buying?

Your plan's terms and circumstances control the next steps. Ask about repayment and potential distribution or offset treatment before borrowing, and obtain personalized tax advice.

What a Five Star Stay Actually Costs to Deliver

Published February 4, 2026·6 min read

Owners frequently assume that excellent reviews require expensive properties. The drivers of review scores are cleanliness, accuracy, communication and check-in friction, and none of them are capital items. The cost of a five star stay is mostly attention.

The four drivers, ranked

Cleanliness first. It is the most frequently cited factor in negative reviews across the industry by a wide margin, and it is entirely a function of the cleaning process rather than the property.

Accuracy second. Guests rate against expectations, so an overselling listing produces disappointment regardless of quality. This costs nothing to fix.

Communication third. Response speed and proactive information, both of which are systems rather than spending.

Check-in friction fourth. A guest who cannot find the property or get in has formed a view before seeing a room.

What the cheap items actually cost

None of these appear in a listing photograph and all of them appear in reviews. That asymmetry is why properties that look identical online perform differently.

The expensive item that matters

Mattresses. Sleep quality is the most common driver of negative reviews after cleanliness, and a rental mattress does several times the work of a residential one.

The right approach is to buy quality and replace on a two to three year cycle in the most-used bedrooms rather than buying cheap and replacing more often. The cost per year is similar and the review outcome is not.

This is the one line in the furnishing budget that should not be optimized down, and it is the one most frequently cut when the budget runs over.

The cleaning cost is the review cost

A cleaner who is 15% cheaper and rushes is the most expensive economy available in short-term rental operation, because cleanliness is the top review driver.

Pay a rate that keeps a good team engaged, use photographic checklists so quality is verifiable rather than assumed, and confirm what happens on a same-day turnover when someone calls out before it happens.

Depth on the bench matters more than the rate. A missed turnover in peak season is not a late check-in, it is a cancellation, a refund and a review.

Attention is the largest input

The check-in follow-up message costs nothing and is the highest-value item in the entire guest sequence, because it surfaces problems while they are still fixable.

The review request costs nothing and materially improves review volume, which dilutes the weight of any individual bad review.

The response to a problem costs nothing beyond speed, and speed is the single largest factor in whether a mid-stay problem becomes a bad review.

What guests do not reward

High-end appliance brands, oversized televisions, expensive art and complex smart home systems. All cost real money and none appear in review language.

Elaborate welcome gifts. Appreciated, and less impactful than the basic consumables that prevent a first-night grocery run.

Excessive decor. It photographs as clutter at thumbnail size and it breaks.

The compounding argument

Reviews are an input to a ranking algorithm that determines how many people see the listing, which determines bookings, which determines reviews. The loop runs in both directions.

That means the cheap items are not merely nice, they are compounding. A property that earns consistent five stars from month one enters its first peak season ranked like an established listing, and that advantage persists into the second year.

It also means the cost of getting it wrong is not one bad review. It is a slower accumulation of reviews, a lower ranking position, and a property that has to compete on price rather than on position for its entire first year.

Where the money is actually wasted

The most common misallocation is spending on things that photograph well and are not used, while under-spending on things that are used constantly and never photographed.

Decorative throw pillows that guests move to the floor on the first night. A statement light fixture in a hallway. Expensive bedding covers over an inadequate mattress. Each of these is money that changed the listing photograph and not the stay.

The inverse holds too. A property with excellent mattresses, sharp knives, blackout curtains and good lighting delivers a materially better stay than a comparable one without, and none of it is visible in a search result.

The resolution is not to choose between them. Photography sells the booking and the stay produces the review, and both matter. The point is that the second category is far cheaper than the first, and it is the one that gets cut when the budget runs over.

Frequently asked questions

What drives five star Airbnb reviews?

Cleanliness first by a wide margin, then listing accuracy, communication and check-in friction. None of them are capital items, which means review performance is largely a function of process and attention rather than property quality.

What is the cheapest way to improve reviews?

Basic consumables supplied, blackout curtains, good pillows, sharp knives replaced annually, entry lighting and a printed entry card. None appear in listing photographs and all appear in reviews.

Where should I not economize?

Mattresses and cleaning. Sleep quality is the second most common review driver after cleanliness, and a cleaner who is cheaper and rushes is the most expensive economy available in short-term rental operation.

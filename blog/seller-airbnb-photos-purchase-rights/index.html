Can you use the seller's Airbnb photos after buying an STR?

Published September 25, 2026·6 min read

Do not assume that buying an operating short-term rental gives you the right to copy the seller's Airbnb photos into your new listing. The home, furniture and image files can change hands while the copyright or commercial-use license remains with a photographer, manager, agency or platform. Before paying a 'turnkey' price, request the photo contracts and a file-by-file rights list. Obtain a written license or assignment that covers your intended platforms and marketing, or price and schedule a new shoot. If the launch depends on those photos, resolve this before waiving purchase protection. Book a call to underwrite the STR's real launch handoff.

Separate ownership of the house from rights in its photos

The U.S. Copyright Office explains that the person who takes a photograph is generally its initial copyright owner, subject to limited work-made-for-hire exceptions. Its copyright statute says a transfer of copyright ownership generally requires a signed writing. That does not mean every buyer needs to own copyright: a sufficient commercial-use license may be enough. It does mean a seller's invoice, the property deed or a folder of image files is not, by itself, proof that the seller can pass all needed rights to you. Have counsel read the actual agreements.

Airbnb's current terms say hosts who provide content, including photos, must own it or have authority to grant Airbnb the specified rights. The terms note that Airbnb may own content it paid for or facilitated under supplemental terms. A seller's ability to display photos on their old listing does not establish that a new owner may download and republish those images. The seller-listing handoff guide handles account and booking control; photo rights are a separate asset question.

Build a photo-rights schedule before the diligence deadline

Ask for the original photographer or agency name, engagement agreement, invoice, any assignment or license, the manager's media terms, and any separate platform-photography terms. Match each current listing image to its creator and agreement. Check who may use the image, on which platforms, for how long, whether sublicensing or transfer to a buyer is permitted, and whether the license ends when the seller or manager leaves. Ask for original high-resolution files only after rights are confirmed; access to files is not the same as permission to publish them.

Then ask your broker and attorney to list the specific photos or media rights to be delivered at closing and any third-party consents required. If the seller cannot grant rights, contact the actual rights holder for a buyer license and written price and timing. Do not rely on a verbal 'we paid for the shoot' assurance or scrape images from the seller's live listing. Airbnb's content policy prohibits content that violates another person's intellectual-property rights. This is a purchase diligence check, not a recommendation to reuse images without counsel's approval.

Recalculate launch timing and maximum offer if a reshoot is needed

Get an actual local photography quote, scheduling window, editing turnaround and access date. Confirm whether furnishings and amenities in the seller's photos remain in the sale; even licensed photos may be misleading if the property will look different after closing. Link the shoot to cleaning, staging, permit status and the date you can lawfully publish your own listing. The new-listing ramp and cash-needed model should carry any media cost and no-booking interval. Do not insert an invented average photography cost or assume the seller's reviews transfer with the images.

Illustrative only: suppose your offer model assumed the buyer could launch on closing day, but written rights cannot be obtained and the first available shoot plus edits pushes publication two weeks later. This is a timing sensitivity, not a forecast. Replace the two weeks with a vendor's actual schedule, remove unsupported booking revenue for that interval, add the quote to cash needed, and ask whether your maximum offer still works. If the seller promised usable media as part of the purchase, have counsel evaluate the representation and a documented cure or credit; a credit cannot create rights the seller does not own.

Make a purchase decision, not a photography decision in isolation

Proceed when rights are documented for the planned use or a reshoot is funded and scheduled, and the buyer's conservative launch case still works. Renegotiate if missing media measurably changes cash needed, launch date or the value represented as included in the purchase. Delay under valid protection only for a specific rights-holder consent or quote. Reject if the deal requires immediate seller-photo reuse to meet debt service and no lawful, timely path is available. Frequently asked questions

Does the seller own the Airbnb photos because they paid the photographer?

Not necessarily. Payment alone does not establish ownership or a transferable commercial license. Review the signed photography agreement and obtain advice on its scope.

Can I copy the seller's photos from Airbnb for my new STR listing?

Do not assume so. Airbnb requires the person posting photos to own them or have authority to grant the platform its required rights. Obtain documented permission or shoot new images.

What if the property comes furnished but the photos do not transfer?

The furnishings and photo rights are separate. Price a new shoot and any launch delay, then compare that revised purchase case with other candidate properties.

Should missing photo rights kill an STR purchase?

Not automatically. If a lawful replacement shoot is feasible and the revised economics work, proceed or renegotiate. Reject a deal that depends on an immediate launch you cannot support.

Airbnb vs Long-Term Rentals: Which Actually Makes More Money?

Published August 10, 2026·7 min read

An Airbnb-versus-long-term-rental comparison should answer whether you should buy the property, not which strategy has the more impressive revenue headline. Compare the same address under two lawful uses, with actual buyer financing, supported receipts, owner-paid costs and setup cash. A higher-income buyer also needs a separate adviser-reviewed tax assessment, but a large tax bill does not make weak property economics acceptable. Neither strategy automatically makes more money.

Bring a candidate property and both rental scenarios to an acquisition call. This guide is for a purchase within the next zero to six months. It does not recommend an STR, a long-term lease or a tax position without the property's evidence and the buyer's constraints.

Start with rights and evidence, not a revenue multiple

Confirm the new owner's intended STR permission, applicable licensing, covenants and insurer acceptance. Separately establish whether the proposed long-term lease is allowed and feasible. Do not assume losing short-stay permission leaves an immediately executable lease fallback. Existing tenants, repair work, advertising time and contractual obligations can delay a change of use. The long-term fallback checklist starts that separate review.

For the STR case, reconcile seller receipts to the purchased property and support comparable revenue, available nights and seasonality. For the lease case, request comparable executed or supportable lease terms, vacancy and collection assumptions, concessions and responsibility for utilities. Asking rent is not collected rent. A strong holiday week is not an annual STR forecast. Remove owner-blocked dates and unavailable rooms before comparing either plan.

Ask the lender and insurer to confirm the actual use and terms for each alternative. Holding debt constant is useful for a controlled worksheet, but the final decision must reflect any real differences in eligibility, loan payment or coverage. Do not represent an investment STR as a second home to improve the quote. Set the evidence deadline while usable contract protections remain.

Record amounts, evidence dates, responsible reviewers and unresolved items. A management percentage may apply to a different revenue base than your worksheet assumes; establish what it covers and which services cost extra. Use the prospective operator's arrangement, not a universal expense band or workload-hour promise. Keep capital work and ordinary repairs distinct and do not deduct a replacement allowance twice.

Airbnb's current service-fee guidance describes split and single fee structures and an ongoing migration away from split fees. It states most single-fee hosts pay 15.5%, with exceptions. That is not a universal fee for every reservation or platform. Verify the actual prospective listing/manager treatment and the fee base, then include it once in the STR cost case. A seller's historical 3% assumption may not describe your new operation.

The STR base produces twice the collected revenue but only $8,000 more before debt. It has $2,000 cash after debt and before the retained allocation, not $72,000 of profit. Allocating $5,000 to future replacements creates a $3,000 funding requirement beyond current operating cash. The allocation remains cash if retained; it is not a repair already incurred. Do not call the debt-inclusive or allocation-adjusted balance NOI.

The downside reduces receipts by $15,000 while only $3,000 of costs disappear. The remaining $37,000 includes fixed and continuing obligations; lower bookings do not eliminate every bill. The resulting $15,000 after-allocation funding requirement must fit independently available capital. Neither base case passes a buyer requiring nonnegative annual cash after the stated allocations. An STR's relative advantage does not turn an unacceptable absolute result into a suitable purchase.

Now compare early cash. Suppose common purchase/closing funds total $130,000, while STR setup requires $35,000 and long-term preparation $8,000. Before retained reserves, commitments are $165,000 and $138,000, a $27,000 difference. Add the actual reserve target and separately dated carry costs. Do not add setup to the annual operating expense table or count a deposit again if credited at closing. Use the purchase budget to test all-in funds.

Evaluate tax suitability separately from purchase cash

IRS Publication 925 distinguishes rental-activity exceptions from material participation and distinguishes active from material participation. The seven-day average-use exception is not an automatic salary offset. The publication also addresses basis, at-risk and passive-loss restrictions; qualified advisers must evaluate applicable limitations and your ownership/use facts. Its currently published edition is 2025, checked October 7, 2026.

Ask the CPA to compare usable deductions and changes in tax payable under each actual strategy, including participation, personal use and disposition. Do not multiply an unsupported depreciation estimate by a marginal rate and call the result a refund. Long-term treatment is not universally a zero benefit, and short-term treatment is not universally an immediate benefit. See the smaller, delayed or unavailable benefit test. Closing and launch must work without speculative tax receipts.

Choose a purchase that survives its real responsibilities

Neither a lease nor a short reservation guarantees collection or timely possession. Ask local counsel about applicable occupancy and remedies, and ask the insurer about the intended operation. Request management response coverage, emergency responsibilities and service exclusions. Replace generic hours-per-month promises with a documented responsibility plan. Hiring help does not eliminate every owner task or determine tax participation.

Proceed when lawful use, supported receipts, quoted costs, early cash and a meaningful downside meet the buyer's actual requirements. Reprice a property when verified costs change an otherwise viable plan; do not assume a lender will accept the new structure. Delay within valid protections while decisive evidence is missing. Decline when both strategies fail or the fallback depends on unverified permissions, instant lease-up or an assumed refund.

Frequently asked questions

Do short-term rentals make more money than long-term rentals?

Not necessarily. Compare supported collected revenue, owner-paid costs, debt, setup cash and replacement reserves for the same property. Higher gross receipts can coexist with worse cash flow.

Are short-term rentals riskier than long-term rentals?

The exposures differ. Review legal use, permission changes, seasonality, collection, possession, insurance and operating responsibilities for each strategy. Neither guarantees timely payment or lawful vacant possession.

Why might a high-income buyer consider an STR?

The property may fit the buyer’s acquisition objectives, but income and a large tax bill do not establish suitability. Have a CPA separately assess activity classification, participation and loss limitations without funding the purchase from an assumed refund.

Can a long-term lease rescue an unsuccessful STR purchase?

Only if the actual long-term use is lawful and its separate rent, expenses, conversion costs and timing support the fallback. Do not substitute a percentage of STR revenue for local lease evidence.

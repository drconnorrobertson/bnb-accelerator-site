Should you buy an STR if the seller won't share revenue records?

Published September 25, 2026·6 min read

An operating short-term rental is listed at a price justified by its reported earnings, but the seller offers only a headline number or a handwritten spreadsheet. Should you still make an offer? You can, but do not pay a proven-income premium for revenue you cannot verify. Set a short, specific request for source records, protect the diligence deadline, and underwrite an alternative price based on the real estate and your own forward case. If the seller will not permit verification and the deal works only on the claimed history, reject it. Book a call to screen an STR seller's revenue claim before you commit.

Separate an access problem from a proof problem

Ask the seller or manager exactly who controls each booking channel and payout account. A listing agent may have a summary without access to the host dashboard; a manager may have platform reports but need the owner's consent. Request a dated, property-specific, month-by-month record of booked nights, guest charges, refunds, channel fees, taxes, owner payouts, and management deductions for the period being marketed. Specify whether you need an export, a redacted statement, a supervised screen-share, or secure access for an accountant. Do not ask for passwords, guest identities, payment card details, or unrestricted access to the seller's private account.

This is a feasible request, not an assumption that every seller can instantly produce the same format. Airbnb's current host instructions explain that hosts can download monthly and annual earnings reports, create custom listing-and-date reports, and export a CSV of earnings. Vrbo's owner instructions describe downloading payment history. If a third-party manager operated the property, ask for the manager's property-level ledger and the owner's statements too. These reports still need reconciliation; they are not a guarantee that the buyer will replicate past performance.

Reasonable privacy, accountant, or manager-approval concerns can be solved with a narrow data request, redaction, a confidentiality agreement reviewed by counsel, or a controlled review. The key buyer question is whether the seller will cooperate in an auditable way before your protections expire. A claim that records 'do not exist' deserves a different valuation than a claim that they exist but need to be prepared securely.

Protect the STR offer and the evidence deadline

Before signing, have your agent and local attorney identify the exact contract right to receive and review operating information, the seller's delivery deadline, the buyer's review period after delivery, and what happens if records are late, incomplete, or inconsistent. Do not rely on an oral promise that documents will come 'after acceptance' when your diligence clock has already started. A general inspection contingency may not say what operating data must be produced; local contract language controls, and counsel should draft or select the appropriate provision.

Use a minimum evidence list rather than an open-ended demand: channel statements or exports by listing and month; bank or manager remittance evidence with unrelated account activity masked; a booking ledger showing stays versus payouts; expenses needed to turn gross revenue into an owner-level result; and a record of owner-blocked dates, major outages, refunds, or channel changes. Ask the lender what it will accept if financing depends on STR income. The payout-reconciliation guide explains the math once records arrive; this page is about whether to place capital at risk when they do not.

Set one documented follow-up, a realistic delivery date, and a decision date before earnest money or other rights become harder to recover. An extension should be written and signed under the contract, not assumed because someone is 'working on it.' If records are partial, state which periods or channels remain unverified and whether the missing portion is material to the price.

Price only what the evidence supports

Build two cases: an independently supported acquisition case using the property's physical features, legal STR eligibility, market comps, realistic launch costs, and your own conservative revenue estimate; and a seller-history case that is admitted into the model only to the extent source records reconcile. Do not treat a broker's projection, listing screenshots, calendar occupancy, or future bookings as settled cash. Airbnb explains that host payouts can differ from guest prices because of service fees and other adjustments in its payout calculation guide.

Illustrative only: a seller asks $640,000, citing $92,000 of annual gross guest charges but will not share platform or manager records. The buyer's independent analysis supports a $575,000 ceiling based on a conservative launch case. The $65,000 gap is not automatically the value of the claimed history; it is the amount the buyer is being asked to justify without proof. If records later support a stronger transferable operating case, re-run the maximum-offer worksheet. These invented amounts are not a market statistic, appraisal, or expected return.

Account for transfer risk separately. The seller's listing, reviews, manager economics, direct-booking audience, and permit status may not follow the deed. Even verified historic earnings do not automatically become the buyer's first-year earnings. The operating-STR purchase guide covers what may or may not transfer. If the seller is pricing a business that is not actually part of the sale, the evidence gap is larger than a missing spreadsheet.

Decide: proceed, reprice, delay, or reject

Proceed when the seller provides property-level source records, they reconcile sufficiently for your purpose, the financing and legal-use checks pass, and the price works even after transfer and launch costs. Renegotiate when claims are materially lower after verification, or when the seller will not provide proof and the price assumes an operating premium; show the revised buyer model rather than accusing the seller of misconduct. Delay under a valid written extension if a manager can deliver the missing reports before rights expire. Reject when the seller refuses any reasonable verification, the contract leaves you exposed, and the deal needs the unproven revenue to work.

A seller's refusal is not proof of fraud; it is a limit on the buyer's knowledge. Do not bypass privacy rules to obtain guest or account data. Use local counsel for disclosures, contract rights, and remedies, and a qualified accountant and lender for financial and financing questions. Frequently asked questions

Should I offer on an Airbnb for sale without seeing its earnings records?

Only if your contract protects a timely review and your price can be justified without assuming the unverified history. Specify the records and delivery deadline with local counsel before the offer becomes binding.

What if the seller says Airbnb will not provide a revenue report?

Airbnb currently describes host-downloadable monthly, annual, custom, and CSV earnings reports. Ask who owns the listing and whether the manager or seller can provide a property-specific, privacy-conscious export or controlled review.

Can I value an operating STR using only a seller spreadsheet?

Treat the spreadsheet as an assertion to test. Reconcile it to platform or manager records and owner payouts, then make a separate forward model for the buyer's post-closing operation.

When should I walk away from an STR with missing records?

Reject it when the seller will not allow reasonable verification, your protections are expiring, and the purchase price only works if the unproved revenue claim is true.

Sevierville and Gatlinburg Airbnb Guide

Published July 21, 2026·8 min read

The Sevierville, Pigeon Forge, and Gatlinburg corridor is the most proven large cabin short-term rental market in the United States. It is also the market where the difference between a good purchase and a mediocre one is largest, because a single variable, sleeping capacity, drives revenue more than anything else.

Why the demand is durable

Great Smoky Mountains National Park draws more visitors annually than any other national park in the country, and unlike most park adjacent markets, this one has a built out entertainment economy alongside it: Dollywood, Ripley's attractions, dinner theaters, distilleries, and an enormous concentration of restaurants and shopping.

That combination produces four distinct demand seasons. Summer family travel, fall leaf season which is frequently the strongest of the year, the winter holiday period, and spring break. Markets carried by a single season live and die on twelve weeks. This one does not.

It is also overwhelmingly drive-to. The corridor sits within a day's drive of a large share of the eastern United States population, which is why occupancy has held up in years when fly-to leisure markets softened.

The bedroom count math

This is the part most out of state buyers get wrong. Revenue does not scale linearly with bedrooms. It steps.

  • Two to three bedrooms. Couples and small families. The most competitive segment with the most alternatives, including hotels.
  • Four to five bedrooms. Two families traveling together. Better rate, meaningfully less competition.
  • Six to eight bedrooms. Multi family reunions and small groups. The guest is now comparing against booking multiple hotel rooms, which resets their price anchor entirely.
  • Nine or more bedrooms. Family reunions, church groups, corporate retreats, and wedding parties. Thin inventory, minimal direct competition, and rates that reflect it. One nine bedroom property we underwrote produced roughly $20,000 in a single month. The full breakdown is here.

The amenity package matters in proportion. At six bedrooms and above, guests expect a game room, a hot tub, theater seating, and enough parking for four vehicles. A large cabin without those loses to a similar cabin that has them, at the same price.

What we underwrite against

  1. Supply growth. Cabin permits have grown for years in Sevier County. New inventory arrives newer and better amenitized. We model a softening rate assumption rather than trailing twelve month performance.
  2. Road access and grade. A steep, unpaved, or switchback approach costs bookings in winter and generates negative reviews year round. This is a physical due diligence item, not a detail.
  3. View quality. Genuine mountain views command a real premium. A view of the neighboring cabin does not, whatever the listing photos suggest.
  4. Insurance and maintenance cost. Hot tubs, decks, and mountain weather produce a heavier maintenance line than flatland inventory. Budget it honestly.
  5. Extended winter stays. Some owners fill January and February with month long bookings. Excellent for occupancy, dangerous for the seven day average that the tax position depends on.

Why this corridor suits the tax strategy

Two reasons. First, average stays are short, typically two to four nights, so satisfying the seven day average test under Treasury Regulation 1.469-1T(e)(3)(ii)(A) is straightforward if you manage the booking mix. Second, cabin inventory is unusually rich in short life property: hot tubs, decking, game room finishes, specialty lighting, and extensive site work including driveways and retaining structures.

That combination is why reclassification percentages on these properties frequently land at the higher end of the typical 25 to 35 percent range. See cost segregation for Airbnb properties, material participation, and our partner firm's material on cost segregation studies.

Frequently asked questions

Is Gatlinburg a good Airbnb investment market?

It is the most proven large cabin short-term rental corridor in the country. Great Smoky Mountains National Park draws more visitors than any other, the surrounding entertainment economy produces four distinct demand seasons, and the market is drive-to rather than fly-to, which supports occupancy in soft travel years.

How many bedrooms should a Smoky Mountain cabin have?

Revenue steps rather than scaling linearly. Two and three bedroom cabins compete in the most crowded segment. Six to eight bedrooms reach guests comparing against multiple hotel rooms. Nine or more reach reunions, church groups, and corporate retreats with thin competing inventory and rates that reflect it.

What amenities do Smoky Mountain cabins need?

At six bedrooms and above, guests expect a hot tub, a game room, theater seating, and parking for several vehicles. A large cabin missing those loses to a comparable cabin that has them at the same nightly rate. View quality and road access also materially affect both rate and reviews.

What is the biggest risk in the Sevierville market?

Supply growth. Cabin permits have expanded for years and new inventory arrives newer and better amenitized, so we underwrite against a softening rate assumption rather than trailing twelve month performance. Road grade, view quality, and maintenance cost are the other recurring due diligence items.

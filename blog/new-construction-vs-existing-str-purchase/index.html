Should you buy a new-construction STR or an existing property?

Published September 24, 2026·6 min read

Buy the property with the stronger permission-adjusted, all-in return—not the one with the newer kitchen or the longer listing history. A new build may reduce near-term repair exposure and let you design for guests, but it can add delivery, approval, furnishing, and no-revenue carrying risk. An existing home may launch sooner and offer better local comparables, but condition, layout, and past revenue require verification. Compare both at the date each can legally take its first booking, then test a delayed-opening case before making an offer.

First, define which two purchases you are comparing

A completed spec home, a presale home still under construction, a never-rented existing house, and an operating Airbnb are four different evidence sets. Ask for an address, parcel, completion status, seller inclusions, HOA documents, and the exact proposed use. Do not call a builder's concept art a revenue comparable or assume an operating listing's host reviews will transfer. The operating STR versus conversion guide handles the latter choice; this guide focuses on the construction and delivery decision.

Comparing a builder offer with an existing STR in the same target market? Book a BNB Accelerator acquisition call to put both properties on one all-in-cost and first-booking timeline before you commit.

Confirm lawful hosting for each address before comparing projected returns. Airbnb's responsible-hosting guidance notes that zoning, registration, permits, and local rules can restrict short stays. Obtain current written guidance from the actual jurisdiction and review recorded covenants and HOA rules. A builder's phrase 'Airbnb friendly' is not a permit, and a seller's current use does not prove a new buyer can continue it. See the permit-transfer diligence guide.

Build one comparable acquisition ledger

Put each option in the same model. Separate contract price from the dollars and months needed to produce a legal, guest-ready listing. Use quotes, lender terms, and local comps—not a generic percentage premium for new construction or an advertised gross-revenue estimate.

Compare lender offers on matching assumptions. The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau's Loan Estimate guide directs borrowers to compare rate, fees, lender credits, monthly payment, and cash to close; a lower quoted rate alone does not settle the deal. For a property not yet complete, ask the lender how the appraisal, completion verification, rate lock, and any extension are handled. Fannie Mae's completion guidance describes verification for new or proposed construction in applicable loans. Your lender must confirm the rules for your actual product.

Underwrite the calendar, not just the annual revenue

Model month-by-month cash flow from contract signing through twelve months after first booking. A presale may require deposits and upgrades before closing; after closing, debt service, insurance, taxes, utilities, setup, and management costs may begin before guest revenue. A completed resale may still need weeks of renovation or permit work. The key date is not 'closing' but the first date the property is legal, safe, furnished, photographed, listed, and bookable.

Illustrative only: assume two candidates each support the same $100,000 annual gross revenue after stabilization. Candidate A requires $40,000 more all-in cash and opens four months later than Candidate B. At an illustrative $8,000 monthly gross run rate, the delayed period represents $32,000 of gross bookings that A cannot earn—not $32,000 of profit. Subtract variable costs only from bookings actually expected, and add A's carrying costs and any seasonal timing effect. Replace these figures with property-specific comps and signed bids. Cash-to-buy and downside underwriting help keep those lines separate.

Do not count builder warranty as business-interruption coverage. The FTC explains that new-home warranties vary and generally do not cover out-of-pocket consequences of defects and repairs. Read the actual warranty, exclusions, claim process, and remedy; then obtain an STR-specific insurance quote for the intended use.

Use a three-gate decision before signing

  1. Permission gate: obtain address-specific written confirmation of zoning, permit path, occupancy limits, HOA or covenant restrictions, and any construction or resale transfer conditions.
  2. Capital gate: obtain a complete, itemized all-in cost and written financing terms. Include the required liquidity reserve after closing and launch, not merely the down payment.
  3. Evidence gate: validate guest demand with appropriately matched operating comparables. Discount a builder's projection, a seller's unverified spreadsheet, and properties with materially different location, capacity, amenities, or operating history.

Proceed when all three gates pass, the launch date is credible, and the delayed-opening downside remains acceptable. Renegotiate if the builder's upgrades, timeline, financing or the resale's repair scope changes the supported value. Walk away—or make approval a true contract contingency—if STR permission is unresolved or the only path to the required return depends on an unapproved amenity, an optimistic opening date, or unverifiable revenue.

A done-for-you acquisition team should compare actual available alternatives, document the assumptions, and make the tradeoff visible; it cannot guarantee permits, completion dates, revenue, or returns. Frequently asked questions

Is new construction automatically a better Airbnb investment?

No. Compare lawful hosting, all-in cost, first-booking date, credible revenue comps, financing, and delayed-opening downside for the specific properties.

Can I use a builder's Airbnb revenue projection to finance the purchase?

Treat it as a sales assumption until independent local comparables support it. Ask the lender what income evidence it accepts for the actual loan product.

Does a builder warranty eliminate early STR operating risk?

No. Read the warranty's specific coverage and exclusions, and model potential downtime and out-of-pocket costs separately.

When should I prefer an existing STR?

It may win when permission is verified, condition and past performance withstand diligence, and a faster or lower-cost launch produces a better risk-adjusted result than the new-build alternative.

Blanket loans vs individual mortgages for a short-term rental portfolio

Published September 24, 2026·7 min read

Before financing another STR, compare the cash and contractual flexibility of separate mortgages with one facility secured by multiple properties. One payment can be convenient, but it does not prove lower total costs, protected personal assets or an easy sale of one rental. If your next acquisition depends on selling a property from the pool, test the written release conditions before committing that money. This guide provides a document register and hypothetical release-cash comparison—not a lender recommendation or an approval estimate.

Discuss the next acquisition with a funded budget and a lender-question list. BNB Accelerator does not lend or arrange loans; financing terms and legal conclusions belong to the appropriate independent professionals.

How each structure works

With separate mortgages, review the note and security documents for each property. Each loan can have different qualification, payment, maturity and prepayment conditions. With a blanket facility, multiple properties secure one obligation or coordinated facility, and the documents govern how individual assets enter or leave it. Do not assume a product called a portfolio loan necessarily has the same structure as another lender's similarly named product.

Separate collateral and separate liability are different questions. An individual-property loan may still involve a personal guarantee, shared borrower obligations or cross-default provisions. Neither an LLC nor a one-property mortgage, by itself, proves that difficulties cannot affect other assets or financing. Counsel should identify the actual exposure, not infer it from the number of monthly statements. The guarantee and entity guide addresses the signer question separately.

Provider-specific example: Visio's lending-process page says entity owners must sign personal guarantees. That is a statement about that provider's process, not a universal requirement or a description of your proposed loan. Ask who signs, what obligations the document covers and what would release the signer.

Where consolidation can help—and what to price

A coordinated facility may simplify administration or support an acquisition plan using several stabilized properties. Request a dated comparison of every closing cost, ongoing reporting obligation, reserve requirement and later release fee. One closing does not eliminate separate title, valuation or legal work. A larger advertised loan amount is not unrestricted cash available for the next down payment.

Ask the lender to reconcile gross proceeds to existing payoffs, financing expenses, restricted reserves and cash actually disbursed. Match each outflow to the relevant property or facility and deduct it once. If stronger rentals support weaker ones in a pooled calculation, retain property-level operating downside as well: passing a combined test does not make the weaker purchase economically sound.

CoreVest's rental-portfolio description lists multiple-property financing, several shorter term options and both recourse and non-recourse choices, with exceptions for the latter. Its STR product page describes multiple-property financing with a thirty-year term. These are different product descriptions, not quotes or approval for your portfolio. Do not conclude that every blanket loan must have a short balloon—or that any STR qualifies for either product.

The sale-release cash worksheet

Hypothetical arithmetic only, not a client result, current loan quote or typical release formula. Compare two alternative financing structures for the same assumed future $400,000 property sale. Assume $24,000 of distinct selling outflows in both cases. In the individual-loan case, the complete settlement payoff is $260,000, with no additional lender charge under these assumptions. In the blanket case, assume a transaction-specific $300,000 principal release payment and $5,000 of separate release costs. No percentage rule or provider program is implied.

Illustrative caseSale cash before tax and other obligations
Individual mortgage$400,000 − $24,000 − $260,000 = $116,000
Blanket release before separate costs$400,000 − $24,000 − $300,000 = $76,000
Blanket release including separate costs$76,000 − $5,000 = $71,000
Assumed $95,000 next-purchase requirementIndividual case: $21,000 headroom; blanket case: $24,000 shortfall

The $45,000 difference between $116,000 and $71,000 is a cash-availability difference, not automatically a loss: the higher principal payment also reduces debt. Tax provisions, remaining portfolio debt, original capital and operating flows are omitted. Replace every input with reviewed figures before judging affordability or return. An unresolved tax or payoff input is not zero. The $95,000 requirement is also invented; build your actual closing and reserve budget independently.

A second gate: does the remaining pool qualify?

Continue the fictional blanket case with $900,000 of facility principal before release. If the entire $300,000 release payment is credited to principal and the $5,000 fee is separate, the remaining balance is $600,000. That calculation does not establish the remaining payment or approval. Request the lender's actual recalculation and accounting treatment.

Suppose, solely for illustration, the remaining properties produce $72,000 of lender-recognized annual net operating income and $60,000 of annual debt service, under a stipulated matching calculation basis. Coverage is 1.20. If this fictional agreement requires 1.25 after release, it would need $75,000 of recognized income at unchanged debt service—a $3,000 gap. These are invented conditions, not a CoreVest or Visio rule. STR income recognition and debt-service definitions must come from your lender.

Thus a buyer could afford the release payment yet fail the remaining-pool test. Do not manufacture extra income, assume further paydown produces a particular payment, or count on an available refinance. Ask for written alternatives and their costs. If release is unavailable, a whole-facility payoff or different transaction may be needed; neither is guaranteed to fit the next property's deadline.

Rates, terms and prepayment

Compare rate period, payment calculation, amortization and contractual maturity separately. A thirty-year amortization with earlier maturity can still leave principal due before your intended hold ends. A rate fixed through the full maturity avoids a scheduled rate reset within that period, but not higher insurance, taxes, repairs or a forced early-sale decision. Price the relevant prepayment conditions and release costs instead of treating one fixed-rate label as risk-free financing.

How to decide

Separate mortgages may fit staggered sales and different property plans, but confirm actual payoff and liability terms. A blanket facility may fit a documented coordinated hold, provided its release, reserve, reporting and downside conditions are acceptable. Compare written offers on the same proposed assets, dates and cash basis. The lower initial rate cannot decide an unfunded release or maturity problem.

Proceed when the next acquisition is funded without unapproved proceeds, the portfolio downside fits your limits and professionals have resolved decisive document questions. Reprice when verified financing costs change a viable deal. Seek a protected delay when critical lender review is incomplete; do not assume extension or cancellation rights. Decline a transaction that requires an unsupported release, refinance or tax receipt.

Frequently asked questions

Do separate mortgages completely isolate each property’s risk?

No. Separate collateral does not establish the limits of personal guarantees, cross-default terms or shared borrower obligations. Have counsel review the actual documents alongside each lender’s written conditions.

Can I sell one STR out of a blanket loan?

Only on the terms applicable to your loan and transaction. Obtain the release amount, fees, remaining-pool requirements, approval process and timing in writing before relying on sale proceeds.

Does a blanket loan always have a short term or balloon?

No. Provider descriptions and products differ. Compare the actual maturity, amortization, rate period and prepayment conditions; a long amortization schedule alone does not prove a long maturity.

Is the release payment my investment loss or taxable gain?

No. It is a settlement cash input under the stated assumptions. Loan balances, asset basis, tax character and total investment return require separate reconciliations.

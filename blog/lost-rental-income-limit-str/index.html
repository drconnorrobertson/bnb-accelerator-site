How Much Lost-Rental-Income Coverage Does an STR Need?

Published September 23, 2026·Updated October 8, 2026·6 min read

Size lost-rental-income coverage against the specific policy and the property's seasonal restoration exposure before buying an STR. There is no universal number of months or gross-margin formula that makes a purchase protected. Ask a licensed insurance professional how the proposed form calculates a covered loss, then separately test whether your accessible cash can pay continuing bills before any claim money arrives. A headline limit, annual-average revenue or the seller's policy cannot settle both questions.

For a buyer closing within the next six months, this is an acquisition decision: can the property remain financially supportable through an interruption without relying on an unapproved claim, future bookings or a tax benefit? Start with the address-specific quote and binding checklist, then add this exposure file before deciding what capital to commit.

Ask which income definition the proposed policy uses

Travelers' general commercial coverage explanation describes business income as net income plus continuing normal operating expenses. It separately discusses extra expense, restoration and possible extended income after operations resume. A waiting period or deductible may apply. This is general insurer guidance, not an STR quote or approval for your address.

Proper's STR coverage page instead markets an actual-loss calculation based on gross booking revenue, seasonal earning records and a selected dollar limit through restoration. This is Proper's commercial description, not a universal definition or evidence that your loss will be paid. Its disclaimer says actual terms, conditions and loss circumstances control. Have the professional identify the form you would purchase; do not import one provider's formula into another policy.

Compare like-for-like answers, not merely the word “income.” Ask whether quoted amounts concern fair rental value, business income, business revenue or another defined benefit, which expenses affect the calculation and which records support a newly acquired or not-yet-open property. No seller forecast, lender model or marketing page establishes the claim amount. Obtain actual documents and written explanations rather than an assumed reimbursement rate.

Build a six-field coverage-answer register

Keep the proposal date, form reference, responsible reviewer and unanswered question beside each field. Request clarification before relying on a limit in your purchase case.

Use qualified contractors and the relevant authority to identify likely repair dependencies; ask the insurance professional how those dependencies interact with the policy. A modeled permitting delay is not automatically an insured period. Building repair, lawful reopening and demand recovery may have different dates. Do not assume extended income, extra expense or unlimited duration is included because a quote lists business income.

Original seasonal closure worksheet: averages hide the timing

Invented planning figures only: suppose a candidate's supported hypothetical annual booking revenue is $120,000. A simple six-month average is $60,000. Instead model an April-through-September closure, with the following fictional monthly schedule. These are not market statistics, a seller result or a claim calculation.

The six closed months contain $72,000 of revenue, $12,000 more than the annual-average shortcut. For consistency, the other six fictional months total $48,000; the whole year remains $120,000. Confirm each month's revenue sources, refunds, owner blocks and expense treatment using the matching-receipts-and-costs guide.

In this simplified operating comparison, $72,000 minus $20,000 avoided costs minus $18,000 continuing bills would have produced $34,000 before other items. During closure, zero revenue and those continuing bills produce negative $18,000. The difference is $52,000, not $72,000 plus $18,000: continuing bills are already present in both cases. That economic comparison is not a settlement estimate. A gross-revenue form and a different income definition need their own insurer-reviewed calculation.

Separate the claim question from cash available to pay bills

Continue the fictional example with $65,000 accessible cash after the purchase and unrelated launch allocations. Protect a $30,000 operating floor. Six months of continuing bills cost $18,000; add $6,000 distinct emergency mitigation bills and a stipulated $5,000 owner-funded deductible obligation. These amounts are invented, not typical terms. Early outflows total $29,000, leaving $36,000 cash and $6,000 above the floor. Do not add the $72,000 missed bookings as another invoice.

Test two further months offline. Assume $6,000 additional continuing bills and $4,000 distinct mitigation or reopening work, not already in any earlier line. Cumulative cash outflows become $39,000; cash falls to $26,000, a $4,000 gap below the protected floor. Analyze extra foregone revenue separately. The longer delay is an operating stress, not proof the policy must cover every day.

If an illustrative $12,000 claim payment actually clears only after those bills were due, it would later raise $26,000 to $38,000. It cannot fund earlier invoices. This is a timing demonstration, not a promised advance, coverage decision or recovery amount. Also test no payment. Never include an unresolved claim receivable, projected tax refund or unapproved credit as accessible cash. Identify actual financing, price changes or additional funds before committing; none is assumed available.

Turn the answer into a purchase decision

Proceed when the actual coverage explanation, binding path, supported seasonal exposure and independently funded cash stress fit your requirements. Request a revised proposal or purchase economics when verified gaps change the offer ceiling. Use a valid contractual extension only after the agent or counsel confirms its terms. Reject or defer a purchase that requires unsupported coverage, unavailable funds or a reopening date no responsible party can substantiate.

For high-income buyers with deployable capital, the ability to buy does not establish the ability or willingness to carry an interrupted rental. Keep the purchase cash worksheet separate from the insurance calculation. Bring the candidate, coverage-answer register and cash stress to a BNB acquisition call.

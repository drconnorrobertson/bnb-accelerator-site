Short-Term Rental Investing for Attorneys

Published May 5, 2026·6 min read

Attorneys ask better questions than most buyers and get stuck in the same two places: whether the position is genuinely supportable, and whether the documentation would survive scrutiny. Both are reasonable concerns and both have concrete answers.

The authority, plainly stated

The provision is Treasury Regulation 1.469-1T(e)(3)(ii)(A), which excludes from the definition of rental activity an activity where the average period of customer use is seven days or less. This is not an aggressive reading of an ambiguity. It is an explicit exception in the regulation, and the consequence is that the automatic passive characterization applicable to rental activities under Section 469 does not attach.

From there, the ordinary material participation analysis applies. Most owners rely on the test requiring more than 100 hours with no other individual participating more, which is one of seven tests in the regulations.

What the position requires is not a novel argument. It requires facts: an average stay computed correctly, participation that actually occurred, and records that demonstrate both. See the seven day rule and how it differs from real estate professional status.

The evidentiary standard

Think of it as a file you would want to hand to opposing counsel. Three elements:

  1. Average period of customer use. Arithmetic supported by a complete platform reservation export showing every booking with dates.
  2. Material participation. A contemporaneous log with specific descriptions, corroborated by independent evidence such as message timestamps, vendor communications, receipts, and travel records.
  3. Depreciation position. An engineering based cost segregation study with documented methodology, land allocation, and a supportable placed-in-service date.

Reconstructed logs with round numbers are the recognizable failure pattern. See documentation that holds up.

The practical constraint for practicing attorneys

Billable hour requirements create the same problem clinical hours do: real estate professional status is unavailable, and the 100 hour test has to be met without a full service manager absorbing the work. A co-host structure that leaves pricing, calendar, and guest communication with the owner is the common resolution, and for married attorneys a spouse's hours are generally counted. See co-hosting versus self managing and spousal participation.

Structure questions attorneys raise

  • Entity ownership. A single member LLC is generally disregarded for federal income tax purposes and does not change the tax analysis, though it may serve a liability purpose and does affect financing. See holding an STR in an LLC.
  • Regulatory risk. Municipal ordinances, HOA covenants, and pending council action are all knowable before closing and should be verified in writing. See the verification checklist.
  • Exit consequences. Accelerated depreciation reduces basis and increases gain, with recapture applying to portions of it. Model the exit before the study. See recapture explained.

Frequently asked questions

What is the authority for the short-term rental tax position?

Treasury Regulation 1.469-1T(e)(3)(ii)(A) excludes from the definition of rental activity an activity where the average period of customer use is seven days or less. It is an explicit exception rather than an aggressive reading, and the ordinary material participation analysis then applies.

What documentation supports the position?

Three elements: a complete platform reservation export supporting the average period of customer use computation, a contemporaneous participation log with specific descriptions corroborated by independent evidence, and an engineering based cost segregation study with documented methodology and placed-in-service date.

Can a practicing attorney meet material participation?

Usually through the test requiring more than 100 hours with no other individual participating more, which requires a management structure that does not absorb the work. A co-host arrangement is the common resolution, and for married attorneys a spouse's hours are generally counted toward the tests.

Does holding the property in an LLC change the tax analysis?

A single member LLC is generally disregarded for federal income tax purposes, so it does not by itself change the analysis. It may serve a liability purpose and it does affect financing, since conventional residential loans are generally made to individuals rather than entities.

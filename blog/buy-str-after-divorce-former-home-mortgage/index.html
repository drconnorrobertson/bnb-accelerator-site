Can you buy an STR after divorce while still on the old mortgage?

Published October 6, 2026·7 min read

Possibly, but your former spouse paying the old mortgage is not the same as the creditor releasing you from it. Before offering on an investment STR, have the new lender determine the old debt's treatment from the actual documents, and test whether retained cash can absorb the exposure alongside the rental's launch costs. This guide is for a buyer ready to acquire a short-term rental, not a replacement-home or divorce-planning guide. Book a call to screen STR purchases against your verified post-divorce financing and cash limits.

Resolve old mortgage liability before setting the STR search budget

Start with the note, current mortgage statement, relevant court or settlement documents, any creditor release and actual payment records. Ask qualified counsel what obligations remain and ask the new lender which evidence it needs to qualify the proposed STR loan. Do not tell the acquisition team the old payment has disappeared simply because you moved out, changed title or agreed that your former spouse would pay.

Rocket Mortgage's post-divorce buying guide distinguishes remaining mortgage responsibility from removing a name from the loan and discusses creditor release or refinancing. Your purchase decision has two tracks. The new investment lender decides whether the existing obligation enters its qualifying calculation. Separately, the buyer and counsel assess continuing exposure under the old documents. Passing the first track does not erase the second. A property-income lending product can also have its own borrower conditions; a DSCR label is not proof that the old debt or cash obligations are irrelevant.

Use the preapproval-before-property framework with the revised individual borrower file. Ask for the investment-STR product, accepted debt treatment, cash requirements and unresolved conditions. Set the search ceiling from that reviewed file rather than from a pre-divorce approval or the income previously available to a joint household.

Do not combine court-assigned debt with the paid-by-others exception

Under applicable Fannie Mae monthly-debt guidance, court-ordered assignment can allow a lender to omit a contingent debt from recurring obligations even without creditor release. Pre-assignment payment history still matters. A different mortgage-paid-by-others route requires an obligated paying party, documented twelve-month payment history without delinquency and no qualifying rental income from that property. Ask the lender to state the specific treatment and supporting evidence, not merely that divorce paperwork is on file. An informal payment arrangement and a court-assigned obligation are not interchangeable categories. Do not manufacture a payment history, conceal joint debts or ask an acquisition adviser to decide which legal document should be treated as a court order.

Review property count and retained-asset requirements separately using the next-STR financed-property register. Carry the actual former property and obligations into the lender's complete inventory instead of assuming one favorable answer clears every test.

Budget the STR from released funds and the current household obligations

Build the acquisition cash sheet from money actually available to you and accepted by the lender. An anticipated settlement payment, future home sale or proposed refinance is not a completed dollar transfer. Have counsel confirm the relevant terms and authority for the funds; have the lender verify source and timing. If payment depends on another transaction, do not set an STR deposit deadline as though that transaction is already finished.

Keep ongoing housing, documented support or equalization obligations, legal commitments and the new investment loan visible in the household plan. The lender must review the actual qualifying treatment. Your own cash planning should also reflect amounts you expect to pay, including obligations that do not operate identically in a debt-to-income worksheet. Do not count a former spouse's income as your borrowing support without an actual accepted borrower arrangement.

Illustrative purchase budget, not a required reserve amount: the buyer has $210,000 of verified available cash. The STR requires $115,000 down, $15,000 closing expenses and $30,000 opening costs. Those uses total $160,000, leaving $50,000. Suppose the buyer independently allocates $20,000 to existing personal/legal commitments and preserves a $20,000 rental planning buffer. Only $10,000 remains outside those allocations; the headline $50,000 balance is not all acquisition flexibility.

Reconcile this plan with actual lender reserve requirements, which are a separate calculation. Do not assume every chosen household buffer qualifies as a lender reserve asset or can be freely spent after closing. Use the complete purchase-and-launch planner and date each funding assumption. If released funds are lower than anticipated, reduce the candidate budget or wait; do not refill the gap with assumed early bookings.

Stress-test simultaneous old-home exposure and a weak STR opening

A lender's accepted exclusion does not make the old payment harmless in the buyer's own downside analysis. Ask counsel which exposure remains and how an actual problem would need to be handled. Model a plausible cash contingency without treating it as a prediction of your former spouse's behavior. Keep private family facts out of a public investment model and share only necessary documents through appropriate secure channels.

Continue the illustrative budget: suppose the buyer tests three months of covering a $3,000 old-home monthly payment while resolving an issue, plus three months of a $1,500 monthly STR cash shortfall. The separate stress amounts are $9,000 and $4,500, totaling $13,500. Against the example's $10,000 unallocated margin, the test is $3,500 short. These are hypothetical cash assumptions, not legal conclusions that you must make either payment or a forecast of rental performance.

The result is a purchase warning, even if the new lender accepts the file. Review an appropriately smaller acquisition, additional genuinely available cash or delaying until exposure and commitments are better resolved. Simply spending the chosen rental buffer changes the risk plan rather than fixing the shortfall. Do not count a seller credit toward obligations it cannot actually cover under the contract and loan rules.

Underwrite the STR independently with the property downside framework. Verify legal rental use, realistic expenses, revenue evidence and launch timing at the address. Strong borrower eligibility is not a reason to accept a weak property. Conversely, a profitable-looking forecast is not evidence that an unresolved personal obligation or unreceived settlement payment can fund closing.

Choose the offer outcome from documents, liquidity and dates

Proceed when the lender confirms the actual investment loan and debt treatment, counsel resolves the relevant ownership/funding questions, verified funds cover closing and opening, and the combined downside plan is acceptable. Renegotiate price or timing when a defined cash or documentation issue can be addressed through an appropriate protected agreement. Delay while a release, settlement transfer or lender review is only expected. Reject a purchase plan requiring undisclosed debt, unavailable money or unsupported income to perform the contract.

Prepare a concise acquisition memo with the proposed STR address, current borrower income, lender-reviewed old-debt treatment, remaining creditor exposure as explained by counsel, available dollar funds and the combined cash stress. Name the unresolved condition and its deadline. Do not include intimate family history that does not affect the acquisition. Have the licensed agent and attorney review appropriate contingencies before deposit protection expires.

Frequently asked questions

Can I buy an STR if my former spouse pays the old mortgage?

Possibly, but disclose the debt and obtain the actual lender's documented treatment. Separately review any remaining creditor exposure and whether the new purchase cash plan can absorb it.

Does an old mortgage excluded from qualifying mean I am released from it?

No. A lender's qualifying treatment and a creditor's release are different questions. Confirm the actual obligations with qualified counsel and the loan records.

Can I use an expected divorce settlement for the STR down payment?

Do not rely on an uncompleted transfer. Verify legal availability, receipt, lender acceptance and the full purchase-and-launch budget before setting a funded offer.

Does a DSCR loan eliminate all post-divorce purchase concerns?

No. The actual product has its own conditions, and borrower obligations, available cash, title and property underwriting still need review.

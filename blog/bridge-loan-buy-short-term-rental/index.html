Should you use a bridge loan to buy a short-term rental?

Published September 25, 2026·6 min read

A bridge loan can get a short-term-rental buyer to closing before permanent financing is available, but it does not make a weak property a good acquisition. The purchase only works if the loan's maturity, carrying cost and payoff path survive a slower launch, lower appraisal or refinance denial. Before you offer, compare a bridge-funded purchase with waiting for a permanent loan and with passing on the property. Get property-specific terms from the bridge lender and a separate written view of the intended exit lender's requirements. If the plan is simply 'refinance once Airbnb bookings arrive,' do not take on a hard payoff date yet.

Decide what the bridge loan actually solves

Name the constraint: a seller's closing deadline, a property that needs documented work before a permanent lender will finance it, or a buyer who has cash tied up until another transaction closes. Ask whether the existing purchase loan can close with a seller cure, later closing, or a different permanent program. The property-loan mismatch guide covers that first diagnosis. A bridge is worth comparing only when it solves a real timing or property-eligibility obstacle at a price the deal can carry.

Do not confuse a bridge loan secured by the STR with an all-cash purchase followed by delayed financing. Those have different liens, closing documents and refinance rules; see the cash-first financing guide. Ask the bridge lender for a term sheet naming the borrower and property, purchase and repair draws, rate, points, fees, reserve holdbacks, extension rights, prepayment terms, maturity and payoff calculation. A quoted monthly payment without the full payoff is not a funding plan.

Underwrite cash through the first legal guest and the payoff date

Build a dated cash schedule: earnest money, closing cash, lender points and fees, repair deposits, furnishing, permits, insurance, taxes, utilities, debt payments, reserves, extension charges and refinance closing costs. Revenue begins only after the exact address is legally permitted, insured and actually launch-ready. Compare a base case with a slower opening and weaker bookings; use the cash-through-launch worksheet. A purchase that survives only if peak-season STR income arrives immediately has no debt cushion.

Illustrative only: suppose an STR buyer borrows $360,000 on a twelve-month bridge, pays $9,000 in upfront loan costs, and expects $3,000 monthly interest and carrying costs before guest revenue. A four-month launch delay uses another $12,000, for $21,000 beyond the down payment, repairs and furnishing. These are hypothetical inputs, not a loan quote, current rate or market forecast. Add the actual lender's extension and payoff terms; do not assume the loan can simply roll over.

The Consumer Financial Protection Bureau's balloon-payment explanation is a plain-language warning that a low periodic payment can leave a large amount due at maturity. It is not a statement that a particular business-purpose bridge loan falls under consumer mortgage rules. Have the lender and counsel explain the actual note and default consequences for your transaction.

Prove the refinance exit before relying on it

Ask the intended permanent lender which loan product would repay the bridge, whether the buyer and property qualify, what appraisal or value basis it uses, whether STR income counts, how much seasoning or operating history is required, and the maximum proceeds after fees. Get the timing in writing. Do not treat an online DSCR estimate or a lender's informal confidence as a commitment. If you need a conventional cash-out refinance, Fannie Mae's current cash-out refinance guide includes existing-loan and ownership timing requirements that can matter; your lender must determine which transaction and rules actually apply. Other products have different requirements.

Run the refinance proceeds under a lower appraisal and a lender's more conservative treatment of STR income. The property might be profitable to operate yet fail the lender's proceeds test. The stabilization refinance guide addresses later underwriting; for this purchase, the key is whether the bridge payoff survives even if that later loan is smaller or slower than hoped.

Set an offer ceiling and a hard walk-away rule

Compare three versions of the same property: permanent financing at purchase, bridge then refinance, and no purchase. Include all points, interest, extensions, repair and launch costs in the bridge version. The ability to offer faster may improve your negotiating position, but any price premium has to be less than the added debt and risk. Have the acquisition team and lender agree on a maximum offer from conservative forward earnings, not the seller's historic gross or a hoped-for after-repair value.

Proceed if the property is legally viable, bridge terms are documented, total cash remains adequate and a conservative refinance or independent payoff clears before maturity. Renegotiate if added points, carry or a smaller expected takeout reduce the price you can defend. Delay under contract protection if a property-specific exit quote, insurance binder or permit answer is pending. Reject when the only exit is an uncommitted refinance, the loan matures before a realistic launch and seasoning period, or an extension would exhaust reserves. Do not waive financing protection merely because a bridge lender says it can fund quickly; have local counsel review the contract.

Write a one-page purchase memo with the bridge term sheet, legal first-guest date, downside cash burn, takeout lender requirements, conservative net refinance proceeds, backup payoff and maximum offer. Frequently asked questions

Can I buy an Airbnb with a bridge loan and refinance later?

Possibly, but first obtain property-specific bridge terms and a separate lender assessment of refinance eligibility, timing and net proceeds. Model a delayed or denied refinance before committing to the purchase.

Does projected Airbnb income guarantee a bridge-loan payoff?

No. A takeout lender may use different income documentation, valuation and seasoning rules. Projected bookings are not payoff cash.

What if the STR is not ready before the bridge loan matures?

Check extension rights and costs, carry reserves, and an independent payoff source. If the debt cannot be retired without a favorable refinance, the purchase may be too risky.

Is bridge financing the same as buying cash and refinancing?

No. A bridge loan is debt at purchase with its own lien and maturity. An all-cash purchase followed by financing can face a different transaction classification and lender requirements.

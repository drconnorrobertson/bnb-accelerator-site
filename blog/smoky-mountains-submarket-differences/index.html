Sevierville, Pigeon Forge and Gatlinburg Are Three Markets, Not One

Published January 26, 2023·6 min read

We have transacted more properties in the Smoky Mountains corridor than anywhere else, and the most common mistake buyers make is treating Sevierville, Pigeon Forge and Gatlinburg as one market with one set of comparables. Two cabins with identical specifications ten minutes apart routinely sit in different pricing tiers.

Three different products

Gatlinburg sits closest to the Great Smoky Mountains National Park entrance and has the most walkable downtown. Its inventory skews smaller and more urban, with condominium and chalet product alongside cabins, and the guest values being able to walk to restaurants and the Parkway.

Pigeon Forge is the entertainment core, built around Dollywood, the Island, dinner shows and the attraction corridor. The guest is frequently a family whose trip is organized around attractions rather than the park, and proximity to Dollywood specifically is a checkable rate driver.

Sevierville spreads wider with more land, larger cabins and a lower price per square foot. Most of our large-cabin transactions happen here, including Victoria's nine-bedroom at $1,375,000 and Vishal's five-bedroom at $1,250,000, because the acreage that a nine-bedroom lodge requires exists here and does not in downtown Gatlinburg.

What actually moves the rate

Drive time to the Parkway is the first variable. A cabin fifteen minutes from the corridor and a cabin thirty-five minutes from it are serving different guests, and the more remote cabin has to compensate with view, privacy or amenities.

View is the second and it cannot be added. A genuine mountain view from the deck and hot tub is worth substantially more than a wooded view, and listing photography is shot from whatever angle produces the best result. Verify from the actual deck.

Amenity fit is the third. Hot tub, view and game room are what the comparable set has. A cabin missing any one of them is priced against properties that have all three, and the gap has to be funded at purchase rather than deferred.

Bedroom count and which calendar you buy

A four-bedroom serves two families, a small friend group or a couple with in-laws, and that demand exists every week of the season. Alfredo and Millie bought at $865,000 and Adam at $775,000, both four-bedroom, both into the deepest and most consistent demand pool in the market.

A nine-bedroom competes for reunions, corporate retreats and weddings. Victoria's produced roughly $20,000 of cash flow in June from about $38,000 in bookings against $18,000 in expenses. That is an enormous month and the calendar behind it is narrower, because there are only so many twenty-six-person groups.

For a first purchase we generally steer toward four and five bedrooms. The steadiness is worth more than the peak, and both acquisition cost and turnover complexity are dramatically lower.

The seasonality trap

Smokies revenue concentrates June through October with a winter holiday spike. January through March is thin. A model built on the annual shape survives that; a model built by multiplying a June figure by twelve does not.

This is the single most common underwriting error in the market, and it usually originates in the seller's proforma. Listing packets here are routinely assembled from peak-season rates extrapolated across the year, producing a revenue figure the property will never reach.

Underwrite against actual booked-night data for genuinely comparable inventory in the same submarket, across a full twelve months. Not the seller's spreadsheet, and not a peak quarter annualized.

Why the corridor still works

Great Smoky Mountains National Park receives more annual visitors than any other US national park, roughly double the second place park. That demand base is deep enough to absorb supply that would flood a smaller market, which is why the corridor has continued to perform through several waves of inventory growth.

Tennessee also levies no state income tax on wages, which removes a layer of state-level complexity for high-earning buyers. It does not change federal treatment of the property, but it simplifies the picture relative to a California or New York purchase.

The 2018 Tennessee Short Term Rental Unit Act generally prevents local governments from prohibiting units that were lawfully operating before a local ordinance took effect, which is a meaningful protection for existing operators.

What we look for in the corridor

After transacting more properties here than anywhere else, the filters have narrowed to a consistent set.

Basis below the comparable set for the same amenity tier, because that advantage is permanent and never has to be re-earned. Genuine view verified from the deck rather than from listing photography. Hot tub, game room and view all present, or the gap priced and funded at purchase. Drive time to the Parkway inside a range the comparable set supports. Road access that works in February, since steep gravel switchbacks generate cancellations and refund requests.

And a twelve-month revenue model built from actual booked nights for genuinely comparable inventory, stress tested at 75%. A cabin that clears all of those is rare, which is why we screen roughly a thousand deals a week across all markets and eliminate about 98% of them. The corridor produces more of our transactions than anywhere else not because the filters are looser here but because the inventory is deep enough that something always clears them.

Frequently asked questions

Are Sevierville, Pigeon Forge and Gatlinburg the same market?

No. Gatlinburg is closest to the park entrance with a walkable downtown and smaller inventory. Pigeon Forge is the entertainment core around Dollywood. Sevierville has more land, larger cabins and a lower price per square foot. Drive time between them moves rates materially.

What amenities are required in a Smokies cabin?

Hot tub, genuine mountain view and game room are what the comparable set has. A cabin missing any one is priced against properties that have all three, so the gap should be funded at purchase rather than deferred.

Should You Sell an STR Furnished or Empty?

Published September 23, 2026·Updated October 7, 2026·6 min read

When selling an STR furnished or empty, compare the actual buyer's use plan, documented inventory, transaction terms and both sides' cash. For the investor preparing to buy, a higher furnished price may still require less opening cash—but only when the included items are usable, truly transfer and replace costs the buyer would otherwise incur.

Neither original retail receipts nor attractive listing photos establish a premium, financing treatment or an earlier opening. Request evidence and compare written alternatives before committing. This is a transaction-choice worksheet, not an appraisal or a reason to add the seller's historical furnishing spend to the real-estate price.

Define furnished and empty alternatives precisely

Put both proposed offers beside the same address. For a furnished option, identify the item schedule, accepted condition, exclusions, ownership questions, possession date and remaining buyer work. For an empty option, specify what the seller removes, what remains as part of the property, the agreed condition after removal and what the buyer must install. Do not use “everything stays” as the entire agreement.

Worked seller comparison: the package label is not net proceeds

Hypothetical only: no real offers, current market prices, provider fees or client outcomes are represented. Assume a $510,000 furnished offer and a $500,000 empty offer. Stipulate $30,000 total seller sale expenses and a $330,000 loan payoff in each case. The empty option also requires a distinct $4,000 removal bill, while the furnished option requires no additional seller work in this simplified case.

Furnished cash before seller income taxes and unmodeled obligations is $510,000−$30,000−$330,000=$150,000. Empty cash is $500,000−$30,000−$330,000−$4,000=$136,000. The difference is $14,000: $10,000 price plus $4,000 avoided removal. If the furnished documents describe an $18,000 inventory allocation already within the $510,000 price, it is not another $18,000 receipt. The example does not authenticate that allocation.

Replace the stipulated expenses with actual quotes, payoff statements, taxes, contract obligations and dates. This net-cash test does not establish taxable gain, the appropriate asking price or the probability that either offer closes. A different buyer or additional seller preparation can change the result. A seller's old $100,000 furnishing receipt total cannot answer those questions by itself.

Worked buyer comparison: a higher price can require less opening cash

Use the same fictional alternatives, but assume separately reviewed lender proposals require $150,000 buyer price cash for furnished and $147,000 for empty. These are stipulated cash requirements, not standard down-payment rules, accepted appraisal values or proof that furniture is financeable. Buyer closing/finance outflows are $15,000 in each case. Complete setup is $8,000 for the furnished option versus $32,000 for empty. Both plans retain a protected $40,000 reserve.

Now assume the final inventory review finds missing or unsuitable included items requiring a distinct $7,000 replacement cost, not already inside the $8,000 setup. Furnished allocation rises to $220,000 and margin falls to $5,000. A separate $3,000 net carrying-cost delay raises it to $223,000 with $2,000 remaining. Do not assume a later seller reimbursement pays an earlier vendor invoice. Another $5,000 distinct bill would create a $3,000 funding gap if the reserve remains protected.

Price, allocation and permission are separate reviews

IRS Publication 551 (December 2025) explains allocating a lump-sum acquisition among assets received. Have the CPA determine treatment from the actual transaction; a replacement budget is not established tax basis. The asset-cost reconciliation guide handles source records and duplicate costs separately.

Have the lender and closing professionals review any personal-property terms or proposed credit before relying on the financing plan. Do not invent an appraisal exclusion, a financing allowance or permission for an undisclosed side payment. The furnished appraisal-gap worksheet addresses a different question: cash needed when actual accepted collateral or funding differs.

Included beds, amenities or a prior listing do not establish lawful occupancy or STR permission. Verify address-level use, insurance, possession and opening tasks. A furniture package does not transfer a seller's platform account, reviews, reservations or other rights merely because those appear in the marketing; investigate each separately with authorized records and appropriate advisers.

Choose an evidence-supported transaction, not a turnkey adjective

Proceed when both the accepted package and remaining work fit verified financing, cash and permissions. Negotiate documented exceptions through the actual contract and qualified professionals; do not assume a right to new retail replacements, cancellation or a fee waiver. Compare an empty purchase when the included package does not fit, and decline a purchase that depends on unverified assets or unfunded opening costs.

Frequently asked questions

Does the seller’s original furniture spend establish the package price?

No. Compare documented usable inventory, accepted condition, ownership and the actual buyer replacement scope. Original retail spending does not establish today’s transaction premium or tax allocation.

Can a higher furnished price require less buyer cash?

It can under particular written financing and setup assumptions. The hypothetical example requires $213,000 allocated capital furnished versus $234,000 empty; replace those inputs with actual terms rather than assuming a universal result.

Is an inventory allocation extra sale cash?

Not when it is already included in the agreed package price. The example’s proposed $18,000 allocation is not another receipt and is not authenticated tax basis.

What if included items are missing at handoff?

Document the exception and replacement cash, then ask the transaction professionals what the actual contract permits. Do not assume reimbursement, cancellation rights or new retail replacements; check payment dates and preserve your reserve.

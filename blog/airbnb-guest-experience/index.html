Airbnb Guest Experience Details That Matter

Published September 21, 2023·6 min read

Guest experience is where small amounts of money produce disproportionate returns, because the things guests remember are almost never the expensive things. A $40 decision made well beats a $4,000 decision made carelessly.

The arrival is most of the review

Guests form their opinion in the first fifteen minutes and spend the rest of the stay confirming it. That makes arrival the highest leverage moment in the entire experience.

  • Lock codes tested before every arrival, because a code that fails at 11pm produces a review that nothing later fixes.
  • Clear directions including the thing people get wrong, whether that is an unmarked turn, a gate code, or a driveway that looks like a private road.
  • Lights on and temperature set before arrival, which costs nothing and is noticed every time.
  • A visible welcome note with wifi details and the two things they need to know immediately.

The cheap details that appear in reviews

  1. Blackout curtains in every bedroom. The most frequently praised inexpensive item in short-term rentals.
  2. Genuinely good coffee with a simple method, plus enough for the first two mornings.
  3. Extra phone chargers, the most commonly forgotten guest item.
  4. A local guide written by you, not a generic list. Three restaurants you actually like, the grocery store that is worth the extra five minutes, and the trail that is not in the guidebook.
  5. Good water pressure and a good mattress, which are not cheap but outrank every decorative decision in review text.
  6. A small welcome item matched to the market, such as local coffee or a bottle of something regional.

Experience details are cheap and compound

Review velocity in the first ninety days sets a ranking trajectory that is expensive to fix later. We build the launch around that.

Communication that reads as hospitality

Automate the schedule and personalize the content. The sequence that works: a booking confirmation asking one question about the trip, directions three days out, a morning of arrival message with the code, a short check the morning after arrival, and checkout instructions the day before.

The morning after arrival message is the highest value one, because it surfaces problems while they are still fixable rather than at review time. See how to get five star reviews.

Building toward repeat guests

The highest value guest is one who returns, because they cost nothing to acquire, they treat the property better, and they book directly if you have made that possible.

Practically: remember returning guests and say so, note preferences from the previous stay, and send one well timed message a year ahead of the season they usually visit. That single habit is what converts a good property into a business with owned demand. See direct booking.

What not to spend on

Elaborate welcome baskets that go uneaten, luxury toiletries guests do not notice, premium appliance brands, and decorative upgrades in spaces that never appear in photographs. Every one of those is a cost that does not show up in a review. See amenities that increase revenue.

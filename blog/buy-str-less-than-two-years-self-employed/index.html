Can you buy an STR with less than two years of self-employment?

Published October 6, 2026·6 min read

Do not assume you must always wait two years, or that strong recent business deposits automatically qualify you today. Ask the lender whether it can accept your documented earnings history for an investment-STR purchase, what income it will actually use, and which evidence must exist before closing. Choose properties from that confirmed file. Book a call to match an STR search to your verified self-employed financing and opening cash.

Separate business age, income evidence and the purchase product

Three questions determine the next step: how long the business has operated, what periods your documents substantiate, and which loan can accept that file for investment STR use. A new company registration is not proof of stable earnings. A profitable month is not a complete lender income calculation. A primary-home approval example is not an investment-property commitment. Present your actual ownership, work history and intended rental use to the lender before searching at a price inferred from gross receipts.

Fannie Mae's self-employed borrower guidance generally uses two years of prior earnings. It permits consideration of shorter self-employment history when the most recent signed personal and business federal returns show a full twelve months from the current business, with documented prior income at the same or greater level in related work or responsibilities. Experience and business debt also require review. This is a conditional path, not automatic approval with one return. Ask the lender whether it applies to your complete file and product.

The same source separately describes reduced return documentation for established businesses with specified five-year history and ownership conditions. Do not confuse that established-business provision with permission for a newly formed company. Tell the lender about each income source and ownership interest; let it classify and document the actual file rather than changing labels to fit an online exception.

Check the tax-return period rather than counting months on a calendar

Illustrative calendar-year businesses, not tax or eligibility advice: Buyer A started operating July 1, 2025. In October 2026 the business is about fifteen months old, but its 2025 calendar-year return covers only six months of this business's income. A rolling twelve-month profit-and-loss report does not itself make that return a full twelve-month return. Ask the lender whether an actual available path accepts the documents now and what later evidence would change its answer.

Buyer B started January 1, 2025 and has a signed filed return covering the full 2025 calendar year. In October 2026 the business has less than two years of history, yet the full-year return may satisfy one part of the referenced shorter-history test. Buyer B still needs lender acceptance of prior related earnings, current stability, obligations and the investment purchase. Neither buyer should interpret these dates as an approval letter.

Discuss truthful filing timing and accurate records with your CPA, and loan-document timing with your lender. Do not fabricate a full year, backdate business activity or omit expenses to accelerate eligibility. If an extension or pending filing affects document availability, ask for the actual lender treatment before promising the seller a closing date. Use lender-accepted earnings to set the STR price range

Wells Fargo's current self-employment explanation describes review of business net income and expenses, stability and supporting records, and notes that lender requirements differ. It is general mortgage guidance, not a Wells Fargo STR-loan offer. Ask your originating lender for the specific investment-file document list and calculated income rather than treating receipts as spendable personal earnings.

Illustrative arithmetic only: business receipts average $24,000 monthly and business expenses average $15,000, leaving $9,000 before the lender's particular adjustments and review. Suppose a proposed STR housing payment plus other qualifying obligations totals $5,000 monthly. Dividing by gross receipts gives 20.8%; dividing by the illustrative $9,000 gives 55.6%. Neither ratio proves eligibility. The contrast shows why using $24,000 as accepted income can create a misleading search ceiling. Replace the illustrative $9,000 with the lender's actual qualifying figure.

Keep acquisition assets separate from that income calculation. Money needed for payroll or committed company expenses cannot simultaneously be treated as unrestricted furnishing cash. Resolve both questions if company cash is part of your funding plan.

Investigate another path without replacing one missing approval with another

If the selected income-qualified loan cannot accept the history now, ask whether another actually available product fits the investment. A bank-statement quote still needs its own history, deposit and expense rules reviewed; it is not proof that business-age conditions disappear. A property-income loan shifts the analysis toward accepted rent but still has borrower, property, credit, cash and other conditions. Obtain written terms before treating either as your backup.

Use the bank-statement purchase framework for evaluating that product, or the conventional-versus-DSCR comparison for another channel. This decision starts earlier: which documented earnings file exists now, and which lender will actually accept it? Keep alternatives tied to the same address, truthful occupancy, full payment and conservative revenue evidence. Seller projections cannot replace the lender's required documents.

Before changing products, recalculate required cash, fees, reserves and any prepayment costs, then test the property without assuming a near-term refinance. Compare delaying the purchase, choosing a lower-price candidate or proceeding through a genuinely accepted product. Do not compensate for a missing income history by exhausting opening liquidity or assuming the business will grow fast enough to subsidize disappointing bookings.

Make the offer only after the document and cash dependencies are resolved

Proceed when the lender has accepted the actual history and income treatment, remaining conditions can be met within protected contract dates, and independent legal-use, property and downside diligence support the acquisition. Renegotiate price or closing timing when verified financing or document dates change the viable plan. Delay while required evidence is unavailable or the lender's calculation is unresolved. Reject an offer structure that depends on false business history, hidden expenses or a hoped-for financing replacement.

Your practical next step is a lender-confirmed income and document checklist, a realistic purchase timetable and an all-in cash budget. Ask the acquisition team to screen from those parameters rather than from your best month of invoices. Book a call to compare STR candidates that fit your current self-employed purchase file. Share sensitive records only with the professionals who need them through secure channels; do not provide bank credentials or client-private data unnecessarily.

Frequently asked questions

Must I always be self-employed two years before buying an STR?

Not necessarily. Ask whether an available investment-STR product accepts your documented shorter history and what conditions apply. Do not assume recent profit alone proves eligibility.

Is fifteen months in business the same as a full-year tax return?

No. A return can cover only a partial year of business activity even when the business is now older. Have the lender check the actual covered period and required records.

Can I use gross company receipts as my qualifying income?

Do not assume so. Ask the lender to calculate the usable income after its expense, stability and documentation review, and set the property search ceiling from that result.

Will a bank-statement or DSCR loan automatically solve short business history?

No. Each actual product has its own conditions. Obtain lender confirmation, compare full cost and cash requirements, and independently underwrite the property.

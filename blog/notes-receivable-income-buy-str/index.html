Can payments from a prior sale note qualify you to buy an STR?

Published October 6, 2026·7 min read

Potentially, but a promised payment stream is not automatically accepted income, and the unpaid note balance is not automatically cash for closing. If you receive payments from a prior property or business sale and plan to buy an STR in the next six months, ask the actual lender to review both receipt history and the remaining payment term. Waiting can improve one clock while exhausting the other. Book a call to compare STR candidates against your lender-reviewed income window and separately verified purchase cash.

Identify which side of the note you are on

This guide concerns the buyer who receives payments on an existing note from a prior sale and wants a mortgage for a different STR purchase. You are the payee of that existing obligation. It does not explain negotiating seller financing for the new STR, where you would owe payments to its seller. That opposite decision is covered in buying an STR with seller financing.

Before expanding the shortlist, get the actual lender's view of the proposed investment loan, the payment stream and the property's intended rental use. A qualifying-income scenario, a legally enforceable payment right and funds available for a closing deadline answer different questions. Do not combine them into a single reassuring balance.

Use three separate lines in your purchase file: accepted recurring income, cash actually received and available, and unpaid contractual principal. A $300,000 illustrative unpaid balance is not a $300,000 bank deposit. Do not enter it as purchase cash merely because it appears on a note or personal balance sheet.

Get the lender's accepted evidence and two dates

Fannie Mae's current notes-receivable guidance calls for the executed note showing amount and duration, plus evidence of full, regular, timely receipt during the most recent twelve months. A minimum twelve-month receipt history is required; notes executed within the past twelve months are not stable income under that guidance regardless of duration. Payments must be expected to continue for at least three years from the new mortgage note date. Have the actual lender apply the current rules to your file rather than assume a promised amount or long maturity establishes eligibility.

Ask for a written answer identifying the accepted payment amount, the earliest date the history can satisfy the chosen product and the latest proposed mortgage-note date supported by remaining payments. Have qualified counsel explain the actual contractual end conditions. Do not substitute an application date for the relevant new-note date or round a near-boundary closing in your favor.

Not every product follows this agency guidance. If considering another financing path, obtain its actual accepted terms rather than assume a different loan label bypasses documentation. The conventional-versus-DSCR comparison helps frame that choice, but the actual lender decides eligibility, accepted income, property use and cash requirements.

Calculate whether an eligible timing window can exist

Illustrative timing screen only, not a lender approval formula: assume an originally executed note provides equal monthly payments for 54 months, with twelve full months received on time and no modification or early payoff. At elapsed month 12, 42 payment months remain. At elapsed month 18, 36 remain. At elapsed month 20, only 34 remain. Under the history and continuation conditions described above, the constructed overlap is months 12 through 18, subject to the actual dates and all other requirements.

Now use an illustrative original term of 42 months. When the twelve-month history first exists, only 30 payment months remain. Before then, receipt history is too short; afterward, remaining duration only falls. Waiting alone cannot create the missing overlap. This is why 'come back after more payments' is not an adequate STR acquisition plan unless the lender also checks the remaining term.

That arithmetic follows the stated conditions; it does not account for every contract, underwriting exception or actual day-level deadline. A closing delay can matter even when the payment amount never changes.

Do not try to cure a missing window through an informal promise to extend the note. Any real amendment needs appropriate legal review and fresh lender assessment of its evidence and payment history. A newly signed document is not assurance of accepted income. Compare buying within a verified window, qualifying without this stream, choosing an actually available alternative loan, or delaying the investment for a different reason.

Keep accepted note income separate from STR closing and launch cash

Illustrative acquisition cash model, not lender terms or a property recommendation: assume $180,000 of verified liquid cash, a $500,000 STR price, a chosen 25% equity assumption of $125,000, $15,000 of closing expenses, $30,000 of setup and $30,000 the buyer intends to retain. Total cash allocation is $200,000, leaving a $20,000 shortfall. An illustrative accepted monthly note payment of $3,200 does not erase a cash deficit due at closing.

A lower price may improve the cash requirement, but calculate the complete change. At $460,000 with the same hypothetical equity fraction and unchanged other allocations, equity is $115,000 and the total is $190,000. The deficit is still $10,000. Do not call the revised candidate funded merely because the price came down. Actual loan terms, closing expenses and launch scope need their own quotes and review.

If the existing note pays off early, separate what actually arrives as usable cash from recurring income that no longer exists. Ask the lender to reassess the changed financing case before removing protections or committing funds. Do not count both an unchanged payment stream and the full payoff as though each remains available indefinitely.

Use the first STR purchase budget planner with actual cash timing. Separately test address-specific legal hosting, supported revenue and an opening delay in the STR downside scenario. Income acceptance does not make an overvalued or legally unusable property a good acquisition.

Make the offer decision with a dated financing memo

Proceed when the actual lender confirms an executable scenario for the proposed note date, available cash covers the full acquisition and launch, and the property clears the buyer's independent due diligence. Renegotiate price or choose another candidate when a documented cash reduction genuinely closes the gap. Do not assume a seller credit changes the history or remaining duration of a separate existing note.

Delay if receipt evidence, contract interpretation or lender acceptance is unresolved. Reject a plan that treats waiting as a cure for an empty timing window, counts unpaid principal as liquid cash, or relies on both a payoff and continuing income without review. Have the agent and qualified counsel address realistic financing protection and settlement timing before contractual deadlines become binding.

Your next step is a dated file with the accepted payment amount, relevant timing window, proposed STR closing date and complete cash allocation. If the accepted scenario changes, revisit the shortlist and offer ceiling. The objective is a purchase the buyer can actually close and launch, not the largest price that fits an incomplete worksheet.

Frequently asked questions

Is this the same as using seller financing to buy the STR?

No. Here you receive payments on an existing note from a prior sale. With seller financing for the new purchase, you owe its seller payments.

Does more payment history always help an STR loan application?

Not necessarily. Remaining payment duration falls as history grows. Have the actual lender review both conditions and the proposed mortgage-note date.

Can I enter the unpaid note balance as STR closing cash?

Do not assume it is liquid cash. Document funds actually available by the relevant deadlines and ask the lender how it treats the asset.

What if the note pays off before the STR closing?

Ask for fresh review of the resulting cash and changed income case. Do not assume both the original recurring stream and full payoff remain available.

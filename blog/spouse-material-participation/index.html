Spousal Hours and Material Participation

Published August 25, 2025·6 min read

A surgeon working sixty hours a week cannot realistically log 100 hours on a rental property. Their spouse frequently can. For married couples, that fact often decides whether the entire strategy is available, and it is one of the most useful and least discussed provisions in this area.

My BnB Accelerator, LLC is a real estate acquisition firm, not a CPA firm. This is a plain English explanation so you can have an informed conversation with a qualified professional, not tax advice. Our tax partner is AE Tax Advisors, an independent firm.

The rule in plain terms

For purposes of the material participation tests, participation by a taxpayer's spouse is generally taken into account, whether or not the spouse has an ownership interest and whether or not the couple files a joint return.

The practical translation for a two income household where one partner has more available time: the hours that count are the household's hours, not just the higher earner's. This is what makes the short-term rental strategy accessible to physicians, attorneys, and executives whose own calendars make participation impossible.

What this looks like in practice

A common arrangement among our clients: one spouse handles the property as the operating role. Guest communication, pricing and calendar management, vendor coordination, purchasing, and bookkeeping. The other spouse contributes occasional physical work and decisions.

The operating spouse's hours are documented the same way anyone else's would be, with dates, specific descriptions, and corroborating evidence such as platform message timestamps, vendor communications, and receipts. See how to keep a defensible log.

The competing participation point still applies

Under the 100 hour test, no other individual may participate more than the taxpayer does. That includes a property manager or a cleaner, which is why a full service management arrangement can defeat the test even when a spouse is doing meaningful work. The management structure decision comes first. See co-hosting versus self managing.

Household structure is part of the plan

The participation question frequently has a household answer rather than an individual one. We raise it during acquisition.

The distinction from real estate professional status

Real estate professional status works differently. Its requirements, including more than 750 hours and more than half of personal services in real property trades or businesses, are generally applied to an individual rather than aggregated across a couple. That is a meaningful difference and it is a common source of confusion.

So a household can find that the short-term rental path is available through a spouse's participation while real estate professional status is not available to either person. See the comparison of both routes.

Documenting a household arrangement

  1. Log by person. Record whose hours they are, not just how many.
  2. Keep the evidence in that person's name where it naturally occurs: their platform account activity, their email and text threads with vendors, their receipts.
  3. Be realistic about the split. A log showing the busy spouse contributing eighty hours a year is fine. One showing them contributing an implausible amount is not.
  4. Track vendor and manager hours too, since they are part of the competing participation analysis.

The specifics of how these rules apply depend entirely on your facts, including filing status and the structure through which the property is held. Frequently asked questions

Do my spouse's hours count toward material participation?

For purposes of the material participation tests, participation by a taxpayer's spouse is generally taken into account, whether or not the spouse holds an ownership interest and whether or not a joint return is filed. For a household where one partner has more available time, that is frequently what makes the strategy workable.

Can one spouse run the property while the other qualifies?

A common arrangement is for one spouse to take the operating role covering guest communication, pricing, vendor coordination, purchasing, and bookkeeping, with the other contributing occasional work. The hours are documented per person with corroborating evidence in that person's name.

Does spousal participation help with real estate professional status?

Generally no, and this is a common source of confusion. Real estate professional status requirements, including more than 750 hours and more than half of personal services in real property trades, are applied to an individual rather than aggregated across a couple.

Does a property manager still affect the test if my spouse participates?

Yes. The 100 hour test requires that no other individual participate more than the taxpayer does, and a manager's or cleaner's hours count regardless of who in the household is doing the owner side work. The management structure decision comes before the participation plan.

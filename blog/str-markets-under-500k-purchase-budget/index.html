Which STR markets fit a $500K purchase budget?

Published September 25, 2026·6 min read

If you plan to buy a short-term rental for less than $500,000, first calculate the price your cash and lender actually support after closing costs, launch spending and reserves. Then screen markets for properties at that lower ceiling—not merely cities with a sub-$500K median home price. Branson, Missouri, and the Kissimmee, Florida, area are two places to investigate because their recent all-home sale medians fall below $500K, but neither median proves an STR-eligible property can be bought, financed and operated profitably. Verify a specific address, legal use, offer price and downside cash flow before choosing either market. Book a call to build your STR purchase shortlist around your actual buying power.

Calculate your real acquisition ceiling before searching markets

A $500K search filter is not a $500K acquisition budget. Start with liquid funds available for this purchase. Set aside the reserves required by your lender and your own downside plan, quoted furnishing and launch costs, due diligence, closing costs, and an insurance and tax cushion. Only the remainder can fund the down payment. Ask a lender to confirm down payment, eligible property types, debt-service tests and reserve requirements for the precise intended STR use; a generic preapproval may not cover a condominium or a non-owner-occupied vacation rental. The cash-to-close guide details the full uses of cash, while this page converts that answer into a market-search ceiling.

Illustrative math, not a loan quote: a buyer has $150,000 liquid, reserves $25,000, budgets $20,000 for furnishing and launch, and allows $15,000 for closing and diligence. That leaves $90,000 for a down payment. At an assumed 25% down, the price ceiling is $360,000 ($90,000 / 0.25). A $500,000 purchase at the same assumed down payment would need $125,000 down plus the other $60,000, or $185,000 total—$35,000 more than this buyer has. If the actual lender requires a different percentage or reserves, recompute rather than treating these illustrative numbers as a market rule.

Use market medians only to choose where to investigate

Redfin reports an August 2026 all-home median sale price of $249,735 in Branson. Its comparable Kissimmee series reports $349,769. Both figures cover all home types and closed sales, not current asking prices for STR-permitted homes. Neither measures furnishings, permit eligibility, insurance, buyer cash flow or obtainable financing. They are screening signals, not investment recommendations or claims of available inventory.

Kissimmee's median is closer to that hypothetical ceiling, leaving less room for a higher-priced STR-eligible property or an unexpected launch bill. Verify the jurisdiction and parcel before valuing an STR

An address labeled Branson can lie outside Branson city limits. The City of Branson's short-term-rental page directs buyers to check boundaries and explains its permit, business-license and inspection process for in-city properties. Use the city's nightly-rental map as a first screen, then obtain confirmation from planning staff for the parcel and planned use. Check deed restrictions and HOA documents independently; a city permit process does not override private restrictions.

A Kissimmee mailing address likewise does not establish the governing jurisdiction. For an unincorporated Osceola County parcel, the county STRPD guidance calls for a zoning/overlay check and points to state vacation-rental licensing. If the parcel is within Kissimmee city limits, confirm city requirements with the city instead. Do not apply county permission to a city parcel—or assume the seller's current listing, permit or HOA approval transfers with ownership. The pre-purchase legal-use checklist shows the evidence to request before removing a contingency.

Turn a market screen into an offer decision

For each candidate under the true price ceiling, record the asking price and all-in cash required; the parcel's written zoning and HOA position; lender approval for that property; independently supported STR revenue comps; and actual management, insurance, tax, utility and maintenance quotes. Model a weaker booking period, a slower launch and a repair reserve in the downside case. A cheap purchase price is not a bargain if legal use fails or debt service depends on unsupported revenue. The property-search guide helps turn this buy box into candidate addresses.

Proceed when the specific property clears legal use, lender, cash-to-close and downside-return gates at the proposed price. Renegotiate if verified repairs, taxes, insurance or supported revenue reduce the maximum offer; use the revised model, not the market median, to set that price. Delay only with valid contract protection when a defined permit, HOA or financing answer is pending. Reject a property that cannot lawfully operate as planned, cannot be financed within your cash ceiling, or only works with optimistic occupancy. Keep searching if neither market produces a qualifying deal.

Frequently asked questions

Is $500,000 enough to buy a short-term rental?

It may be, but price alone is insufficient. Ask a lender for property-specific terms and leave cash for closing, launch, reserves and operating risk before setting a price ceiling.

Does a market median below $500,000 mean STR deals exist there?

No. An all-home median includes properties that may be ineligible, unsuitable or unavailable at your ceiling. Verify actual listings, parcel rules, financing and underwriting.

Should I pick Branson or Kissimmee for a sub-$500K STR purchase?

Neither automatically. Use the cited home-price medians to decide where to investigate, then compare address-level legal rights, all-in cash, lender terms and conservative property economics.

What if my lender-approved price is lower than $500,000?

Use the lower financed and cash-supported ceiling. Renegotiate a candidate only if its supported economics justify an offer within that ceiling; otherwise search another property or market.

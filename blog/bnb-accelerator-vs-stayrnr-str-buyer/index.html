BNB Accelerator vs StayRnR: which fits your STR purchase and renovation?

Published October 6, 2026·7 min read

If you intend to buy an STR that needs renovation, compare who is responsible for both selecting the property and delivering the construction scope. StayRnR describes sourcing, in-house renovation, furnishing and ongoing operation; BNB Accelerator describes acquisition coordination and local design, furnishing and manager pairings. The useful comparison is the proposed build, cash exposure and decision rights on a real property—not which provider sounds more hands-off. Book a call to test BNB Accelerator's acquisition scope against your STR purchase and renovation plan.

Choose the delivery model before choosing the house

StayRnR's investment page describes a three-phase sourcing, renovation and operation model. It says construction runs in-house, establishment pricing is project-specific and the buyer keeps ownership. It lists investment placement where it already operates: Broward/Miami, the NYC-adjacent New Jersey corridor and eastern Pennsylvania. Confirm the specific address and available capacity; this is not approval of legal STR use in those regions. The page does not provide a universal establishment-fee amount.

BNB Accelerator's published acquisition process describes sourcing, underwriting, negotiation and closing coordination, followed by local design, furnishing and management pairings. Its FAQ says a buyer can bring their own manager. Do not infer an in-house general-contractor obligation, a fixed renovation price or guaranteed completion from that coordination description. Ask BNB for the actual project scope, vendor contracts, fees and duties applicable to your candidate.

StayRnR may fit a buyer who wants to investigate an integrated renovation-and-operator team in its placement markets. BNB may fit a buyer seeking acquisition coordination with a separately specified local delivery team. These are conditional fit statements, not claims about construction quality or returns. An integrated model can reduce handoffs; it still needs independently reviewed plans, pricing and legal permission. A coordinated model can allow different vendors; it still needs a named person responsible for each dependency.

Require three scopes even when one team coordinates the purchase

Separate acquisition services, construction and furnishing, and ongoing management in the proposal. Identify the legal contracting party, price, payment trigger, deliverable and buyer approval for each. One contact person does not establish that one agreement covers every task. The construction scope should name the work, exclusions, permit responsibilities, change-order process, inspection milestones and acceptance standard before you make an offer whose economics depend on that renovation.

Ask both sides how the construction estimate was produced before purchase. A visual concept is not a measured scope, and a completed project elsewhere is not a quote for this building. Obtain independent inspections and professional permit review. If the required work cannot be priced or authorized within the purchase protections, delay the offer decision. Do not treat selecting an integrated team as a reason to waive independent review of the property or the proposed work.

Ask counsel which agreements remain in force if you do not close, the approved renovation changes or a manager is replaced. Clarify deposit refund terms, unfinished-work responsibilities and any linkage between contracts. These are questions to resolve in the actual documents, not assertions about either firm's terms. StayRnR's public website terms largely discuss site use and guest bookings; do not treat them as a substitute for a buyer's construction and service agreements.

Do not apply the management headline to acquisition and construction

StayRnR's management page publishes 20% of gross revenue and says cleaning, supplies, maintenance and repairs are separately billed to the owner. That operating fee is not an all-in construction, acquisition or expense budget. Ask for the definition of gross revenue, treatment of taxes and cleaning collections, payment timing and every excluded cost in the agreement. Request the comparable operator terms for the BNB/local-vendor route rather than assuming a standard percentage.

The StayRnR FAQ describes no lock-in and no setup cost, while its investment page describes an agreed establishment fee and individually priced projects. Do not infer that purchasing, renovating and furnishing a house is free. Ask which service the FAQ statement covers, what establishment charges apply, and how the purchase, build and management agreements interact. Request written pricing from BNB and every local vendor too; this article cannot declare which actual proposal costs less.

Illustrative fee-base calculation only: 20% of a hypothetical $100,000 contractual revenue base is $20,000. If the base instead includes an additional $12,000 of cleaning collections, 20% of $112,000 is $22,400. The $2,400 difference is why the definition matters; neither base is asserted to be StayRnR's contract definition or forecast revenue. Both calculations exclude other expenses. Use the definition and conservative revenue inputs actually applicable to the candidate before setting an offer ceiling.

Model construction cash and a delayed opening before offering

Illustrative independent project, not a provider quote or result: a $420,000 candidate needs a hypothetical $105,000 down payment, $14,000 closing costs, $75,000 construction, $25,000 furnishing and $15,000 combined service and project charges. These uses total $234,000 before retained cash. A buyer with $270,000 has $36,000 left. Make sure charges are not duplicated and that no required cost has been omitted; obtain lender confirmation of financing and all actual contracts.

Suppose a professional scope update adds $18,000 of work, and a three-month opening delay requires another $9,000 of carrying cash. The same purchase now leaves $9,000, not $36,000. If the buyer's independently chosen retained-cash floor was $25,000, the revised plan is $16,000 short. A provider's coordination promise cannot pay that gap. Investigate a lower price, different scope that still satisfies requirements, more acceptable funds or another property. Do not delete mandatory work to make the budget fit.

Put each proposed payment on the timeline: what is due before closing, after ownership transfers, at verified construction milestones and before launch. Ask whether any amounts are refundable if the property does not close, and who verifies completed work. Include inspection, lender and insurer conditions and applicable approvals. A nominally cheaper provider can create more cash exposure if substantial payments occur before dependencies are resolved; an integrated plan can still be the better fit when its verified scope and cash demands suit the buyer.

Compare a smaller-renovation candidate as a real alternative rather than treating major construction as inevitable. Use the purchase-and-launch cash planner for uses and liquidity, and the opening timeline for date dependencies. Neither a portfolio occupancy claim nor an attractive finished-room photograph establishes the acquired property's permitted use, supported revenue or first-guest date. Base the offer on this address and a conservative operating case.

Pick the provider only after the project passes its own purchase test

Proceed with either approach when the candidate independently merits purchase, the written delivery plan identifies its accountable parties, all material construction and permit questions are resolved within protections, and the complete budget retains adequate downside cash. Renegotiate service scope, project price or the property offer when a priced gap can be resolved. Delay while quotes, contracting parties, approval requirements or deadlines remain unclear. Reject a purchase that relies on unpriced construction, unauthorized rental use or assumed immediate bookings regardless of the provider name.

Before paying, ask for the named agent, construction entity, relevant professional credentials, insurance, compensation and referral disclosures, and the exact management choice and exit process. Provide both proposals to independent local advisers. Do not assume affiliation, guaranteed warranties or a particular legal relationship from branding. Frequently asked questions

Does StayRnR help buy and renovate an STR, or only manage one?

Its investment page describes sourcing, in-house renovation and furnishing, followed by operation. Confirm the actual scope and contracting entities for the property you intend to buy.

Does StayRnR's 20% fee include the purchase and renovation?

Do not infer that. Its management page describes a recurring gross-revenue fee with operating costs separately billed, while project establishment and construction pricing require their own quote.

Is BNB Accelerator or StayRnR the cheaper acquisition route?

A fair answer requires current itemized proposals for the same candidate, build scope, payment timing and operating assumptions. Neither a fee headline nor a management percentage answers the whole purchase question.

What if the renovation delays the STR opening?

Recalculate carrying cash and retained liquidity from a realistic revised timeline. Ask who owns remaining dependencies and what the actual contracts permit; do not rely on a promised first-booking date.

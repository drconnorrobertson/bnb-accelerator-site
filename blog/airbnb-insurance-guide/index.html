Airbnb Insurance: What Investors Actually Need

Published July 28, 2026·7 min read

A standard homeowners policy is written for a house you live in. A landlord policy is written for a tenant on a lease. A short-term rental is neither, and the gap between what people assume they have and what they actually have is where six figure losses live.

Why the standard policy fails

Homeowners policies commonly contain a business use exclusion. Operating a property as a nightly rental is a business activity, and an insurer that discovers it after a claim can deny the claim and may cancel the policy. This is not an obscure edge case. It is the most common insurance failure in this asset class.

Landlord and dwelling fire policies are closer but still typically contemplate a long term tenant in possession under a lease. Frequent transient occupancy is a different risk profile: more people, more turnovers, more unfamiliarity with the property, and more liability exposure per year.

What coverage a short-term rental actually needs

  • A policy written for short-term or vacation rental use. Either a specialty short-term rental product or a commercial policy endorsed for the use. The distinguishing feature is that the carrier knows what you are doing and has priced it.
  • Liability limits appropriate to the exposure. Hot tubs, pools, docks, fire pits, stairs, and decks all raise it. Many owners carry $1 million per occurrence at the policy level and add an umbrella above it.
  • Business income or loss of rents coverage. If a covered loss takes the property offline for four months in peak season, this is what replaces the revenue.
  • Contents and furnishings coverage at replacement cost. A furnished five bedroom cabin can hold $80,000 to $150,000 of personal property that a dwelling only policy will not cover.
  • Peril specific coverage where the geography demands it. Flood is excluded from standard property policies and requires a separate policy. Wind and hail may be excluded or carry a large separate deductible in coastal markets. Wildfire availability has tightened in several western markets.

Platform protection is not insurance

Booking platforms offer host protection programs, and they are useful. They are also conditional programs with exclusions, claim procedures, and limits, and they are not a substitute for a policy written for your use. Treat them as a supplement that may respond to guest caused damage, not as your coverage.

What it costs

Premiums vary enormously with geography, replacement cost, and amenities, and any national average is meaningless. What is consistent is the direction of the differences: coastal wind exposure, wildfire exposure, older roofs, pools, and hot tubs all increase premium, sometimes dramatically. In several coastal markets, insurance has moved from a rounding error to one of the three largest line items in the operating budget.

This is why we underwrite insurance as a real number rather than a percentage assumption. A pro forma that assumes 0.5 percent of value for insurance in a Gulf Coast market is not a pro forma, it is a guess. See how to analyze a deal for the full expense build.

Entity structure and insurance are different questions

Owners frequently ask whether an LLC replaces insurance. It does not. An entity may provide a liability shield in some circumstances, but it does not pay claims, and a lender may have views on title vesting. The two decisions interact and neither substitutes for the other. See holding a short-term rental in an LLC, and confirm the specifics with your own attorney and insurance professional.

Questions to ask before binding

  1. Does this policy explicitly permit nightly or short-term rental use, in writing?
  2. What are the liability limits, and do they contemplate a hot tub, pool, or dock?
  3. Is business income or loss of rents included, and for how many months?
  4. Is contents coverage at replacement cost, and what is the sublimit?
  5. What is excluded: flood, wind, hail, wildfire, mold, ordinance and law?
  6. What is the deductible structure, including separate wind or named storm deductibles?
  7. Are there occupancy conditions, such as maximum guest counts or vacancy limits, that could void coverage?

Frequently asked questions

Does homeowners insurance cover an Airbnb?

Usually not. Standard homeowners policies commonly contain a business use exclusion, and operating a property as a nightly rental is a business activity. An insurer that discovers the use after a claim may deny it and cancel the policy. A policy written for short-term rental use is the correct product.

Is Airbnb host protection enough coverage?

No. Platform host protection programs are conditional programs with their own exclusions, limits, and claim procedures. They can be a useful supplement, particularly for guest caused damage, but they are not a substitute for a policy written for your specific use and geography.

What insurance coverages does a short-term rental need?

A policy that explicitly permits short-term rental use, liability limits appropriate to the amenities, business income or loss of rents coverage, contents coverage at replacement cost for the furnishings, and separate peril coverage where geography requires it, such as flood, wind and hail, or wildfire.

Does an LLC replace short-term rental insurance?

No. An entity may provide a liability shield in some circumstances but it does not pay claims. Entity structure and insurance are separate decisions that interact, and both should be reviewed with your own attorney and insurance professional.

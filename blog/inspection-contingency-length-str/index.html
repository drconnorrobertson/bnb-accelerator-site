How Long Should an STR Inspection Contingency Be?

Published September 23, 2026·Updated October 6, 2026·7 min read

When buying a short-term rental, choose an inspection contingency long enough to obtain and evaluate the evidence your purchase depends on, not a familiar number of days. Physical condition is only one workstream. Supported rental use, capacity, insurance and financing may require separate records, specialists and contract protections. There is no universal ten-day or fifteen-day answer. Start with actual availability and the executed contract's clock before committing to an offer.

Book a call to map this STR's purchase evidence and review deadlines before making an offer. Bring the proposed terms and provider availability; local counsel should determine the legal protections.

First establish what the inspection clause actually protects

Have your agent and local counsel identify the start event, duration, exact expiry time and time zone, calendar-versus-business-day definition, holidays, access conditions and required notices. Ask which findings the clause covers and which need a separate financing, insurance, association-document or lawful-use condition. A physical inspection provision does not automatically protect every commercial-use question. Earnest-money treatment can change under different clauses and amendments; do not invent a single universal nonrefundable date.

Record when a notice must be sent or received, who receives it, and what documents or signatures are required. Distinguish a request for repairs from a termination, waiver or extension. Ask counsel what happens if the seller does not answer. An unanswered request or pending inspector appointment is not a signed extension. This page is a planning worksheet, not a clause to copy into a purchase contract.

CFPB inspection guidance recommends prompt independent inspection and time for additional checks, distinguishes an appraisal from an inspection, and notes that some lending programs impose repair conditions. It does not prescribe a national STR diligence period. Obtain the inspector's actual scope and reporting time rather than assuming an inspection appointment completes the review.

Build a deliverable schedule, not an appointment list

Ask each provider what evidence it needs first, the earliest feasible appointment, access requirements, report delivery and time for follow-up. Record estimates as estimates, not promises. A booked site visit without a written result can still leave the purchase decision unresolved. Arrange parallel checks where justified; do not postpone known septic, well or legal-use issues until the general report merely because they appear on another checklist.

Here the distinct question is whether those findings can arrive in time.

Worked hypothetical: the slowest dependency determines the window

Illustrative scheduling exercise only. Assume counsel has already translated every applicable contract date into the same business-day planning scale; day0 is the confirmed start and day10 the relevant decision/notice expiry. This is not a rule that contracts use business days. Ignore no actual calendar date, holiday, time zone or delivery rule when replacing these invented inputs.

A general inspection occurs on day4 and its report arrives day5. A finding then triggers a specialist with an assumed three-business-day wait: appointment day8, report day9. Allow an assumed two further business days for professional interpretation, updated buyer underwriting and required notice delivery. The dependent path finishes day11: four plus one plus three plus one plus two equals eleven. A day10 deadline therefore misses the assumed ready date by one business day, even though the first inspection took place well before expiry.

Other assumed results arrive in parallel: insurance day6, association review day7, buyer-use evidence day8 and the required lender update day10. If the same two-day final review starts only after all decisive results, the full schedule finishes day12: the latest result on day10 plus two review/notice days. The day10 deadline then misses readiness by two business days. A day14 deadline leaves two planning business days beyond day12 only if these are sufficient, confirmed deliverables and the signed terms cover the necessary questions. It is not a recommended minimum or a promise the seller will accept it.

If a justified specialist review can be ordered independently from day0 and returns day5, the specialist chain no longer waits for the day5 general report. But with the lender result still on day10 and two final review days, the overall finish remains day12. Faster inspections alone have not solved the deadline mismatch. Identify the bottleneck again when actual delivery or review requirements change. Likewise, a municipal reply on day8 stating only that an application is received is not the buyer-use evidence assumed above.

If a decisive permit cannot be issued before buyer ownership, simply requesting a longer inspection window may never resolve that dependency. Ask counsel, the authority and lender whether a separately protected transaction structure or a genuinely acceptable alternative use is possible. Do not price unapproved STR revenue as supported. The pending-permit extension guide handles the separate decision to pay for another protected interval.

Negotiate the evidence path before waiving protection

Proceed when the covered decisive deliverables can be obtained, reviewed and acted on within the agreed dates and the purchase case works. Renegotiate the proposed window, access, document delivery or specific conditions when the path is feasible but the current clock is not. Delay under a fully executed, appropriate amendment when a named answer is outstanding. Reject the STR thesis when required use is unavailable or decisive evidence cannot be obtained under protection you are willing to accept. A longer closing date alone does not necessarily move another deadline.

Check lender lock, financing, possession and vendor dates separately when changing a window. Before agreeing to charges, record incremental expenses and whether any consideration is credited or refundable under actual terms. Do not assume the held deposit is the maximum downside. CFPB closing guidance cautions that breaking a purchase contract can have consequences beyond the deposit and lender fees. Obtain transaction-specific legal advice before any notice, waiver or refusal to close.

Book a call to compare this STR's evidence timeline with a candidate whose purchase conditions can be verified sooner. Substantial available capital does not replace lawful-use evidence or valid contract protection. Questions to resolve before committing to this purchase

How do I schedule STR specialist reviews within a diligence window?

Identify systems excluded from the general inspection and obtain availability early. Build the timetable from required evidence rather than selecting a customary number of days.

How do I match STR permit review to inspection deadlines?

List government and association responses needed for the intended use. Ask counsel which contract protection covers those questions instead of assuming a physical inspection clause does.

How do I budget STR inspections before choosing contingency length?

Price general and specialist work, including possible follow-up review. Keep sufficient cash and time to investigate a material finding rather than spending the entire window on the first report.

How do I request an STR inspection extension before expiry?

Ask counsel about notice and signed amendment requirements while protection remains available. An inspector delay or seller conversation does not automatically move the contractual deadline.

How do I decide whether an STR inspection waiver is affordable?

List unknown condition, use and cash exposure before assessing the waiver. Compare potential funding needs with actual liquidity and counsel-reviewed remedies rather than market pressure alone.

Record the evidence, responsible reviewer, cash effect and transaction deadline for any unresolved item. Bring those findings to the STR acquisition call before deciding whether to proceed, revise the offer or exercise available contract protection.

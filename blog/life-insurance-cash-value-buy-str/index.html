Should you use life insurance cash value to buy an STR?

Published October 6, 2026·8 min read

An existing policy with accessible cash value may help fund a short-term rental purchase, but its death benefit is not your down payment and a policy loan is not free money. Before offering, compare the actual insurer's loan and surrender options, obtain lender acceptance, and check whether the purchase remains affordable while protecting needed insurance coverage. This is a purchase-budget review for an STR buyer planning to close within zero to six months—not a recommendation to buy life insurance to invest in real estate. Book a call to set an STR offer budget using verified funding and a fully funded launch.

Identify the policy value actually available for your STR purchase

Start with your existing contract and an insurer-issued statement. Ask who owns the policy and can authorize access, the current cash value, outstanding loans and interest, available additional loan amount, net surrender proceeds and relevant charges. The amount printed as life coverage is not the amount available to close. Do not add a possible death benefit to your available acquisition assets or assume an employer's term policy has a borrowable balance.

The NAIC consumer guide distinguishes cash-value coverage from term insurance, which generally does not build usable cash value. It also warns that replacing coverage may be costly. Use the guide to frame questions about your actual contract, not to select a policy based on a property listing. Buying or converting coverage now does not establish that enough cash will be available by your offer deadline.

Get two separate, current insurer quotes: the additional loan you could receive without surrendering the policy, and the net payment if you surrender it. Ask how long processing and delivery would take, whether consent or additional documents are required, and how long each quote remains valid. An illustrated future cash value, a request awaiting approval or an agent's rough estimate is not received closing cash.

A death-benefit payout already received by a beneficiary is a different source of funds. A policy settlement with a third party is another distinct transaction. This framework concerns the owner's access to an existing policy's cash value. Have the relevant professionals review any other arrangement rather than applying the same assumptions to it.

Compare a policy loan with surrender before committing to the property

With a policy loan, ask the insurer for the interest method, rate-change provisions, effect on policy values, required premiums and the circumstances that could jeopardize coverage. With surrender, ask exactly which coverage and riders terminate and what net payment you receive. Do not assume that surrendering a policy and later replacing it restores the same coverage on the same terms.

The New York Department of Financial Services FAQ explains that outstanding loans can reduce surrender proceeds and death benefits, and describes the risk when loans and interest exceed policy loan value. It describes New York's setting; do not import its notice periods or rate provisions as nationwide contract terms. Ask your insurer to apply the actual policy and jurisdiction.

Have the purchase lender review the exact proceeds and obligations

Fannie Mae's cash-value-life-insurance topic allows net policy-loan or surrender proceeds for down payment, closing costs and reserves under that channel. For closing funds it calls for evidence of receipt from the insurer; reserve-only cash value has different documentation without necessarily requiring liquidation. The lender must review additional obligations. Payment exclusion from its debt ratio is conditional, not automatic.

That source-of-funds rule does not approve your borrower, property, disclosed STR use or entire financing file. Ask your actual lender to confirm current acceptance, receipt documents, reserve treatment and repayment requirements for the proposed transaction. A DSCR lender may apply its own rules. Do not substitute a household coverage discussion for underwriting approval, or underwriting approval for a coverage decision.

Give the lender the proposed loan or surrender documents and disclose existing policy borrowing. Ask how much remaining policy value, if any, is countable after the transaction. Keep a reconciliation with three separate amounts: net proceeds received, cash assigned to closing and launch, and remaining assets accepted as reserves. Do not use the original policy value in addition to the money extracted from it.

Mortgage qualification and household affordability are separate tests. Even where a lender's debt calculation excludes a policy-loan payment, interest and premiums can still affect your actual finances. Confirm the closing agent's transfer instructions through trusted channels, and give sensitive policy and financial records only to authorized professionals through secure systems.

Run a funding-gap and policy-carry comparison for one STR offer

Illustrative only: a candidate needs $88,000 for down payment and closing, $24,000 for furnishing and launch, and $28,000 of retained operating cash. Total required funding is $140,000. The buyer has $95,000 of usable bank cash, so the gap is $45,000. A $500,000 death benefit does not solve that gap. Neither does a $75,000 displayed policy value if the insurer's net additional loan quote is only $42,000.

Using that hypothetical $42,000 quote produces $137,000 of cash, leaving a $3,000 shortfall before any other costs change. Do not draw the shortfall from the $28,000 reserve and still label reserves fully funded. Ask whether a supported price or permitted closing-cost change actually reduces the cash requirement; a $3,000 price reduction does not necessarily reduce a financed buyer's required cash by $3,000. Otherwise select a lower-entry-cash property or delay.

Now assume the insurer's separate net surrender quote is $57,000. Combined with bank cash it produces $152,000, a $12,000 cushion over the hypothetical budget. That larger cushion does not make surrender the winner: it comes with a different coverage outcome, and the quote is not a personalized after-tax conclusion. An adviser may identify tax cash or protection costs that consume part of the apparent cushion. Keep the insurance decision visible instead of valuing it at zero.

For a separate carrying-cost test, suppose the quoted policy-loan balance is $42,000, annual interest is 6%, paid annually, and required premiums are $2,400 a year. Those invented terms imply $2,520 of annual interest and $4,920 of combined interest and premiums, before any principal repayment. Premiums may already be a household expense; distinguish existing expense from the incremental $2,520 rather than calling all $4,920 new loan cost. If illustrative property cash after operating costs and its mortgage is only $2,000 yearly, it does not cover that incremental interest. Test who pays during a no-bookings launch, and do not treat interest-only funding as principal repayment.

Replace every amount with actual quotes and the complete purchase-and-launch budget. Run the property through the downside calculator separately from the household policy schedule. Do not assume future refinancing, property appreciation or non-guaranteed policy credits will repay the borrowing. These examples are arithmetic checks, not offered insurance terms, lender requirements or expected rental results.

Turn the verified funding choice into an executable STR offer

Proceed when the exact funding option is authorized, proceeds can be received and accepted before closing, needed protection has been reviewed, and the property and household downside both work. Renegotiate only where documented purchase costs can actually change within the transaction. Delay if policy values, tax effects, coverage needs, lender treatment or disbursement timing remain unresolved. Reject the funding plan if it requires sacrificing needed protection or assumes rental income always pays the policy obligations.

Reject an unsuitable property independently of the available funds. Verify legal short-stay use, seller revenue, actual operating costs, purchase conditions and the launch plan. Insurance cash cannot make prohibited guest use or a weak deal acceptable. Have your licensed broker and attorney align the contract's financing and diligence deadlines with real funding clearance; do not promise cash before the insurer and lender have established it.

Bring the two insurer quotes, professional coverage and tax questions, lender conditions and one actual listing to the acquisition discussion. Frequently asked questions

Can life insurance cash value fund an STR down payment?

Potentially, using an existing policy's net accessible loan or surrender proceeds, with actual insurer documentation and lender acceptance. Do not count the death benefit or an illustrated future value as available closing funds.

Is surrender better than a policy loan when buying an Airbnb?

Not automatically. Compare net cash, tax review, coverage lost or retained, interest, premiums and downside funding. A larger surrender payment may not justify losing protection you need.

Can I count the policy's original value and extracted cash as reserves?

Do not double-count them. Ask the lender to reconcile net proceeds, cash uses and the policy value remaining after the actual transaction.

Does lender acceptance mean the policy loan is affordable?

No. Test actual interest, premiums and repayment against household funds and delayed STR opening, regardless of the lender's debt-ratio treatment.

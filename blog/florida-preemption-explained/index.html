Florida's Short-Term Rental Preemption and Why the Map Is Uneven

Published December 9, 2021·6 min read

Florida is frequently described as a friendly state for short-term rentals, and at the state level that is broadly accurate. The complication is a grandfathering provision that leaves some of the state's most restrictive local rules fully enforceable.

What the preemption does

Florida law preempts local regulation that prohibits vacation rentals or regulates their duration or frequency, for local ordinances adopted after June 1, 2011.

That prevents a Florida city from adopting a new ban, a new minimum stay requirement, or a new limit on how often a property may be rented.

It does not prevent local governments from regulating in ways that apply to all residential properties, or from imposing registration, life safety and noise requirements. Those remain available and are widely used.

Short-term rental rules change frequently and the controlling rule is usually local rather than statewide. Treat this as orientation, then verify the current position with the city or county directly and read any association declaration separately. This is not legal advice.

The grandfathering problem

Ordinances in place before June 1, 2011 were grandfathered and remain enforceable. Several Florida cities operate under restrictive frameworks that could not be adopted today.

Miami Beach is the most-cited example, with a restrictive framework and substantial penalties. Older cities with older ordinances are exactly where the surviving restrictions sit.

The counterintuitive consequence is that a city's friendliness toward short-term rentals correlates more with when it wrote its rules than with how tourism-dependent it is. A historic city with a 2009 ordinance can be far more restrictive than a beach town that never wrote one.

The Panhandle vacation markets and the purpose-built Orlando resort communities are the most straightforward, which is a substantial part of why our Florida transactions concentrate there.

The licensing layer

A vacation rental license from the Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation is generally required for properties rented more than three times a year for periods of less than 30 days, or advertised as such.

That is a state requirement separate from any local registration, and it carries life safety and inspection obligations. It is administrative rather than restrictive, and it does have to be done.

Local registration typically sits on top of it, and county tourist development tax registration on top of that. Three separate filings for one property is normal in Florida.

The tax layer

State sales tax applies to transient accommodation, and county tourist development tax applies on top at rates set locally.

Platform collection varies by county. A platform may collect state sales tax and not the county tourist development tax in the same location, and the split differs between adjacent counties.

Confirm directly with each taxing authority for the specific address which taxes remain your responsibility, and register where required even where the platform collects, since many jurisdictions require registration regardless.

The insurance reality

Regulation is not Florida's main underwriting challenge. Coastal insurance is. Wind and flood coverage has repriced sharply across the state and is now a material line item rather than a footnote.

Condominium and homeowner association special assessments are the related risk, driven by structural reserve requirements and post-storm repairs. These arrive as large one-time bills rather than a smooth monthly line.

Quote insurance for the specific address before an offer, confirm the flood zone designation and elevation certificate, and model a disrupted peak season rather than only a clean year.

What this means practically

Do not rely on a general belief that Florida is permissive. Verify the specific parcel, because the grandfathered ordinances are exactly the ones that a general rule would miss.

Ask the city directly, in writing, for the exact address, and retain the answer. Historic use is not the same as permitted use, and a property that has operated as a rental may have been operating outside a grandfathered ordinance.

Read any association declaration separately. State preemption reaches local governments; it does not reach private covenants, and much of Florida's coastal inventory is condominium product with its own rental rules.

Frequently asked questions

Does Florida allow short-term rentals everywhere?

No. State law preempts local ordinances adopted after June 1, 2011 that prohibit vacation rentals or regulate duration and frequency, but ordinances in place before that date were grandfathered and remain fully enforceable.

Do I need a state license for a Florida vacation rental?

Generally yes. A vacation rental license from the Department of Business and Professional Regulation is typically required for properties rented more than three times a year for periods under 30 days, separate from any local registration.

What is the biggest underwriting risk in Florida?

Insurance rather than regulation. Wind and flood coverage has repriced sharply, and association special assessments driven by structural reserve requirements and storm repairs arrive as large one-time bills.

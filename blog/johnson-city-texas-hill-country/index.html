Johnson City and the Texas Hill Country Wine Corridor

Published March 9, 2023·6 min read

Laxman and Sabitri bought a six-bedroom in Johnson City, Texas for $680,000. The property sits in the Hill Country wine corridor between Austin and Fredericksburg, and the thesis is a specific guest: a group of eight to twelve people from Austin, San Antonio or Houston out for a wine weekend, a wedding or a bachelorette.

The wine corridor guest

US-290 between Johnson City and Fredericksburg has become one of the more concentrated wine tourism corridors in the country, with dozens of tasting rooms along a short stretch of highway.

The guest that draws is a group rather than a couple, and they want one large house within driving distance of the tasting rooms rather than separate hotel rooms in Fredericksburg. Six bedrooms is the right target because the competing inventory is largely four-bedroom ranch houses.

Wedding and event-adjacent demand is a significant secondary segment. The Hill Country hosts a large number of venue weddings, and the accommodation demand around them is substantial and books far in advance.

Unincorporated land is the regulatory advantage

Much of the Hill Country inventory sits on unincorporated county land in Blanco, Gillespie and surrounding counties, outside city short-term rental ordinances entirely.

That is a genuine advantage compared with Austin proper, where the licensing regime restricting non-owner-occupied short-term rentals has been litigated and portions held unconstitutional, producing years of uncertainty.

It is also a reason to verify precisely rather than assume. A property inside Johnson City limits and a property two miles outside them are governed differently, and the mailing address does not tell you which.

Confirm with the county assessor whether the parcel is inside or outside city limits before relying on any regulatory analysis. In the Hill Country this single fact frequently determines whether the property can operate as intended.

Spring and autumn, not summer

Texas summers are hot enough to suppress outdoor-driven demand in July and August, which inverts the seasonality relative to most of the country.

Wildflower season in March and April is a genuine peak, with bluebonnet traffic drawing substantial visitation. Autumn harvest is the second peak, running roughly September through November.

The model has to reflect that shape. A Hill Country proforma built on a summer-peaking assumption will be wrong in both directions, understating spring and autumn and overstating July and August.

What the property needs

  • Large gathering and dining space, because the wine weekend group eats and drinks together rather than dispersing.
  • Outdoor living with shade, since Hill Country evenings are the product for most of the usable season.
  • A pool, which is close to expected at this price point given Texas heat.
  • Bedroom count over square footage, because the guest is optimizing cost per person.
  • Parking for multiple vehicles, since groups drive separately from three different metros.
  • Genuine well and septic capacity for group occupancy, which is a common rural constraint.

Risks worth pricing

Water is the substantive one. Much of the Hill Country runs on wells, and drought conditions in Central Texas have been persistent. Well capacity and water availability deserve real diligence, and a property that cannot supply a twelve-person group in August has a problem that no amount of marketing fixes.

Event-adjacent demand carries the usual noise and neighbor exposure. Rural properties have more tolerance than suburban ones, and the tolerance is not unlimited, particularly as the corridor develops.

The market is also newer than it looks. Wine tourism in the corridor has grown quickly, and rapid growth invites both new supply and eventual regulation. Neither is imminent, and both belong in a long-hold model.

Fredericksburg comparison

The obvious alternative to Johnson City is Fredericksburg, twenty-five miles west and the corridor's commercial center.

Fredericksburg has the shops, the restaurants, the Main Street that guests walk, and correspondingly higher prices and a permit program with density limits in some districts. Johnson City is quieter, cheaper, and much of the surrounding inventory sits on unincorporated county land outside city ordinances.

The guest calculation is whether the group wants to walk to dinner or drive to it. A wine weekend group with a designated driver is happy to stay fifteen minutes out for a substantially larger house on more land. A couples trip prioritizing walkability will pay the Fredericksburg premium.

For a six-bedroom group property, the Johnson City side of the corridor is generally the better trade: more land, more privacy, lower basis, and a regulatory position that is lighter and less likely to tighten. Verify the parcel's incorporation status either way, because that single fact frequently determines what is permitted.

Frequently asked questions

Why is the Texas Hill Country attractive for short-term rentals?

Much of the inventory sits on unincorporated county land outside city short-term rental ordinances, while drawing wine weekend, wedding and group demand from Austin, San Antonio and Houston.

When is peak season in the Hill Country?

Spring and autumn. Wildflower season in March and April and the autumn harvest window carry the year, while July and August heat suppresses demand. That is the reverse of most US markets.

What should I check on a rural Hill Country property?

Well capacity and water availability, given persistent Central Texas drought, plus septic capacity sized for group occupancy rather than a single family. Also confirm whether the parcel sits inside or outside city limits.

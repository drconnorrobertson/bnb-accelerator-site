Does buying an STR through an LLC remove the loan's personal guarantee?

Published October 6, 2026·6 min read

No—not simply because title and the borrowing entity are an LLC. An STR purchase loan may still require owners to sign a separate personal guarantee. Before offering, obtain the lender's signer requirements and draft guarantee, have independent counsel explain its scope, and test whether you are willing and able to accept that exposure. Property-income qualification is not the same as nonrecourse debt. Book a call to compare the STR acquisition's leverage and downside before accepting the loan structure; BNB Accelerator does not provide legal advice or approve guarantees.

Read the LLC STR purchase loan and guarantee separately

Ask for the proposed borrower, title owner and individual signers in writing. Read the note, security instrument and guarantee as separate documents with your lawyer. An LLC on the deed answers an ownership question; it does not tell you what obligations a person accepted elsewhere. Similarly, a loan evaluated using rental income rather than employment income can still require personal credit review and a repayment guarantee.

For a current primary-source example, Visio Lending's published process says it requires entity owners to sign a personal guarantee and may require a spouse's consent document. Host Financial's STR financing page describes full recourse and personal guarantees for majority owners, with ownership thresholds depending on the program. Reviewed October 6, 2026, these are provider descriptions, not universal lender rules or approval for your file. Obtain the actual proposed documents.

Do not infer nonrecourse from phrases such as no personal-income documentation, LLC eligible or investment-purpose financing. If nonrecourse is advertised, ask the lender and your attorney to identify exactly which obligations remain, which exceptions apply and whether the option is available for this STR purchase. This article does not establish that either cited provider offers a nonrecourse STR product.

Build a signer-and-obligation map before going under contract

Give the lender the complete ownership and control structure early. Ask whose credit, liquidity and signatures are required, including indirect owners where relevant. Do not rearrange ownership merely to hide a person the lender needs to review. A change to the entity or borrower after an offer can require underwriting and transaction-document changes; ask your agent, lender and counsel to align the contract from the start.

If the draft cannot be supplied before you must make capital nonrefundable, ask counsel how to protect the review window or choose a different financing path.

Test downside dollars without pretending to predict a legal claim

Illustrative only: suppose an STR is purchased for $600,000 with a $450,000 loan. At a later downside point, assume a $440,000 loan payoff, $430,000 gross sale proceeds and $25,000 of selling costs. The $405,000 net proceeds would leave a $35,000 arithmetic debt shortfall. Actual liability is not established by that calculation: the loan documents, guarantee, applicable law, fees and enforcement facts require counsel's review. Use it to identify a question you must resolve, not to forecast litigation.

If the lender-required guarantee exposes a person to obligations beyond the collateral, ask what amount and circumstances are relevant under the actual contract. Do not cap the possible obligation at the example's $35,000 simply because it is the shortfall in one scenario. Interest, expenses, other pledged collateral or contractual provisions may matter. Your lawyer should explain the applicable obligations without relying on a generic internet summary.

Compare a lower-price acquisition or lower leverage using the same supported STR revenue and expenses. Lower debt can reduce a modeled shortfall, but a larger down payment can also consume launch and operating cash. Pair the purchase budget with the operating downside test. A smaller modeled shortfall is not proof of limited recourse, and a full guarantee does not itself prove the property is a bad investment. Decide from both the contract and the property evidence.

Resolve partner risk before choosing the property

Illustrative ownership test: two buyers intend to own an STR equally, contribute the same acquisition cash and split operating proceeds. The lender nevertheless requires a particular guarantee from one or both people. A fifty-fifty operating agreement does not by itself tell you how the lender can pursue a signer. Ask counsel to compare the proposed guarantee with your internal contribution, reimbursement and dispute arrangements before either buyer commits.

Have each person explain the downside obligation they believe they are accepting. If the answers differ, stop the signature process until the contract is understood. Discuss how emergency cash calls will work, who can authorize a property sale, what happens when one person refuses more capital and what evidence permits releasing a guarantor. Internal indemnification or reimbursement rights are not the same as a lender releasing its claim, and collecting from a partner is not guaranteed.

Our STR partnership guide addresses ownership and management decisions. This loan review comes first when a guarantor disagreement would prevent financing or change the amount each buyer is prepared to risk. Do not wait until closing to discover that the intended passive partner will not sign the required document. Match the acquisition timeline to informed signer approval.

Choose the purchase and financing together

Proceed when every required signer has accepted reviewed terms, the borrower and title structure align, property evidence is sound, and the purchase budget preserves enough liquidity for conservative operations. The lender's willingness to approve you is not an instruction to accept any guarantee or maximize leverage. Use the complete term-sheet review for rate, maturity, prepayment and other conditions alongside this liability map.

Renegotiate loan scope or compare an actually available alternative when the proposed liability differs from what you agreed to consider. Renegotiate acquisition price or leverage if downside dollars exceed your risk limit. Delay within appropriate contract protections while draft terms or partner approval remain unresolved. Reject a financing path when an essential signer refuses its terms, a required liability limit is unavailable or the acquisition depends on misunderstanding recourse. Reject the property separately when legal STR use or supported economics fail.

Bring the draft guarantee, lender's signer list, ownership diagram, purchase budget and candidate listing to the next discussion. Frequently asked questions

Does an LLC eliminate a DSCR loan's personal guarantee?

Not automatically. Entity ownership and a person's guarantee are separate issues. Obtain the actual lender requirements and have counsel review the proposed guarantee before committing to the STR purchase.

Does no personal-income documentation mean no personal liability?

No. A property-income qualification method does not establish a nonrecourse loan. Review the borrower, guarantors and contract scope separately.

Are all STR DSCR loans full recourse?

Do not make a universal assumption. The cited providers describe personal-guarantee requirements, but your proposed product and documents control. Verify any claimed nonrecourse option and exceptions with the lender and counsel.

Does selling my LLC interest release the STR loan guarantee?

Do not assume so. Ask counsel and the lender which written conditions release each signer; a partner agreement or ownership transfer is not necessarily a lender release.

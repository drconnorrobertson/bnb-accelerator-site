Cash-Out Refinancing a Short-Term Rental

Published April 24, 2026·6 min read

Refinancing a performing short-term rental to fund the next one is the standard path to a portfolio, and it is also the standard path to being overleveraged in a soft season. The difference is entirely in how the numbers are stress tested.

Why owners refinance

  • To fund the next down payment without waiting to save it.
  • To access appreciation without triggering a taxable sale, which is a genuine advantage over selling. See exit strategy.
  • To move from a conventional loan to an entity friendly product such as a DSCR loan, which changes what the portfolio can do next. See DSCR loans explained.
  • To consolidate higher cost debt used during acquisition or furnishing.

The stress test before you do it

  1. Model the new payment against the trough month, not the annual average. A property that services debt comfortably in July and not in February is a property with a timing problem. See cash reserves and seasonality.
  2. Model a twenty percent revenue decline. If the property still services debt, the leverage is defensible. If it does not, you are betting on a market that has never once cooperated indefinitely.
  3. Keep the reserve out of the proceeds. Refinancing and deploying every dollar into a down payment leaves two properties with no buffer instead of one property with a buffer.
  4. Check the appraisal basis. Short-term rental properties can appraise on residential comparables rather than income, which sometimes produces a lower value than the revenue suggests.
  5. Confirm seasoning and occupancy requirements, which differ substantially by lender and product.

The failure mode

It is predictable and it looks the same every time. An owner refinances a strong performer at the top of a good year, deploys everything into a second property, and enters the following winter with two mortgages, two insurance renewals, one thin reserve, and a new property producing first year revenue rather than stabilized revenue.

Nothing about that requires a market downturn to become painful. It only requires a normal off season and a new listing behaving normally. See timeline to profitability.

A defensible pace

Portfolios that hold up generally add one to two properties a year, with each stabilized and producing a documented full season before the next acquisition, and with reserves maintained at every property rather than pooled optimistically.

Cash out refinancing fits that pace well and fits an eighteen month sprint badly. See scaling from one to ten and financing options compared.

Two tax notes

Refinancing is generally not a taxable event, which is why it is attractive relative to selling. The deductibility of interest can depend on how the proceeds are used, which is a tracing question worth raising with your CPA rather than assuming.

Separately, refinancing does not change your depreciation. Basis is driven by the purchase and improvements, not by the loan, which means a cash out refinance does not create new depreciation to claim. See cost segregation for Airbnb properties.

Frequently asked questions

Should I refinance my short-term rental to buy another?

It is the standard path to a portfolio, provided the new payment is stress tested against the trough month rather than the annual average and against a twenty percent revenue decline. Deploying every dollar of proceeds into a down payment leaves two properties with no buffer instead of one with a buffer.

Does a cash-out refinance trigger taxes?

Refinancing is generally not a taxable event, which is why it is attractive relative to selling. Whether interest is deductible can depend on how the proceeds are used, which is a tracing question to raise with your CPA rather than assume.

Does refinancing increase my depreciation deduction?

No. Basis is driven by the purchase price and improvements rather than by the loan, so a cash-out refinance does not create new depreciation to claim. The proceeds can fund another property, which has its own basis and its own depreciation.

How fast should I scale using refinancing?

Portfolios that hold up generally add one to two properties a year, with each stabilized through a documented full season before the next acquisition and reserves maintained at every property. Cash-out refinancing fits that pace and fits an eighteen month sprint badly.

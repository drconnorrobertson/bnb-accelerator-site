BNB Accelerator vs Doing It Yourself

Published August 10, 2026·9 min read

Doing it yourself is the default option and, for a meaningful share of people, the right one. This page is an accounting of what it actually costs in hours and in risk, so you can make the call with real numbers rather than a vague sense that you probably could handle it.

What your hours are worth

Run the arithmetic honestly. A professional whose time bills at $400 an hour and who spends 200 hours on an acquisition has spent $80,000 of economic value, most of it taken out of evenings and weekends rather than billable time, which is a different kind of cost but a real one.

That comparison is not the strongest argument for using a service, though. The stronger one is variance.

The risks you cannot see from a listing page

Time is recoverable. A bad purchase is not. The failure modes we see most often in properties bought independently:

  • A rental cap buried in the covenants. HOA documents run hundreds of pages and the restriction is rarely on page one. Buyers discover it after closing.
  • A municipality mid-process on permit restrictions. Current rules are public. Pending ordinances and moratoriums require someone tracking council agendas.
  • Revenue projections built on peak season. Annualizing three strong months produces a number that will never repeat.
  • Ignoring the average stay length. A market that fills winter with thirty day bookings can quietly break the seven day average that the entire tax position depends on. See the seven day rule explained.
  • Underbudgeting furnishing. Furnishing a five bedroom cabin properly is not a $20,000 exercise, and thin furnishing shows up directly in review scores. See how to furnish an Airbnb.

One bad acquisition costs more than the fee on several good ones. That is the entire economic argument.

When DIY is genuinely the better choice

We turn people away for these reasons regularly.

  • You have 150 to 300 hours available over the next six months and you want them back in the form of a permanent skill.
  • You are buying in a market you already know deeply, ideally one you visit several times a year.
  • Your available capital is tight enough that a service fee changes whether the deal closes at all.
  • You enjoy this. Some people find deal analysis genuinely fun, and outsourcing the fun part is a strange trade.

Frequently asked questions

How many hours does it take to buy a short-term rental yourself?

From a standing start to a live listing, a first independent purchase typically consumes 150 to 300 hours across market research, regulation verification, deal screening, underwriting, tours, negotiation, transaction coordination, furnishing, and listing launch. Elapsed time is commonly four to eight months.

Is a done-for-you acquisition service worth the fee?

It depends on whether your constraint is time or capital. The strongest argument is not the hours saved but the variance avoided: a single bad purchase, such as one blocked by an HOA rental cap or built on peak season revenue projections, costs far more than the fee on several good ones.

When should I buy a short-term rental on my own?

When you have the hours available and want the skill permanently, when you already know the market deeply, when the service fee would meaningfully change whether the deal closes, or when you genuinely enjoy deal analysis.

What do independent buyers get wrong most often?

The four recurring failures are missing an HOA rental cap buried in the covenants, missing pending municipal permit restrictions, annualizing peak season revenue, and ignoring average stay length, which can break the seven day test the short-term rental tax position depends on.

What is RevPAR? Definition, formula and calculator

Published August 15, 2026·5 min read

RevPAR means revenue per available room, or per available rental night for a single short-term rental. Divide lodging revenue by available nights, or multiply average daily rate (ADR) by occupancy expressed as a decimal. It combines rate and occupancy but does not measure operating profit.

Calculate RevPAR from a revenue statement

Enter lodging revenue only, the nights booked and the nights available for the same period. Defaults are a hypothetical 30-night example, not a market benchmark.




Example: $4,200 ÷ 30 available nights = $140 RevPAR; $4,200 ÷ 21 booked nights = $200 ADR; 21 ÷ 30 = 70% occupancy.

Which revenue belongs in the numerator?

Use a consistent lodging-revenue basis. Do not silently combine nightly accommodation revenue with guest-paid taxes, cleaning charges or deposits. If a statement reports total guest payments, reconcile those components first. Record whether the figure is before or after platform fees and refunds so comparisons use the same basis.

What counts as an available night?

Document the denominator. For an operating-performance calculation, use the nights genuinely available for rental and record nights excluded for owner use or maintenance. For a purchase review, also test revenue over the full calendar period. Reducing the denominator can raise reported RevPAR without adding revenue or reducing fixed carrying costs.

Example: a blocked calendar changes the apparent result

A hypothetical property earns $4,200 on 21 booked nights in a 30-night month. With 30 available nights it reports $140 RevPAR. If six unbooked nights are excluded as owner use, the denominator becomes 24 and reported RevPAR rises to $175. The owner still received the same lodging revenue. Label both calculations; do not compare the 24-night result with another property's full-calendar result.

Can I average monthly RevPAR?

Weight periods by available nights. Suppose a property earns $3,100 across 31 available nights and $5,600 across 28. Combined RevPAR is $8,700 ÷ 59 = $147.46. The simple average of monthly results ($100 and $200) is $150 and overstates the combined figure. Apply the same reconciliation across multiple units using available room-nights.

How should a buyer use RevPAR?

Compare similar property types over the same season with the same revenue and availability definitions. Investigate whether a stronger result comes from rate, occupancy or calendar exclusions. Then build the full expense and financing model. RevPAR alone cannot establish a purchase price, reserve requirement or cash-on-cash return.

Two equivalent formulas

RevPAR = ADR × occupancy, or RevPAR = total revenue ÷ nights available

Both give the same answer. The first is easier when you have market data; the second is easier from your own statements.

Property A looks far stronger on rate and is very slightly weaker on the number that pays the mortgage. RevPAR exposes that in one figure. It also has a second advantage: A has fewer booked nights, but turnover frequency also depends on length of stay. Without booking counts and expense records, neither operating cost nor net cash flow can be inferred. Revenue metrics rank properties; only a full expense build tells you which one to buy.

What RevPAR does not tell you

  • Nothing about cost. Two identical RevPAR figures can produce very different cash flow depending on turnover frequency, cleaning cost, and management rate.
  • Nothing about seasonality. An annual RevPAR hides whether the year is evenly earned or concentrated into four months, which determines your reserve requirement.
  • Nothing about capital. RevPAR ignores purchase price entirely. A property with excellent RevPAR bought too expensively is still a bad investment.

Frequently asked questions

How do you calculate RevPAR for a short-term rental?

Multiply average daily rate by occupancy, or divide total accommodation revenue by the number of nights the property was available for booking. Both methods produce the same figure. Record owner-use and maintenance exclusions explicitly. Excluding unbooked nights raises reported RevPAR without adding revenue; also show revenue across the full calendar period for a purchase review.

Is RevPAR better than ADR?

For comparing properties, yes, because RevPAR accounts for empty nights while ADR ignores them. ADR remains useful for judging pricing power specifically, particularly when compared season by season against local comparables.

What is a good RevPAR?

It is market-dependent and only meaningful relative to comparable properties in the same submarket and period. A strong RevPAR in a low-rate market can be far below a weak RevPAR in a premium one.

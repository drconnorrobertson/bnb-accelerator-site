Price the operating agreement and preserve access before choosing a connected acquisition path.

Turn the proposal into a decision file

This guide is for a buyer already considering BNB Turnkey. It focuses on questions to resolve in the agreement, rather than ranking providers. BNB Turnkey publishes property research, evaluation, inspection and negotiation, design and furnishing, and a management path. Its pre-call material names a management partner. Verify the legal entities and whether that manager is required in your proposal.

Keep a four-column file: requested record, current answer, responsible person and deadline. An unanswered material question is a decision to defer, not a reason to invent a favorable assumption. Review the actual contract with the appropriate professional before funding.

Which company manages the property?

Get the legal name and local contact from the actual agreement. Confirm market availability, authority over pricing and vendor selection, and emergency coverage. A named partner in marketing does not establish your contract.

What is the fee charged on?

Request gross versus net definitions, cleaning-fee treatment, minimum charges, onboarding, supplies, maintenance markups and excluded services. Use the same revenue case when comparing a separate operator.

Who owns accounts and records?

Document listing control, access to booking and payout records, photography rights and software subscriptions. Confirm what remains accessible after termination and who handles existing guests during transfer.

How do I change operators?

Read notice periods, termination charges, booking obligations and vendor handoff duties. Test a hypothetical transition six months after opening before committing to the purchase-and-management combination.

Use a missing-answer test

Hypothetical example: the provider has explained its search or participation process, but has not documented who pays an unexpected $12,000 property cost. Keep that item unresolved until the governing agreement or a written scope revision identifies the responsible party and your exposure. Re-run available cash with the actual obligation; do not treat the brochure’s initial amount as a cap.

Proceed when material rights, duties and costs are documented and fit your plan. Request amendments for vague exclusions. Delay funding or an offer when a missing answer can change control, affordability or legal use. A favorable case study cannot substitute for your agreement.

Original one-address management acceptance sample

Before relying on a connected operator, give the proposed manager the actual candidate address, intended use, closing date and opening work still pending. Request a dated answer identifying the buyer and contracting entity, included first services, excluded work, access dependencies, decision authority and fee triggers. Record which answers remain conditional. A vendor introduction or project update is not the manager's acceptance of a ready-to-operate property.

Invented schedule only: a purchase decision is due on day10, the manager offers a proposal on day12, and the buyer hopes to close on day20. The later proposal does not resolve the day10 question or extend protection. Ask the transaction adviser before the real deadline how to address missing decisive evidence under the actual agreement. A future service date also does not prove insurance, permissions, completed installation or lawful booking procedures. Keep unresolved dependencies visible rather than calling the property turnkey.

Worked fee-base comparison before choosing the operator

All amounts and percentages are invented, not BNB Turnkey or Home Team quotes, industry averages or client results. Suppose the same modeled year contains $80,000 lodging revenue and $12,000 cleaning charges. For hypothetical proposal A, a 20% fee applies only to lodging: $16,000. For hypothetical proposal B, the same rate applies to lodging plus cleaning: $18,400. The $2,400 difference comes from the fee base, not a different rate or independently demonstrated service quality.

Separately itemize cleaning labor, supplies, maintenance, payment charges, onboarding and minimums under each actual proposal. Do not subtract the $12,000 cleaning charges twice or assume charges collected from guests equal the cleaning bill. A base comparison alone does not establish total owner cash or which manager is cheaper once exclusions and service coverage differ. Ask for a sample invoice calculated from the same inputs, then replace the illustration with accepted buyer terms.

Opening cash when a manager deposit is not freely available

Separate hypothetical cash exercise: the buyer has $300,000 accessible capital. The complete acquisition-and-launch allocation is $278,000, including a retained $45,000 operating reserve; $22,000 remains outside those commitments. Suppose additional manager onboarding is $4,000 and a distinct $6,000 manager-held deposit is required. Total committed capital becomes $288,000, leaving $12,000. The deposit is not automatically an expense, but it is not freely available buyer cash while held.

A distinct $9,000 access-and-transition bill then raises commitments to $297,000 and leaves $3,000. If a further, previously excluded $7,000 opening obligation becomes payable, commitments reach $304,000: a $4,000 gap without consuming the retained reserve. This is not a reserve-adequacy recommendation. Count every bill once and establish payment dates. A hoped-for release of the $6,000 deposit does not cure the gap unless entitlement, release conditions and actual availability are documented; this example promises no refund right or timing.

For alternative operating arrangements, use the existing retain-versus-replace purchase analysis. The manager consent and assignment worksheet covers buying an operating property with an existing agreement. Those different purchase questions do not disappear because an acquisition provider introduces an operator. Use the responsibility matrix to define what BNB Accelerator or another retained team actually coordinates, and keep professional approvals separate.

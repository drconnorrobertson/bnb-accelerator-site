Short-Term Rental Furnishing and Design Guide

Published August 15, 2026·9 min read

Furnishing a short-term rental is a revenue decision, not a decorating one. Sleeping capacity, the amenities guests filter on, and photograph quality determine both nightly rate and search placement, which means the furnishing budget buys visibility before it buys taste. Plan on $20,000 to $45,000 for a three to five bedroom property.

The process, step by step

  1. 1. Set the capacity target first

    Decide sleeping capacity before you buy a single item, because capacity drives rate and search placement more than finish level does. Count real beds, not sofa sleepers, and match the local median investment product plus one.

  2. 2. Budget by room, not by shopping trip

    Allocate roughly 45% to bedrooms and beds, 20% to living and dining, 15% to outdoor and amenity, 10% to kitchen and supplies, and 10% contingency. A 3 to 5 bedroom property typically runs $20,000 to $45,000 all in.

  3. 3. Buy the filter amenities before the decor

    Hot tub, pool heat, fast wifi, and pet accommodation are search filters that gate visibility. A beautiful property missing a filter guests apply is invisible to them, which no amount of styling fixes.

  4. 4. Choose commercial-grade where guests touch it

    Mattresses, sofas, dining chairs, and outdoor furniture take hotel-level abuse. Residential-grade versions of these fail within eighteen months and the replacement cost exceeds the original saving.

  5. 5. Design one photograph-defining space

    Guests scroll a grid of thumbnails. One memorable, well-lit focal space, a bunk room, a view-facing living area, a distinctive outdoor setup, does more for bookings than uniformly tasteful furnishing throughout.

  6. 6. Standardise linens and consumables

    Single colour, single size, three sets per bed. Turnover speed and cleaner error rate both improve, and replacement stops requiring decisions.

  7. 7. Photograph professionally after staging

    Book the photographer for a bright day, stage every room, and shoot twilight exteriors if the property has an outdoor amenity. This is the highest-return few hundred dollars in the entire setup.

  8. 8. Leave a defect budget for the first 90 days

    Something always fails in the first two months of real use. Hold roughly 10% of the furnishing budget back rather than spending it on a final flourish.

The four expensive furnishing mistakes

  • Furnishing to your own taste. You are not the guest. A neutral, durable, well-lit property that photographs cleanly outperforms a property that reflects the owner's preferences.
  • Underbuying beds to protect the aesthetic. In leisure markets, capacity is revenue. A bunk room is not elegant and it books.
  • Residential-grade furniture in high-contact positions. Sofas and mattresses in a short-term rental take hotel-level use on residential-grade construction.
  • Skipping professional photography. The listing photo set is the product as far as the booking decision is concerned.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to furnish an Airbnb?

Typically $20,000 to $45,000 for a three to five bedroom property, depending on size, market positioning, and how much you source yourself. Budget roughly 45 percent to bedrooms and beds, since sleeping capacity drives rate more than any other furnishing decision.

What furniture should be commercial-grade in a short-term rental?

Anything guests sit or sleep on: mattresses, sofas, dining chairs, and outdoor furniture. These take hotel-level use, and residential-grade versions commonly fail within eighteen months, at which point replacement costs more than the original saving.

Do I need a professional photographer for my Airbnb?

Yes. The photo set is effectively the product at the point of booking. Professional photography after full staging is among the highest-return expenditures in the entire setup, typically a few hundred dollars against a direct effect on booking rate.

What amenities increase Airbnb revenue the most?

The ones that function as search filters: hot tub, pool, fast wifi, and pet friendly. Missing a filter that a guest applies removes you from their results entirely, which is a visibility problem rather than a pricing problem.

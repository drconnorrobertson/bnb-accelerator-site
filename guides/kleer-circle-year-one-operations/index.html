Turn the proposal into a decision file

This guide is for a buyer already considering Kleer Circle. It focuses on questions to resolve in the agreement, rather than ranking providers. Kleer Circle describes market selection, underwritten deal rounds, transaction coordination, design and listing setup, and a year-one self-operation playbook. Its FAQ describes management introductions later. Confirm any exception in writing before deciding.

Keep a four-column file: requested record, current answer, responsible person and deadline. An unanswered material question is a decision to defer, not a reason to invent a favorable assumption. Review the actual contract with the appropriate professional before funding.

What work stays with me?

Ask for the daily and weekly duty list behind the operating playbook: guest responses, cleaners, repairs, pricing and bookkeeping. Identify service targets and what the systems automate versus what a person must decide.

Who covers nights and holidays?

Name a local primary and backup for lockouts, heating failures and cleaning issues. Get actual availability and prices. A configured messaging system cannot itself repair a failed heater.

What does self-operation really cost?

Budget software, cleaning quality checks, maintenance dispatch, backup coverage and buyer time. Compare that case with a written manager quote. Keep labor assumptions separate from provider fees.

Can I change the year-one model?

The public FAQ describes first-year buyer operation and management introductions later. Ask for any exception in writing, who approves it, which support changes and what additional costs apply. Ask your own CPA how the actual work pattern affects your situation.

Use a missing-answer test

Hypothetical example: the provider has explained its search or participation process, but has not documented who pays an unexpected $12,000 property cost. Keep that item unresolved until the governing agreement or a written scope revision identifies the responsible party and your exposure. Re-run available cash with the actual obligation; do not treat the brochure’s initial amount as a cap.

Proceed when material rights, duties and costs are documented and fit your plan. Request amendments for vague exclusions. Delay funding or an offer when a missing answer can change control, affordability or legal use. A favorable case study cannot substitute for your agreement.

What the current public offer establishes—and what it does not

Kleer Circle's public service page and management FAQ, reviewed October 7, 2026, describe acquisition and launch coordination, a self-management playbook in year one, configured messaging and pricing tools, and later management introductions. This is a public-description reading, not firsthand testing or inspection of a signed customer agreement. The description does not establish who accepts emergency responsibility for your candidate property, an agreed response time, a backup's availability or your actual operating costs. Ask for those answers in your own proposal.

Build a duty-and-availability sheet before choosing a property

Use one row per consequential duty, not one row labeled “self-manage.” Start with guest-message triage, physical access problems, cleaning acceptance, repair authorization, pricing decisions and financial record reconciliation. For each, record the trigger, necessary human action, remote or local location, primary person, backup, unavailable periods, spending authority, escalation contact and accepted cost. These are original buyer planning fields, not a claim about Kleer Circle's contracted deliverables.

Separate notification from completion. An automatic access message can send instructions; it does not prove a guest can enter or identify who is allowed to authorize a locksmith. A cleaner may report a fault without accepting repair dispatch. Request explicit acceptance from each proposed support person, including geography, hours, exclusions and how to contact them. Do not invent universal response-time standards or assume a provider referral has accepted the job. For an actual emergency, use appropriate emergency services; a buyer planning exercise is not an emergency procedure.

Original worked coverage-gap test

Invented pre-purchase scenario, not a client result or recommended service standard: buyer A can handle remote decisions except Tuesday and Thursday from 6–10pm. Proposed backup B can cover Tuesday but is also unavailable Thursday. The cleaner accepts cleaning checks but has not accepted access or repair duties. Automated messages remain active. The sheet therefore shows a four-hour Thursday gap for human decisions; it does not show coverage simply because messages are scheduled.

Assume a prospective local support provider offers Thursday coverage for $90 per four-hour window and requires a separate $150 dispatch charge for each accepted callout. For 12 distinct weekly windows and two callouts, the illustrative incremental cost is (12 × $90) + (2 × $150) = $1,380. These are invented quotes, not Kleer Circle prices or market averages. Twelve weeks is a defined planning period, not a full-year budget; repairs, parts, software and other excluded work remain separate. Do not count dispatch twice if your real availability price already includes it.

The gap closes only if the proposed person actually accepts the property, window, duties and authority. A quote without acceptance still leaves it unresolved. If that support arrangement is inconsistent with the actual year-one agreement, ask for written clarification or an approved change before relying on it. If the buyer cannot establish an acceptable plan, reconsider the property, operating arrangement or timing with the retained advisers. This exercise does not assume guests can be displaced, deadlines extended or agreements cancelled without consequences.

Compare operating models on the same purchase decision

Build separate complete budgets for owner operation and a proposed management arrangement using the same address, opening assumptions and period. Under owner operation, distinguish provider acquisition fees from software, local coverage, cleaning, maintenance, recordkeeping and the buyer's remaining duties. Under management, request the calculation base, minimums, onboarding, pass-through costs, excluded work and retained owner duties. Mark unknowns rather than silently assigning them zero. A lower known subtotal cannot settle which arrangement costs less when material obligations are missing.

Keep time feasibility separate from tax suitability. Being available for four hours, paying a backup, or completing this sheet is not four hours of qualifying participation. Do not count another person's work as your own or assume a self-management label guarantees a deduction. Give your own qualified tax adviser accurate actual work records and the proposed operating structure. IRS Publication 925, reviewed October 7, 2026, discusses evidence of services performed and approximate hours, not this availability worksheet as an eligibility test. This guide makes no eligibility, tax-savings or investment-return conclusion.

Get a decision-ready exception, not an informal reassurance

If you require full management immediately, state that before paying for an acquisition program or relying on a candidate. Request the actual approver, allowed arrangement, effective date, affected services, added fees and continuing buyer duties in writing. An introduction promised later is not approval today.

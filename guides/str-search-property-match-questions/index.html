STR Search Property Match: Questions Before Signing

Before signing an STR Search proposal, define match acceptance, model revisions, payment triggers and the cash needed for unresolved work.

Discuss My First STRCheck My Purchase Cash

Turn the proposal into a decision file

This guide is for a buyer already considering STR Search. It focuses on questions to resolve in the agreement, rather than ranking providers. STR Search markets property matching, data analysis and the Bianchi Method. Its public site distinguishes analysis from a realtor’s transaction role. Confirm exactly which closing, furnishing and operating tasks your proposed tier includes.

Keep a four-column file: requested record, current answer, responsible person and deadline. An unanswered material question is a decision to defer, not a reason to invent a favorable assumption. Review the actual contract with the appropriate professional before funding.

What counts as a match?

Write down budget, market, property type, financing, legal use and acceptable downside. Ask what milestone satisfies the service: delivery of a listing, buyer acceptance, an offer or a closing. Confirm conditions and any remedy in the agreement.

What happens when a matched house fails diligence?

Request rejected-deal examples and rules for a property that fails insurance, inspections, permit checks or lender approval. Ask whether another search is included and whether the service period or fee changes.

Can I recreate the revenue model?

Ask for the comparable set, monthly seasonality, adjustments, expense base and sensitivity case. Treat a model as an estimate and connect important seller claims to records. Ask who revises the model when quotes or inspection findings change.

Who owns closing and launch?

Assign representation, inspection decisions, contingency deadlines, furnishing orders and opening readiness to named parties. Identify included coordination versus introductions. A matching service need not cover all of those tasks.

Read the promise and the paid agreement separately

The STR Search homepage currently advertises a time-bound matching promise while its investment disclaimer says financial performance is not guaranteed. That distinction matters: a service milestone, a successful closing and an investment result are different events. Record the exact milestone, starting event, buyer-response requirements and any remedy in your proposed engagement. This guide does not establish that a refund or a profitable property is owed.

The linked website Terms & Conditions describe access to its websites and informational services. We have not authenticated your paid-client agreement. Do not substitute those public terms for the document naming your service tier, contracting entity, payment schedule, exclusions and dispute process. Ask the provider which documents govern, and have your lawyer resolve any inconsistent promise before signing. These are purchase-review questions, not a legal opinion about enforceability.

Original acceptance work sample: a candidate is not an approval

Hypothetical work sample, not an actual provider file or client outcome: a buyer's screening brief requires a financeable house, a documented path to lawful STR use and a complete cash plan. Candidate version 1 fits the asking-price limit. The model uses an estimated insurance allowance, but no address-specific quote has arrived. The permit information is a seller statement rather than a response from the relevant authority. Mark the candidate “received; insurance and permitted use unresolved,” not “approved to buy.”

Ask the matching team to identify the model version and unresolved assumptions. Ask the licensed transaction professional who may communicate an authorized offer and track its deadlines. Ask the relevant insurance and permitting professionals for the missing evidence. These requests do not automatically create a contractual right to delay, cancel or extend anything. If an offer is already signed, obtain advice about its actual conditions and notice deadlines; an internal spreadsheet flag is not a contract notice.

When the quote or permit response arrives, record the date, source and resulting decision. If the model changes, preserve both versions and the reason rather than overwriting the original conclusion. The buyer can then distinguish a revised economic choice from a service-delivery dispute. For a reproducible comp selection file, use the comparable-set guide; for named handoffs, use the acquisition responsibility matrix. Use a missing-answer test

Hypothetical capital worksheet only; no STR Search or BNB price is implied. Suppose $300,000 is accessible for the purchase. Allocate $160,000 to the down payment, $18,000 to closing costs, $12,000 to an invented service allowance, $30,000 to furnishing, $15,000 to known repairs and $40,000 to a retained operating reserve. The complete allocation is $275,000, leaving $25,000 of unallocated margin. The reserve remains cash reserved for the investment; it is counted once, not expensed and subtracted again.

The provider has discussed the process, but responsibility for an additional $12,000 opening task is unresolved. If your signed scope already includes that same task inside an existing allowance, do not add it again. If it is a distinct buyer-paid obligation, total allocation becomes $287,000 and margin falls to $13,000. Do not assume an introduction includes vendor labor or treat an expected credit as available cash without written confirmation.

Now consider a separate $25,000 repair discovered later. If both additional costs apply, allocation rises to $312,000, creating a $12,000 funding shortfall. If only the new repair applies, the $300,000 allocation uses all initially unallocated margin while keeping the stated reserve. Neither outcome establishes adequate reserves or an acceptable investment; replace every assumption with actual quotes, financing terms and your own requirements. Do not use an expected tax refund to close the gap.

For every unresolved cost, record the task, actual payer, payment date, amount or range, evidence and whether it is already included elsewhere. “Unknown” is a valid entry. Proceed only when material rights, duties and costs are documented and fit your plan; request a scope revision or defer commitment when the unanswered item changes affordability or control.

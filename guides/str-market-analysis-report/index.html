Short-Term Rental Market Analysis Guide

Use this market analysis outline to prepare comparable revenue, occupancy, seasonality, reserves and regulatory questions for your target property. Book a call to discuss a tailored analysis and confirm its scope.

What to include in a market analysis

  1. A comparable set, named. Identify the actual listings, covered periods, availability and inclusion or exclusion reasons. No fixed count establishes adequacy; explain relevance, independent assets, coverage and remaining uncertainty.
  2. Estimated ADR and occupancy for the property profile you describe, with field definitions and supported ranges. A narrow range or median alone does not establish forecast reliability.
  3. Seasonality shape month by month, and the reserve the trough implies.
  4. The regulatory picture as it currently stands, including whether permits are capped and whether the trend is tightening.
  5. An honest verdict. State which assumptions are supported, which remain uncertain and what would make the property unsuitable.

What it is not

This market-level outline is not an address-specific projection or an appraisal. It narrows the search; the purchase decision also needs property-specific underwriting, permission, condition, costs, financing and funded opening cash. An analytics product may offer an address projection, but that does not turn this outline into a completed acquisition decision.

Review a report before it becomes an offer assumption

For a buyer planning an acquisition within zero to six months, the useful output is a dated decision file, not a persuasive average. Name the target market, intended property profile, planned operating calendar and decision deadline. Ask the preparer which answers are modeled, supported by authorized records, observed but unexplained, or missing. A large tax bill does not resolve an unsupported revenue assumption.

AirDNA's Rentalizer documentation, dated June 19, 2026 and reviewed October 7, describes address projections, editable comparable sets and monthly ranges. Its projection assumes 365-day availability and includes cleaning fees. Record those definitions before comparing it with an opening-year or lodging-only model. This is one documented product example, not a test of forecast accuracy or a claim that all services use the same methodology.

Worked opening-window comparison

Invented arithmetic only, not a market forecast or client result: A report's annual lodging-only model is $72,000, including $18,000 for a three-month opening window. The buyer's separate opening plan makes that entire window unavailable. Simply copying $72,000 into the intended-use case overstates the modeled available period by $18,000. Removing that window leaves $54,000 of modeled lodging revenue, not confirmed bookings or an independently validated forecast.

To isolate this one change, suppose distinct variable cash costs equal 25% of lodging revenue. Modeled variable costs fall from $18,000 to $13,500; the contribution difference is therefore $13,500, not $18,000. All other costs are held constant only for this exercise. Fixed bills, debt, opening work and actual receipt timing require their own evidence. Do not deduct variable costs twice or claim the exercise calculates net owner cash, an adequate reserve or a tax benefit.

If the original purchase case depended on that unavailable window, request a revised dated model and complete cash schedule before relying on it. A seasonal window should not be replaced by a flat one-quarter subtraction in a real deal without month-specific support. The exact lost opportunity may be unknown; preserve that uncertainty rather than inventing replacement demand.

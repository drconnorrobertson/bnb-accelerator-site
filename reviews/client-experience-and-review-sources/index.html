Use original public reviews, direct client stories and property records to assess the acquisition experience and ask better reference questions.

Read the original post and any company response before relying on an editorial summary. A star rating records a reviewer’s opinion; it does not independently verify property income, legal compliance or the service agreement.

Match the review to the stage of service

A comment about a helpful conversation answers a different question from a client’s report after purchase and a full operating year. Look for the stage described, the work actually performed and any specific communication or handoff issue. Some brief public comments provide no purchase details. Do not assume that every positive rating represents a completed acquisition.

Include critical and mixed feedback in your questions

The existing directory includes critical and mixed feedback, including questions about fees, transaction communication, tax/entity guidance and comparables. These are topics to examine through original posts, responses and your own documents; the directory does not adjudicate disputed claims. Ask for the fee schedule, representation boundaries, underwriting memo and responsibility matrix before signing. A useful answer identifies the contract clause or source record rather than asking you to rely on a testimonial.

Ask references questions you can compare

Ask what service they purchased, when they closed, what work the team performed and what they arranged separately. Ask about the original budget, additional cash needs, communication frequency and what happened when a task slipped. For financial performance, ask what period the client can document and whether the records include all expenses and financing. Respect the client’s privacy and accept a redacted record rather than requesting personal tax returns.

BNB Accelerator cash flow and returns: how to verify the figures

Check reporting periods, operating expenses, debt service and invested cash before using a published STR return percentage.

Published October 8, 2026 · Company evidence review

Start with the period and definition

Cash flow needs a reporting window and a definition. Ask whether it includes collected rental revenue, cleaning income and refunds; whether operating costs include management, maintenance and replacements; and whether debt service includes principal and interest. A property’s operating income before financing and an owner’s cash flow after financing answer different questions. Do not put them in the same comparison column.

Check the denominator before using a percentage

Cash-on-cash return ordinarily compares a defined cash-flow amount with a defined cash-investment amount for the same analysis. The published tracker percentages do not consistently reconcile with the displayed entry-cost fields. Recomputing the fraction can identify a question, but it cannot establish which denominator was intended or whether the numerator was actually earned. Keep the source fields and request the investment ledger rather than presenting a replacement percentage as a verified client result.

A practical reconciliation sequence

First obtain the property-level booking and payout records for the period. Match gross revenue, refunds, platform charges and net receipts, taking payout timing into account. Then match the operating costs and debt service to the same period. Finally reconcile cash invested, including the treatment of improvements, credits, reserves and later contributions. Note whether owner use or downtime reduced available nights. Each unresolved item should remain visible until a source document explains it.

Keep appreciation, tax and operating cash separate

Appreciation is not operating cash and generally becomes spendable only through an exit or financing transaction. Debt principal paid down affects equity but is still a cash outflow when paid. A tax deduction is not the same as a dollar-for-dollar refund. Keeping these mechanisms separate makes it easier to judge whether a property can cover expenses during a weak season. An attractive combined benefit does not resolve an operating shortfall.

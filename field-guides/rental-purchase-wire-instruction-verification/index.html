Verify Closing Wire Instructions Through a Trusted Channel

AI-assisted editorial guide · Published November 20, 2026

CFPB guidance warns that mortgage-closing scams can use convincing messages to divert a buyer’s funds. A rental-property buyer should establish verification procedures with trusted transaction professionals before the payment deadline. This guide organizes that conversation; it does not confirm bank details or replace the controls of the bank and settlement provider.

Decision processChecklistOfficial sources

Agree on the process early

Discuss how payment instructions will be delivered and verified with the known settlement professional and bank. Record the trusted contact method through an established channel. Do not wait until an urgent message arrives to decide whom to call. A clear process reduces dependence on the appearance of a last-minute email.

Treat unexpected changes as unresolved

CFPB highlights messages that suggest changes to wiring instructions as a scam risk. Pause and verify through a trusted contact method already established independently of the message. Do not use a new phone number or link supplied in the suspicious communication as the sole basis for confirmation. Keep the change unresolved until the appropriate professionals address it.

Protect transaction information

Limit access to closing documents and avoid sending sensitive banking details through casual shared folders or group messages. Use the secure methods required by the bank and settlement provider. Confirm the intended recipient before sharing information. A payment checklist should record that verification occurred without becoming an unnecessary repository of account secrets.

Separate authorization from preparation

Decide who may prepare the payment and who must approve it under the buyer’s process. The person reviewing instructions should understand the trusted-channel requirement. A second signature is not meaningful if both reviewers rely on the same unverified message. Ask the bank about its own controls and follow its instructions.

Keep evidence of the completed checks

Record the date, trusted contact and completion of required verification steps. Retain appropriate transaction confirmations through secure channels. Do not infer that a sent wire has reached the intended recipient until the relevant professional confirms receipt. Escalate unexpected discrepancies immediately rather than assuming a processing delay.

Know the urgent response route

CFPB advises contacting the bank or wire-transfer company immediately if funds may have been sent to a scammer and reporting through appropriate official channels. Prepare those contacts in advance. For a hypothetical late change request, the useful outcome is a paused payment and direct verification, not a rushed attempt to satisfy an email’s claimed deadline. Preserve suspicious communications for the bank and relevant authorities through the channels they request, without forwarding sensitive details unnecessarily.

Prepare the evidence and next action

  • Agreed secure delivery and verification process
  • Independently established trusted contact
  • Unexpected changes paused for verification
  • Clear preparation and approval roles
  • Bank and official reporting contacts

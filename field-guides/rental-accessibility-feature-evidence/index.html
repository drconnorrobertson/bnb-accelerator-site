Document Rental Accessibility Features Before Listing Them

AI-assisted editorial guide · Published November 20, 2026

A guest may rely on a specific accessibility feature when deciding whether a property will work for them. Airbnb provides guidance on photographing such features and reviewing the submitted evidence. Use precise descriptions supported by the actual property. A general label such as accessible is not a substitute for documenting individual features or obtaining professional advice about applicable requirements.

Separate observed features from assumptions

List the features the owner intends to describe and identify what is actually known. A step-free entrance, doorway width and bathroom feature are different questions. Do not infer one from another. Ask a qualified assessor or relevant professional when a feature requires technical evaluation, and avoid claiming that an informal checklist establishes legal compliance.

Use the platform’s current definitions

Review the official requirements for each feature on the platform where it will be listed. Airbnb’s resource explains photographs and, for certain features, visible measurements. Follow the current instructions rather than reusing a screenshot from an older listing. A feature accepted on one service may not use the same description or evidence standard on another.

Prepare clear measurement records

Where a measurement is relevant, record what was measured, the method and who completed it. The platform’s photo guidance includes showing the feature and readable measurement evidence. Keep the original image with its context. Do not crop away a relevant obstruction or choose an angle that makes the usable space appear larger than it is.

Check the complete route

A feature inside the property may be unusable if the route to it differs from the description. Document the relevant approach and connections without pretending that this replaces a professional accessibility review. Identify temporary obstacles, furnishings or seasonal conditions that affect what a guest will encounter. Keep those uncertainties out of an unqualified promotional claim.

Review changes after furnishing

New furniture, thresholds, locks or bathroom accessories can affect an earlier description. Assign a person to review feature records after material changes. Update the listing through the platform’s process and retain the supporting photographs. If a guest asks a specific question that the records do not answer, obtain an accurate response rather than extrapolating from a broad label.

Use a concrete guest-question example

Suppose a guest asks about a doorway shown in the listing. The operating team should be able to locate the current measurement and photograph, explain what they show and identify any limits. If the layout has changed, the old image should not be treated as current evidence. This approach supports an informed guest decision without promising suitability for every person.

Prepare the evidence and next action

  • Feature-specific definitions
  • Current measurements and context
  • Clear original photographs
  • Route and obstruction review
  • Recheck after property changes

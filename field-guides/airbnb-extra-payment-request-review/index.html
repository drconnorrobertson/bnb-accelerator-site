Review an Extra Payment Request on an Airbnb Booking

AI-assisted editorial guide · Published November 20, 2026

An operator can create a dispute by requesting an additional payment without first identifying what the charge represents. Airbnb’s payment rules distinguish mandatory fees, optional services and specific exceptions. A guest’s willingness to pay does not by itself establish that an external collection method is permitted. Use a short review record before sending a request or responding to one from a service provider.

Describe the charge in plain language

Record the service, amount, currency, timing and reason for the proposed payment. Identify whether it is necessary to access what was booked or an optional service the guest freely chose. Do not rename a mandatory charge as optional because it was omitted from the original quote. Keep the reservation and original price breakdown linked to the review.

Check disclosure and platform rules

Airbnb’s current policy requires mandatory fees to be disclosed before booking and generally keeps booking-related payments on the platform. It describes limited exceptions and permits certain optional fees offline by agreement. Read the applicable language for the actual account and charge. Do not treat one exception as permission for unrelated fees or future off-platform reservations.

Identify the authorized collection route

Decide whether the amount belongs in a reservation change, another supported platform process or a documented exception. Verify the recipient and payment instructions through established channels. If a contractor asks the guest to pay directly, confirm that the owner authorized the service and that the proposed arrangement fits the platform rules before passing on the request.

Review an optional-service example

Suppose a guest asks for optional pool heating after booking. The operator checks that it was genuinely optional, confirms availability and agrees the price and delivery terms. The operator then uses an allowed payment route and records consent. The example does not authorize a surprise fee for an amenity already included in the booked listing.

Resolve an omitted mandatory fee

If the operator discovers a required charge was missing, pause the request and ask the platform how to handle the specific booking. Preserve the original disclosure rather than editing it and claiming the guest saw the new version. Do not withhold access to force acceptance of an unsupported demand or ask the guest to misstate the payment purpose.

Close with a traceable decision

Save the classification, policy reference, guest agreement where relevant and payment status. Give the next operator a clear note about what was promised and who will deliver it. If the charge is declined or not permitted, record that outcome too. The worksheet helps prevent duplicate or unauthorized requests; it is not a substitute for resolving disputed legal obligations.

Prepare the evidence and next action

  • Charge purpose and mandatory/optional status
  • Original booking price evidence
  • Applicable policy or exception
  • Authorized recipient and collection route
  • Guest consent and delivery terms
  • Final payment or no-charge outcome

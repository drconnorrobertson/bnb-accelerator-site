Design iGMS Team Roles and Property Access

AI-assisted editorial guide · Published November 20, 2026

iGMS documents roles, permissions and property access as related but distinct parts of team setup. An operator should define the work first, then configure and test the appropriate access. A person invited to the account should not automatically receive visibility into every property or financial function. Use the current product documentation for the actual role being assigned.

Decision processChecklistOfficial sources

Describe the person’s work

List the tasks the team member must perform and the properties involved. Distinguish a cleaner, supervisor, owner and reservation operator rather than giving everyone a broad manager label. Identify whether the person needs to view information, change it or approve a decision. This business description should drive the account configuration.

Review the role and permissions separately

The iGMS guidance describes preset roles and additional permission choices. Check what the proposed role allows and whether changes are needed for the actual assignment. Do not assume a familiar role name has the same meaning as in another software product. Keep an internal record of the intended boundaries.

Assign the correct property scope

Review the property-access settings independently of feature permissions. A cleaner may need access to certain properties without access to every reservation or financial record. Confirm the actual assignments before the invitation is used. Avoid exposing additional client properties simply because a broad account default is convenient.

Use an authorized task test

Have the team member confirm that they can see and complete a representative permitted task. Check that unnecessary information is not visible through the intended workflow. Use approved test methods and protect guest data. Record any access problem and resolve it through the responsible administrator rather than sharing another person’s login.

Define invitation and removal authority

iGMS describes who can invite team members and the scope of some roles’ invitation rights. Match that capability with the business’s authorization process. When staff leave or change assignments, review both feature access and property scope. Removing one task assignment should not be assumed to close every account-level permission.

Review a seasonal-team example

Suppose a cleaner joins for a subset of properties during a busy period. Configure the required role and properties, test the workflow and set a review date for the temporary assignment. At the end, confirm the intended access has been removed. Keep the process specific to the person’s work rather than relying on a shared generic account. Review access again when the team member begins working at a different property or takes on a supervisory role. A change in job title does not automatically establish the right software scope. Record who approved the revised access, what was tested and when the temporary or expanded permissions should next be reviewed.

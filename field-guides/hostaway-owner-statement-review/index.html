Review Hostaway Owner-Statement Delivery and Allocations

AI-assisted editorial guide · Published November 20, 2026

Hostaway’s owner-statement documentation describes report configuration, expenses and access controlled by the property manager. An owner receiving a report and a manager configuring it have different tasks. Use the actual management agreement and source records to evaluate the statement. Software output does not independently verify the economic result or replace professional accounting review.

Decision processChecklistOfficial sources

Identify the report’s purpose

State whether the statement explains a payment to the owner, an invoice from the manager or another agreed reporting outcome. Record the period and properties included. A report title alone does not establish its accounting basis. Ask the manager to explain the intended calculation before comparing figures with a bank deposit.

Check property and owner associations

Hostaway’s support guidance explains that expense inclusion depends on relevant dates and associations. Review the actual item settings when a cost appears on the wrong report. Do not solve the discrepancy by editing the final total without identifying its source. Keep the supporting expense record and the correction history.

Reconcile representative bookings

Select an ordinary booking and an exception, such as a refund or adjustment. Trace the relevant income, fee and expense lines through the configured statement. Keep the owner’s contract and accounting definitions separate from the software’s field behavior. Where a calculation is disputed, obtain the appropriate professional interpretation.

Confirm what the owner can access

The property manager controls which statements and information are shared. Do not assume an owner login displays every internal figure or that vendor support can override the manager’s access choices. Agree on the report package and delivery method. An owner should know whom to contact about a missing or unclear statement.

Review automation before publication

If statements are generated or distributed automatically, identify who reviews them and what happens when late expenses arrive. Test the process with the actual reporting workflow. A scheduled report is not necessarily final or approved. Preserve the version sent to the owner and explain later revisions through a controlled process.

Use an expense-timing example

Suppose a service invoice arrives after a monthly statement has been prepared. Check its date, owner and property associations, then follow the agreed correction process. Keep the revised report distinguishable from the original. That is more reliable than sending a replacement PDF with the same name and no explanation of the changed amount. For each revised statement, explain whether a booking, expense, allocation or presentation setting changed. That allows the owner to distinguish a correction from a new cash movement. Retain the source evidence and the approval for a material adjustment instead of expecting the recipient to discover the difference by comparing totals alone.

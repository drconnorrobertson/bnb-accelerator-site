Review the Lead-Disclosure File for a Pre-1978 Purchase

AI-assisted editorial guide · Published November 20, 2026

EPA explains federal lead-based paint disclosure requirements for many sales and leases of housing built before 1978. A buyer should ask qualified transaction advisers how the rules apply to the actual property and transaction. This guide organizes records and questions. It does not determine an exemption, establish a property’s condition or replace a lead inspection or risk assessment.

Establish the property facts

Record the property and available construction-date evidence. If the date is uncertain, make that uncertainty explicit. Do not infer age from appearance or treat a listing description as a complete legal record. Give the transaction adviser the facts needed to determine which disclosure requirements and any state or local rules need attention.

Collect the complete disclosure packet

Use EPA’s current guidance and the adviser’s instructions to identify the relevant documents. Preserve the disclosure, provided reports and acknowledgements together. A checked box or brief summary should not replace an attached report. If a referenced document is missing, keep it as an open item rather than assuming it contained no material information.

Distinguish knowledge from testing

A statement that the seller has no knowledge of lead-based paint is different from a qualified finding about the property. Read what the document actually says and ask an appropriate professional to explain its limits. Do not restate an absence of records as a clean inspection. Keep any testing proposal separate from the disclosure itself.

Discuss timing before decisions become fixed

Ask the transaction adviser which review or inspection opportunities apply and how they fit the contract. Do not copy a generic deadline into the transaction calendar without confirmation. If professional assessment is proposed, identify its scope, limitations and delivery date. The buyer needs the result in time to make the relevant decision.

Keep renovation questions separate

Disclosure at purchase and the requirements for later renovation are different subjects. If work is planned, give the qualified contractor the property information and ask which rules apply to that scope. Do not interpret receipt of a disclosure as permission to disturb painted surfaces. Preserve the relevant professional advice with the renovation file.

Use an evidence-based example

Suppose a disclosure refers to an earlier assessment but only the signature page is supplied. Request the complete report and let the appropriate adviser review it before summarizing the issue. Record any unresolved question and the next responsible person. A complete file allows the buyer to understand the available evidence without claiming the documents prove more than they do. Keep the full attachments available to the professional reviewing the transaction.

Prepare the evidence and next action

  • Construction-date evidence
  • Complete disclosure and attachments
  • Testing versus seller knowledge distinguished
  • Confirmed review timing
  • Separate renovation questions

Title Commitment vs Land Survey Before Buying an STR

AI-assisted editorial guide · Published November 20, 2026

A title commitment and a land survey belong in the same purchase file, but they are not interchangeable. One describes the title insurer’s proposed coverage and conditions through the applicable document. The other records the survey work and mapped information within its scope. The useful comparison is how the two providers exchange evidence about the property, not which document can replace the other.

Read the commitment as a conditional document

Ask the title professional to identify requirements, exceptions, proposed insured parties and the legal description. Obtain referenced recorded documents when needed for review. A commitment is not the same document as the final issued policy. Ask what must happen before issuance and which exceptions might remain. Refer questions about legal effect to the appropriate adviser.

Order a survey scope that fits

Tell the surveyor why the work is needed and who requires it. A boundary survey, mortgage-related product and ALTA/NSPS land title survey can have different scopes. Do not demand an ALTA label without discussing the actual transaction. ALTA publishes current land title survey standards and optional requirements; ask the qualified surveyor which work is appropriate.

Exchange the underlying records early

Provide the surveyor with current title evidence and relevant recorded documents, and provide the title team with the survey requested for its review. Confirm the version date in each direction. If a revised commitment arrives after the survey, ask whether it changes the work or title review. A shared address alone does not prove the documents describe identical interests.

Review an access example carefully

In an illustrative transaction, the survey depicts a driveway crossing land outside the parcel. The title file contains a recorded access document. Ask the title adviser and surveyor to reconcile the location, description and scope within their assignments. Do not infer permitted guest parking, commercial access rights or maintenance obligations from a line on a map.

Track exceptions through closing

Maintain a simple issue list showing the title item, the survey reference, the responsible reviewer and the written resolution. If a party offers an endorsement or other coverage change, obtain the exact proposed document and explanation. Do not describe an exception as removed merely because someone said the survey looked acceptable during a call.

Preserve the final set

After closing, save the issued policy, its attachments, the final survey and the documents used to resolve material issues. Future repairs, insurance reviews or sales may require the same evidence. These records address defined title and survey questions; they do not independently establish zoning approval, rental licensing or the absence of every possible property dispute. Include any later correction with the original record so a future reviewer can follow the change without guessing which version governed closing.

Prepare the evidence and next action

  • Current commitment and referenced documents
  • Survey type and intended users
  • Matching legal descriptions and dates
  • Access or encroachment issue log
  • Final issued policy and survey archive

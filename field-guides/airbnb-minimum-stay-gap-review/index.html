Review a Minimum-Stay Exception for Calendar Gaps

AI-assisted editorial guide · Published November 20, 2026

A gap between reservations can remain unbookable because of minimum-stay settings, check-in rules or another restriction. Changing a global minimum to fill one gap can alter many dates unintentionally. Use a date-specific review to identify the active rule, confirm operating readiness and test the intended result. This is a platform configuration worksheet, not advice to bypass a legal minimum-stay requirement.

Decision processChecklistOfficial sources

Define the target gap

Record the exact available dates, adjacent reservations and desired arrival and departure combinations. Check turnover needs, owner blocks and any restrictions imposed outside the platform. An empty calendar cell does not establish that every stay pattern is practical or permitted. Obtain the responsible owner’s decision before changing a setting that expands the kind of booking accepted.

Identify the active rules

Review default minimum and maximum nights, custom check-in-day settings and any applicable rule-set. Airbnb notes that an active rule-set can override existing minimum-night rules. Record the source of the rule you intend to change. A screenshot of one global setting may not explain why a particular arrival date remains unavailable.

Limit the scope of the change

Choose the supported date-specific setting where appropriate and write the affected range before saving. Do not relax a global minimum by habit to solve a narrow gap. If the desired result cannot be configured without broader effects, pause and review the tradeoff. Document any separate owner approval for a broader change.

Use a neighboring-booking example

Suppose two confirmed stays leave a short opening between them. Review the gap together with allowed arrivals, departures and cleaning capacity. Apply the intended rule only after the operating constraints are confirmed. Then test a stay wholly inside the opening and another starting outside it, so an unintended neighboring-date change is more likely to be noticed.

Check the guest-facing result

Review the listing as a guest for the relevant dates and party size without completing a booking. Compare the available stay combinations with the worksheet. If the result differs, recheck rule priority and other restrictions rather than repeatedly lowering limits. Record the test time because later bookings or edits can change availability.

Plan the end of the exception

Identify whether the rule expires automatically or needs a scheduled review. Record the original setting and the person responsible for restoring it if necessary. Keep confirmed bookings intact when changing future availability. A successful configuration check shows that the intended dates are presented correctly; it does not guarantee demand or make an otherwise prohibited stay lawful. Keep the guest-view evidence with the change log and note which dates were tested. A future operator can then repeat the same checks after another rule-set or calendar update.

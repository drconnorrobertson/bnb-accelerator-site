Check the Arrival Guide From a Guest’s Perspective

AI-assisted editorial guide · Published November 20, 2026

Airbnb’s arrival guidance recommends complete directions and clear steps for getting inside, with a preview of what guests see. An operator should check the actual guest-facing sequence after property or access changes. A guide that looks complete in the editor may still be confusing when someone arrives tired, after dark or with limited connectivity.

Decision processChecklistOfficial sources

Map the arrival sequence

List the steps from finding the property to entering the correct door. Separate parking, gates, shared entrances and the final access method. Use the actual property arrangement rather than a generic welcome template. Identify where a guest could reasonably choose the wrong route or be unsure which instruction applies.

Review the guest-facing presentation

Use the platform’s supported preview to inspect the arrival information. Check the order and wording of the steps and whether the photos show the relevant detail. Do not expose private access information in a public listing merely to make it easier to find. Keep public directions and reservation-specific access details appropriately separated.

Use current photographs

Replace images after meaningful changes to signs, locks, doors or parking arrangements. A close-up can explain a mechanism, while a wider photograph helps identify its location. Avoid relying on a decorative image that omits the landmark the instruction references. Record who will maintain the photographs when work is completed at the property.

Plan an approved backup contact

Decide how a guest can reach assistance if the digital guide is unavailable or a step fails. Keep the contact current and assign coverage for the actual arrival window. Do not make a guest depend on an unmonitored personal number. Any alternate access arrangement should follow the property’s security process and the relevant provider’s instructions.

Recheck linked automation

Review scheduled messages and house-manual references after editing the guide. A correct guide can be undermined by a message that describes the previous lock or sends an obsolete link. Assign one owner for the update and ask the team to confirm its related documents. Keep secret credentials out of general revision notes.

Use a changed-gate example

Suppose a contractor replaces a gate latch shortly before a reservation. Update the relevant photograph and instruction, preview the sequence and notify the support team. Confirm any scheduled message still points guests to the correct method. This checks the practical handoff without creating a fake reservation or claiming every guest will arrive without difficulty. Keep the latest approved arrival version identifiable to everyone providing support. That helps a remote co-host describe the same entrance the guest sees instead of relying on a remembered layout from an earlier visit.

Prepare the evidence and next action

  • Step-by-step arrival sequence
  • Supported guest-view preview
  • Current location and mechanism photos
  • Staffed backup contact
  • Related messages checked for conflicts

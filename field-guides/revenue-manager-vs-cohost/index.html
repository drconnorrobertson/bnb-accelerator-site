Revenue Manager vs Co-Host for an STR

AI-assisted editorial guide · Published November 20, 2026

A revenue-management engagement can focus on pricing and availability decisions, while a co-host arrangement may include guest communication, listing work or physical coordination. The names alone do not define either service. Compare written responsibilities, platform permissions and decision rights. A rate strategy is not a promise of bookings, and assigning a co-host does not automatically assign responsibility for every operating task.

Decision processChecklistOfficial sources

Define the pricing mandate

List the channels, properties and settings the revenue provider may change. Cover minimum prices, discounts, stay restrictions and owner-use blocks. Decide which changes need approval and how exceptions are recorded. A provider cannot be evaluated fairly if the owner changes availability or rates independently without sharing the same change history.

Define the hosting mandate

List guest messages, issue escalation, cleaner coordination and on-site tasks separately from pricing. Airbnb explicitly says co-host permissions determine platform access and that hosts should still set expectations about help with the property. Confirm the actual agreement rather than interpreting a full-access account invitation as a complete description of services.

Establish one change authority

Identify the final decision-maker when pricing and operations conflict. A maintenance closure may require blocked dates even if a pricing tool recommends availability. Set the notification route and record who updates each channel. Avoid two providers silently reversing each other’s calendar changes or assuming the other party has communicated a restriction to guests.

Review a worked scheduling example

Suppose a planned repair removes three nights from sale. The co-host confirms the operational block with the owner, and the revenue provider reviews remaining dates under its scope. Neither should reopen the closed nights merely to improve a forecast. The record should distinguish operational unavailability from low demand so later performance analysis does not misread the result.

Choose evidence rather than promises

Ask for a change log, explanation of important assumptions and reports with clearly defined measures. Compare achieved figures with available nights and relevant costs, not only a selected percentage improvement. Separate software fees from service fees and understand the cancellation process. No historical case study establishes what this property will earn under different conditions.

Plan the service exit

Document who owns accounts, exports settings and removes access. Save pricing rules and upcoming booking restrictions before changing providers. Confirm that guest support and emergency responsibilities remain assigned during the handoff. A revenue provider’s final report is not a substitute for a complete operational handover, especially when a co-host continues working on the same listing. Agree on the frequency of pricing reviews and the notice required for a material strategy change. That allows the owner to distinguish an intentional decision from a missed update.

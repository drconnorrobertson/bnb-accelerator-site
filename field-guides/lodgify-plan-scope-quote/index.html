Review a Lodgify Plan Quote and Account Eligibility

AI-assisted editorial guide · Published November 20, 2026

Lodgify’s current pricing page presents multiple plans, optional tools and qualifications that can depend on account timing or country. An older review or booking-fee document may not describe a new account’s offer. Prepare a plan comparison using the exact proposed configuration and retain the terms shown when you make the decision.

Start with the tasks you need

List the channels, website functions, messaging and owner-reporting work required for the operation. Distinguish a host managing one property from a company handling several owners. A plan should be evaluated against those tasks rather than its promotional label. Mark functions that are essential before launch and those that can wait.

Read the current plan boundaries

Use the official pricing page to identify included features and property limits. Check the qualifications attached to a feature or offer. Do not assume a plan description applies to every existing or future account. Ask Lodgify to confirm the terms for your account where the published page presents conditions or alternatives.

Separate promotions from recurring charges

Record the billing term, currency and what happens after any promotional period. Identify optional products and applicable payment-related charges separately from the software subscription. A discount percentage is not a complete cost comparison. Preserve the current proposal and the assumptions used rather than relying on a screenshot of only the headline amount.

Check which work remains yours

A website builder, owner statement feature or automated message does not establish that the vendor will prepare your content, reconcile your accounts or manage the property. Ask about onboarding deliverables and support scope. Confirm who supplies the information, checks the configuration and handles exceptions after launch.

Use a plan-specific demonstration

Choose a practical workflow and ask to see it in the proposed configuration. For example, examine whether the person who reviews owner information can access the required report. Record any add-on or upgrade identified during the demonstration. Do not treat a feature shown in a full-product tour as included in a lower plan.

Recheck before a material change

If the portfolio expands or the owner needs a different reporting workflow, compare the new requirements with the current subscription. Ask how the change affects billing and configuration. Keep the original and revised proposals together so the business can understand why its cost or responsibilities changed. If a sales proposal differs from the public page, ask which terms govern the purchase and why. Retain the answer with the dated proposal. Do not silently combine the most favorable feature from one version with the lowest price from another; the comparison should describe one complete offer that the vendor will actually provide.

Prepare the evidence and next action

  • Required workflows and property count
  • Current plan qualifications
  • Recurring versus promotional charges
  • Onboarding and owner responsibilities
  • Written proposal and change terms

Check Airbnb Security-Deposit Eligibility Before Collection

AI-assisted editorial guide · Published November 20, 2026

A security deposit, an insurance product and a reimbursement request are different arrangements. Airbnb’s current pricing and payment policy limits off-platform collection and describes exceptions for some hosts, including select software-connected hosts. Do not infer eligibility from another host’s setup or from a field in third-party software. Resolve the applicable account rule before publishing a deposit requirement or requesting money.

Name the proposed arrangement

Describe whether the owner is proposing a refundable deposit, a nonrefundable fee, a card authorization or a third-party product. Record who would hold the money and what the guest would see. Avoid using the same label for different products. A proposed deposit workflow should be understandable before anyone is asked to decide whether Airbnb permits it.

Verify the account-specific permission

Read the current Airbnb policy and ask support or the connected provider to clarify the setup where necessary. Record the applicable exception and its scope. A provider’s ability to collect a payment is not proof that the platform permits that collection for your listing. Keep the confirmation and date with the account’s operating procedures.

Audit the prebooking disclosure

Where an arrangement is allowed, verify the required disclosure in the appropriate pricing field and the guest’s total-price presentation. Test representative dates and guest counts without making a real booking. Listing prose alone may not satisfy a requirement to include a charge in a structured field. Save evidence of what was displayed before booking, not just a later edited description.

Test an integration mismatch

Suppose software displays a deposit setting while the Airbnb account’s eligibility remains uncertain. Leave the proposed collection inactive until the relevant rule is resolved. Ask the provider which data is transmitted and where the guest sees it. Do not send a surprise external payment link to compensate for a missing disclosure or an unsupported integration behavior.

Define release and dispute records

For a permitted arrangement, document authorization, collection, release timing and dispute handling under the actual terms. Keep payment data within the approved provider instead of copying card information into local notes. Do not describe a deposit as covering every type of loss or guaranteeing reimbursement. Escalate conflicting contract or legal questions to a qualified adviser.

Maintain a dated policy decision

Assign an owner to review the workflow when platform rules, software or listing ownership changes. Stop using outdated screenshots as proof of current eligibility. Record a clear approved, unresolved or not-supported status, with the reason and next step. This avoids turning an administrative assumption into a guest-facing payment demand. Include the integration name and listing identifier in the decision record so approval for one account is not mistakenly reused for another property.

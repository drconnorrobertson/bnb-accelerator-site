Buyer’s Agent vs Real Estate Attorney for an STR Purchase

AI-assisted editorial guide · Published November 20, 2026

A buyer’s agent and a real estate attorney can serve different needs in the same rental purchase. The agent’s engagement may cover finding property, negotiating and coordinating the transaction. An attorney’s engagement may address legal questions, documents or representation. Actual duties depend on the agreements and jurisdiction. Hiring one does not automatically provide the other service or answer every question about rental use.

Identify whom each person represents

Read the agency and legal engagement documents. The CFPB warns that another attorney present at closing, such as the lender’s or seller’s attorney, does not represent the buyer. Similarly, do not assume a listing agent is acting solely for you. Ask directly who the client is, what duties apply and what conflicts or limits should be understood.

Separate negotiation from legal interpretation

A purchase negotiation can involve price, deadlines and requested repairs. A question about the legal effect of a restriction, ownership arrangement or contract provision may require legal advice. Route the issue deliberately instead of asking the person most available to resolve everything. Keep the factual property records accessible so the adviser can review the actual documents.

Define the legal engagement early

Ask whether the attorney’s work covers contract review, entity questions, title matters, closing attendance or specific rental restrictions. Some services may require another specialist or a separate fee. Local closing practices vary; the CFPB describes different participants and state requirements. Avoid waiting until the last day of a contingency period to discover that a needed review was never assigned.

Use a restriction example

Suppose a seller’s listing says rentals are allowed, but the buyer receives association documents with restrictions. The agent gathers the documents and coordinates transaction deadlines. The buyer asks the engaged attorney about the relevant provisions and options under the contract. A marketing statement is not treated as the final legal answer, and the attorney’s review is limited to the documents and questions actually supplied.

Maintain one deadline record

List financing, inspection, document-review and closing dates with the responsible person. Ask how advice and decisions will be communicated and preserved. A professional’s unanswered email is not a contract extension. The buyer should know when a written notice or amendment is needed and which person is engaged to prepare or coordinate it.

Confirm the closing handoff

Before signing, resolve open questions and identify the final documents the buyer should retain. Verify payment instructions through a trusted channel and understand who handles settlement funds. Select advisers based on the defined work and relevant qualifications. Neither an agent’s optimism nor an attorney’s involvement guarantees a successful rental operation or removes all transaction risk. Keep the final advice and the buyer’s decision together, particularly when the buyer accepts an unresolved condition rather than requesting a contractual change.

Prepare the evidence and next action

  • Agency and legal representation documents
  • Questions assigned to the right adviser
  • Legal scope and excluded work
  • Shared contract-deadline record
  • Final document and payment verification

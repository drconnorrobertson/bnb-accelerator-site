Handle Service-Animal Questions Under Current Policy

AI-assisted editorial guide · Published November 20, 2026

Airbnb publishes a policy for Service Animals and Emotional Support Animals, including limits on host questions and different treatment from pets. Local legal requirements can also matter. Staff should follow the current policy and obtain qualified advice where necessary, rather than applying an ordinary pet rule or requesting medical information as a default.

Decision processChecklistOfficial sources

Start with the correct policy

Read the official animal policy for the actual type of booking and jurisdiction. Do not assume the property’s no-pets rule settles a service-animal question. The policy distinguishes categories and describes circumstances that require platform review. Keep a current source link in the operating procedure instead of relying on a copied checklist from a prior year.

Limit questions appropriately

Airbnb identifies the questions hosts may ask and states that hosts cannot request a description of the disability, medical documentation or a demonstration of the animal’s task. Train staff to use the current permitted process. Do not improvise an intrusive questionnaire or ask a cleaner or onsite vendor to make a medical judgment.

Keep charges and conditions under review

Do not automatically apply a pet fee, extra cleaning charge or restrictive pet condition to an animal covered by the policy. Consult the current rules and seek support for a genuine uncertainty. Keep the ordinary pet workflow separate so an automated charge does not contradict the required treatment.

Escalate concerns through the proper channel

If there is a specific issue, describe observable facts and contact the platform or appropriate adviser. Do not assume that the operator can grant itself an exemption. Airbnb describes a process for limited exemptions. Any local legal question should be reviewed separately; a platform response is not a legal opinion covering every jurisdiction.

Communicate consistently with the team

Give only the information staff need to carry out the approved arrangement. Protect the guest’s privacy and avoid spreading medical or disability details. Ensure automated pet messages do not create a conflicting demand. Assign one responsible contact so the guest does not receive different instructions from multiple people.

Use an ordinary booking example

Suppose a guest mentions a service animal after receiving a standard no-pets message. The operator should review the relevant policy, correct any conflicting automated instruction and use only permitted questions if needed. Record the practical arrangement without demanding a diagnosis. This supports a respectful response while leaving genuine legal or policy uncertainties to the proper professionals. If a staff member receives an unfamiliar request, provide a way to pause and seek guidance promptly. The escalation should prevent an improvised refusal or fee demand while still allowing genuine operational questions to be resolved.

Prepare the evidence and next action

  • Current policy and jurisdiction checked
  • Permitted questions only
  • Pet workflow kept separate
  • Proper support or legal escalation
  • Privacy-conscious team instructions

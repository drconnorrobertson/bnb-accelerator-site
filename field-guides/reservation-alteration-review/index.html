Review a Guest Trip-Change Request Before Accepting

AI-assisted editorial guide · Published November 20, 2026

A message asking for different dates is not the same as an accepted reservation alteration. Airbnb provides a trip-change process that updates the booking when the proposed change is accepted. An operator should review the actual request and its consequences before responding. Keep the platform’s reservation record separate from informal promises in a conversation.

Decision processChecklistOfficial sources

Identify the exact requested change

Record which dates, guest details or other reservation terms the guest wants to change. Review the formal request rather than relying on a brief message summary. If the request and conversation differ, clarify that discrepancy through the appropriate channel. Do not assume a proposed extension includes every night or price discussed informally.

Check the current reservation alongside the proposal

Compare the existing and proposed dates, number of guests and displayed financial effect. Look for impacts on later reservations, preparation time and any committed service. An alteration that appears small can change the operating schedule. Keep a copy of the relevant decision information without unnecessarily duplicating sensitive guest records.

Use the authorized platform process

Airbnb’s guidance explains how a host responds to a guest’s trip-change request. Follow the current process and confirm the final reservation status. Do not cancel and recreate a booking merely to approximate a change unless the platform’s appropriate support process directs it. Distinguish acceptance from a message saying the idea sounds workable.

Assign the business decision

Determine who is authorized to accept a change that affects price, dates or workload. A co-host’s technical permissions may be broader than the owner’s agreed approval limits. Escalate material changes using the actual request details. Avoid leaving the guest with conflicting responses from several members of the operating team.

Update the downstream work

After a change is confirmed, notify the people whose tasks depend on the original reservation. Check scheduled messages, cleaning, access timing and any separate approved service. Do not assume every external system updates immediately. Record outstanding adjustments and who will confirm them so a platform change does not become a missed operational handoff.

Use an extension example

Suppose a guest asks for an extra night that overlaps a planned maintenance visit. The operator should resolve availability and authority before accepting. Once the confirmed record changes, reschedule the affected work and check the arrival or departure instructions. If the request is declined, ensure the team continues using the original reservation rather than the discussed alternative. Keep pending requests visible until they are accepted, declined or otherwise resolved. A proposed date should not quietly replace the confirmed date in the cleaner’s calendar while the platform still shows the original booking.

Prepare the evidence and next action

  • Formal request and current reservation
  • Date, guest and financial comparison
  • Approval authority
  • Confirmed platform status
  • Downstream task updates

Calculate RevPAR from a revenue statement

Enter lodging revenue only, the nights booked and the nights available for the same period. Defaults are a hypothetical 30-night example, not a market benchmark.




Example: $4,200 ÷ 30 available nights = $140 RevPAR; $4,200 ÷ 21 booked nights = $200 ADR; 21 ÷ 30 = 70% occupancy.

Which revenue belongs in the numerator?

Use a consistent lodging-revenue basis. Do not silently combine nightly accommodation revenue with guest-paid taxes, cleaning charges or deposits. If a statement reports total guest payments, reconcile those components first. Record whether the figure is before or after platform fees and refunds so comparisons use the same basis.

What counts as an available night?

Document the denominator. For an operating-performance calculation, use the nights genuinely available for rental and record nights excluded for owner use or maintenance. For a purchase review, also test revenue over the full calendar period. Reducing the denominator can raise reported RevPAR without adding revenue or reducing fixed carrying costs.

Example: a blocked calendar changes the apparent result

A hypothetical property earns $4,200 on 21 booked nights in a 30-night month. With 30 available nights it reports $140 RevPAR. If six unbooked nights are excluded as owner use, the denominator becomes 24 and reported RevPAR rises to $175. The owner still received the same lodging revenue. Label both calculations; do not compare the 24-night result with another property's full-calendar result.

Can I average monthly RevPAR?

Weight periods by available nights. Suppose a property earns $3,100 across 31 available nights and $5,600 across 28. Combined RevPAR is $8,700 ÷ 59 = $147.46. The simple average of monthly results ($100 and $200) is $150 and overstates the combined figure. Apply the same reconciliation across multiple units using available room-nights.

How should a buyer use RevPAR?

Compare similar property types over the same season with the same revenue and availability definitions. Investigate whether a stronger result comes from rate, occupancy or calendar exclusions. Then build the full expense and financing model. RevPAR alone cannot establish a purchase price, reserve requirement or cash-on-cash return.

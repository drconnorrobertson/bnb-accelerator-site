Client results

V

Victoria

Nine-bedroom luxury cabin, Sevierville TN

Victoria came in wanting scale rather than a starter property. Large-group cabins in the Smokies are the highest-revenue asset class we underwrite, but they are also the easiest to overpay for, because sellers know exactly what a nine-bedroom with a mountain view is worth on a good day. We modeled the deal against actual comparable booking data for nine-bedroom inventory in the corridor rather than the seller's projections, and negotiated from there.

  • Property type9BR luxury cabin
  • MarketSevierville, Tennessee
  • Purchase price$1,375,000
  • StrategyLarge-group Smokies STR
  • Peak month cash flow~$20,000
Result: Roughly $20,000 in cash flow in a single month during June peak season. Large-bedroom-count cabins in the Smokies capture multi-family and reunion bookings that smaller properties simply cannot compete for.

A&B

Ashley & Billy

First short-term rental · fastest launch to date

The single most expensive mistake a new short-term rental owner makes is a slow launch. Every week a property sits empty after closing is a week of mortgage, insurance, taxes, and utilities against zero revenue, and a listing with no reviews starts at a permanent ranking disadvantage on the platforms.

Ashley and Billy went through our full design and management pairing process, which meant furnishing, professional photography, listing copy, and pricing strategy were all in motion before the property even closed. The listing went live the moment the keys transferred.

  • Experience levelFirst-time STR owners
  • Launch window21 days from go-live
  • Nights booked80 nights
  • Process usedFull design + management pairing
Result: 80 nights booked within 21 days of launch, before the first full calendar month of operation had even closed out.
A

Antonio

Six-bedroom luxury rental · off-season performance

Peak-season numbers are easy to publish and easy to misread. What actually determines whether a short-term rental works is what it does in February, when the weather is bad and leisure travel is at its annual low.

Antonio's six-bedroom luxury property produced roughly $17,000 in cash flow in February. That is the month that tells you the underwriting was honest: a property that carries itself comfortably in the worst month of the year has enormous margin in the other eleven.

  • Property type6BR luxury rental
  • Month measuredFebruary (off-season)
  • Cash flow~$17,000
  • Key driverLarge-group amenity package
Result: About $17,000 in cash flow in February. Off-season performance at this level is the strongest evidence that a property was bought right rather than bought lucky.

The Pattern

What every one of these deals had in common

Four different clients, four different markets, four different budgets. The variables that actually drove the outcomes were the same every time.

Bought right

Every deal was negotiated below asking, and the underwriting used real comparable booking data rather than a listing agent's revenue projection.

Launched fast

Design, furnishing, and photography were in motion before closing, so the listing went live within weeks instead of months.

Right operator

Each property was paired with a manager or co-host already operating successfully in that specific submarket, not a national brand learning it.

Amenity fit

The property matched what that market's guests actually book: bedroom count in the Smokies, pools in Arizona, hot tubs in cabin country.

FAQ

Questions about these results

Are these results typical?

No. These are actual outcomes for specific properties, selected because they are documented, and they are not a promise of what any other property will do. Results depend on purchase price, financing, market performance, management quality, and your own tax situation. Real estate involves risk, including loss of principal.

How is cash flow calculated in these case studies?

Gross booking revenue minus management, cleaning, supplies, utilities, insurance, property tax, and debt service. Where a figure covers a single month rather than a year, we say so, because peak-month cash flow on a seasonal property is not one twelfth of annual cash flow.

Can I speak to past clients?

Yes. Ask for references on your first call and we will connect you with clients who bought in situations comparable to yours. We would rather you do the diligence up front.

What is your repeat buyer rate?

About 80%. It is the number we point people to first, because nobody buys a second property from a firm that got the first one wrong. One client, an Associate Partner at IBM, has closed six properties with us across four years.

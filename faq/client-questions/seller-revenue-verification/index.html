Verifying an STR Seller's Revenue

Check booking reports, payouts, refunds and expenses before relying on an operating rental's advertised income.

Practical answers for buying and launching a short-term rental with BNB Accelerator. Updated September 30, 2026.

All client questionsRead the related guideBook a Call
Which seller records are most useful for verifying rental income?

Ask for platform booking reports, payout histories and property-manager statements for comparable periods. Reconcile them with supporting bank records where available. Request cancellations, refunds, cleaning charges, taxes and owner stays so you can explain differences. A listing screenshot or revenue claim is a starting point for diligence, not proof of spendable income.

Why can booking revenue differ from deposits into the owner's bank account?

Booking totals may include amounts collected for taxes or cleaning, while payouts can reflect platform fees, refunds and timing differences. A manager may deduct its fees and property expenses before sending the owner money. Reconcile the path from guest charges to platform payouts to manager statements to bank deposits instead of comparing unrelated totals.

Should cleaning fees be included in projected rental income?

Use a consistent method. If the projection includes cleaning fees as revenue, include the associated cleaning expense and any fees calculated on those receipts. If the model excludes both, explain that clearly. Mixing gross receipts from one property with rent-only revenue from another can make a comparison misleading.

How should I account for the seller's personal stays?

Identify blocked dates and whether they represent owner use, repairs or unbooked availability. Owner stays may hide potential rental days, but removing them does not guarantee additional bookings. Consider the season and demand for those dates. Keep financial adjustments separate from any personal-use tax analysis, which belongs with your CPA.

What if a seller provides only one unusually strong season?

Ask for a longer history and explain any change in pricing, management, amenities or market demand. Compare the period with current competing properties and a full seasonal calendar. If better evidence is unavailable, underwrite conservatively and treat the uncertainty as a purchase risk rather than annualizing the best months.

How do I evaluate a property with very little booking history?

Treat it more like a new launch. Build an appropriate comparable set, verify the operating path and model the costs needed to deliver the proposed guest experience. Include ramp-up uncertainty and a downside case. Limited history may offer upside, but it does not establish that the property's projected revenue is achievable.

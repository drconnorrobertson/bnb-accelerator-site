STR LLCs, Title and Ownership

Coordinate LLC formation, borrower requirements, deed transfers, business accounts and ownership structure with your advisers.

Practical answers for buying and launching a short-term rental with BNB Accelerator. Updated September 30, 2026.

All client questionsRead the related guideBook a Call
Should I create an LLC for my STR?

We generally recommend considering an LLC as part of your ownership and operating plan. Confirm the structure with your attorney, CPA and lender so it fits your financing, insurance and state requirements. An LLC can provide liability protection, but it does not replace appropriate insurance or automatically create tax savings. SBA business structure guidance

When should I create it?

Start the process between onboarding and closing, once you have confirmed the appropriate structure. Completing an online application can be quick, but state approval and opening a bank account may take longer. Allow time to have the entity and account ready before they are needed.

Should the LLC own the property or just operate the STR?

That depends on your loan and legal structure. Many conventional second-home loans require you to close personally; some investment loan products allow LLC ownership.

If you own the property personally and the LLC operates the rental, have your attorney document that arrangement and confirm it with your lender and insurer. Using an LLC bank account does not put the property into the LLC.

Can I close personally and transfer the property into an LLC later?

Often, yes, but confirm the requirements before transferring the deed. Your loan may contain a due-on-sale clause, and permitted transfers depend on the loan’s rules. Certain Fannie Mae loans allow transfers to borrower-controlled LLCs under specified conditions. Fannie Mae transfer guidance

We can help coordinate with your loan servicer, title company and advisors to review approval requirements, transfer costs and insurance updates. Transferring the deed does not automatically transfer the mortgage or release your personal responsibility for it.

Will my loan type prevent me from closing in an LLC?

It may. Confirm the permitted borrower and title holder with your lender before closing. For a second-home loan, also confirm that your intended rental use and property management agreement meet the occupancy requirements. Forming an LLC does not override those requirements. Fannie Mae second-home requirements

How does the LLC bank account work?

Once the operating structure is established, use a dedicated LLC account to receive its rental revenue and pay its expenses. Keep personal spending separate, and clearly record money you contribute or withdraw.

If the property or mortgage remains in your personal name, have your CPA explain how property payments should be recorded. The account used to pay a bill does not determine its tax treatment.

Which expenses/revenue should run through it?

Route the LLC’s booking payouts and other operating income through the account. Use it for applicable property expenses such as utilities, cleaning, management fees, maintenance, repairs, supplies, furnishings and capital improvements.

Confirm how to handle mortgage payments, taxes and insurance when the property is personally owned. Record mortgage principal separately from interest, and distinguish improvements from operating expenses. Exclude personal-use costs.

When should I start running expenses through the LLC?

Start as soon as the LLC and bank account are established and the operating arrangement is confirmed, ideally by closing. Keep records of earlier expenses paid personally so your bookkeeper can record them correctly as contributions, reimbursements or other appropriate entries.

Do my closing costs need to be paid from the LLC's bank account?

No, not automatically. Follow your lender’s and title company’s instructions for the source of closing funds. If you are closing personally, do not move funds into the LLC immediately before closing without checking with your lender.

If the LLC is the buyer, coordinate its funding with title and the lender. Keep the closing statement and proof of payment so your CPA can properly record acquisition costs and any owner contributions.

Are there transfer taxes when moving the property into an LLC?

Possibly. State and local rules vary, and an exemption may depend on the ownership structure and required filings. For example, Pennsylvania generally taxes individual-to-LLC conveyances, while California may exclude certain transfers with unchanged proportional ownership from property tax reassessment. Transfer tax and reassessment are separate issues. Pennsylvania transfer guidance, California reassessment guidance

Before transferring, ask title or a local real estate attorney to confirm transfer taxes, recording fees, reassessment consequences and exemption paperwork.
